# GD Round — Practice Examples

Use these as models for structure, not speeches to memorise. In a mock GD, make an opening of 30–40 seconds, two distinct contributions of 20–30 seconds, and a neutral summary if invited. Listen and adapt to what others actually say. Do not invent statistics.

## 1. AI in recruitment: opportunity or risk?

**Opening:** “AI can help recruiters handle repetitive screening and scheduling, but hiring decisions affect people’s livelihoods. I would use it to support a structured process, with humans accountable for criteria, exceptions, and final decisions.”

**Build:** “A practical benefit is faster shortlisting when there are many applications. The key is to define job-related criteria before looking at candidates and check whether the tool overlooks qualified people.”

**Counterpoint:** “Speed alone is not success. If historical hiring data contains bias, automating it may reproduce that bias. Candidates should have a way to question errors.”

**Close:** “The group sees value in reducing administrative work, while agreeing that fairness, transparency, and human review matter most for consequential decisions.”

## 2. Should organisations retain hybrid work?

**Opening:** “Hybrid work should be judged by the work and the people involved. It can improve flexibility, but onboarding, collaboration, and service roles need deliberate in-person time.”

**Build:** “Instead of a universal number of office days, teams could set shared collaboration days and measure outcomes and employee experience.”

**Counterpoint:** “We should also consider employees whose roles cannot be remote. A flexible policy must not create unfair access to growth or recognition.”

**Close:** “A workable policy balances flexibility, business needs, and fairness, with periodic review rather than assuming one model fits every role.”

## 3. Is pay transparency good for employees and employers?

**Opening:** “Publishing pay ranges can help candidates make informed choices and push employers to explain how compensation is set. It also requires sound internal pay structures.”

**Build:** “Ranges are more useful when the employer explains grade, location, experience, and variable pay rather than showing a very broad number.”

**Counterpoint:** “Transparency can reveal unexplained differences. That may be uncomfortable, but it gives HR a reason to audit and correct the process.”

**Close:** “The strongest approach combines clear ranges with consistent criteria and a way to discuss exceptions.”

## 4. Skills versus degrees in hiring

**Opening:** “A degree can signal foundational learning, while demonstrated skills show what someone can do now. The right weight depends on the role.”

**Build:** “Work samples, structured interviews, and internships can reveal applied ability, especially for entry-level roles.”

**Counterpoint:** “A single test can also disadvantage candidates unfamiliar with that format. Employers should use several job-related signals.”

**Close:** “Degrees and skills are complementary evidence; fair assessment should focus on the actual requirements of the job.”

## 5. Employee wellbeing: employer responsibility or personal choice?

**Opening:** “Individuals make health choices, but the workplace shapes workload, schedules, and psychological safety. Both have a role.”

**Build:** “Managers can set realistic priorities, encourage leave, and respond to concerns without treating confidential disclosures casually.”

**Counterpoint:** “A wellness event cannot compensate for chronic overwork. We need to examine the work design as well as offer support.”

**Close:** “Meaningful wellbeing combines individual support with responsible management practices.”

## 6. Is a four-day workweek practical in India?

**Opening:** “The phrase ‘four-day week’ can mean fewer hours or the same hours compressed into four days. We should clarify which model we are discussing before judging it.”

**Build:** “A pilot in suitable teams could track service levels, productivity, overtime, and employee fatigue.”

**Counterpoint:** “Customer-facing and shift-based teams need coverage; a policy that works for office teams may need a different design elsewhere.”

**Close:** “A measured pilot and role-specific design are more useful than a blanket yes or no.”

## Practice and feedback sheet

For each topic, write: (1) your opening in two sentences; (2) two distinct arguments; (3) one example you can defend; (4) one response to disagreement; (5) a neutral summary. After a mock, score clarity, listening, relevance, and turn-taking from 1 to 5. Note one change for the next mock.
