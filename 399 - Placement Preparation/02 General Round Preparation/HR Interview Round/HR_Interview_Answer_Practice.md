## Behavioural questions: prepare real STAR stories

| Question | Possible source | What the answer must establish |
|---|---|---|
| Tell me about a time you handled detailed work | GPT onboarding/master sheet | Her own checks, a real outcome, and confidentiality |
| Describe a challenge coordinating with others | Union Bank branch survey | Constraint, respectful approach, response, outcome |
| Tell me about a mistake or setback | One verified internship or dissertation incident | Ownership, correction, prevention; never invent one |
| Describe a time you learned quickly | IOCL payroll/labour documentation | How she clarified rules and checked her work |
| Tell me about teamwork | Induction or project activity | Her distinct contribution, not only “we” |

For each story, write **Situation (1 sentence), Task (1), Action (2–3), Result (1), Learning (1)**. If the impact was not measured, describe a concrete verified outcome rather than making up a number.

## HR domain: short response models

**Recruitment versus selection:** “Recruitment attracts a pool of applicants; selection evaluates candidates against job-related criteria and chooses among them. I would define requirements first and use consistent assessment criteria.”

**How would you handle an employee grievance?** “I would listen without promising an outcome, document the concern accurately, explain the relevant process and confidentiality limits, and route it to the responsible HR person. An impartial review and follow-up matter.”

**How would you improve onboarding?** “I’d first identify where new joiners or managers face delays. Then I’d use a clear checklist with owners and deadlines, verify documentation, ensure role and policy orientation, and gather feedback after joining.”

**An employee is underperforming. What would you do?** “Clarify expectations and evidence with the manager, understand barriers, agree on support and measurable goals, document check-ins, and review progress fairly under company policy.”

**Employees did not apply training after attending. Why?** “I’d look beyond attendance: whether the training matched the job, whether learners practised, and whether managers gave time and feedback to apply it. I’d measure behaviour or work outcomes where possible.”

**A manager asks for confidential employee data without a clear need.** “I would check authorisation and purpose, share only what policy permits, and seek guidance from the responsible HR lead before disclosing sensitive information.”

## Questions to ask the interviewer

- “What would success look like for a new joiner in this role after six months?”
- “Which HR processes would this role own directly, and which would it support?”
- “What systems and training help new team members learn your processes?”
