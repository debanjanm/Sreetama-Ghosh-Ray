# Experience Round — Deep-Dive Questions and Follow-ups

Use with [Experience Round Question Bank](Experience_Round_Question_Bank.md) and the **resume version actually submitted**. These questions expand the existing bank rather than providing scripted answers. For every claim, say whether she **observed, assisted, drafted, calculated, analysed, or owned** the work. Use anonymous hypothetical examples where real records are confidential.

## How to practise

For each question, first answer in **45–60 seconds**: context → exact task → action → check/decision → outcome → learning. Then handle the italicised follow-up without changing the facts. Prepare evidence privately, but do not carry employee identifiers, salary records, questionnaire responses, or internal bank documents into an interview.

## Union Bank — structure, HR operations, and branches

1. What was the purpose of the Chennai North Zonal Office and Chennai South Regional Office in the work you saw? *How did a branch request reach the relevant HR team?*
2. Walk through a leave request as you observed it. *Which parts were policy-driven, and which approvals were required?*
3. What is the distinction between salary, allowances, and TA/conveyance claims in the records you encountered? *Which part, if any, did you process?*
4. What did you learn about transfer policy? *Can you give a hypothetical situation without revealing an employee's case?*
5. How were disciplinary matters handled at a high level? *What should an HR intern avoid assuming or disclosing?*
6. Describe a circular that changed what staff needed to do. *How did you check whether it applied to a particular group?*
7. What was the difference between mandatory training and hybrid learning in the L&D circulars you read? *How did that relate to your dissertation?*
8. What did the Rural & Agriculture Business Department do in the context of the bank? *Why was your exposure relevant to understanding the organisation, while not claiming you worked in lending?*
9. What did you learn from the Currency Chest visit? *Which safety and confidentiality boundaries applied?*
10. Why did you contact both officers and clerical staff? *Could their different roles affect survey responses?*
11. How did you plan visits or communication across 27 branches? *What changed when a branch was busy or a respondent unavailable?*
12. What wording did you use to explain the survey to employees? *How did you avoid pressuring them or disrupting customer work?*
13. How were paper responses stored, transferred, and entered into the dataset? *Who had access to identifiable forms?*
14. How did you detect a missing answer, duplicate, or inconsistent response? *What did you record rather than silently “fix”?*
15. What did staff say about post-amalgamation training and SOP adoption? *Which points are individual views, and which are supported by your data?*

## GPT Group — onboarding, records, and accuracy

16. How does an offer letter differ from an appointment or confirmation letter? *What changed in purpose and timing?*
17. What fields did you cross-check before a letter went for approval? *What source took priority if two records disagreed?*
18. Describe a real discrepancy you encountered in a joining file, if any. *Who approved its correction?*
19. What did you understand about the purpose of PF, ESIC, nominee, identity, and tax documentation? *Which requirements varied by employee or business unit?*
20. How did you track documents still missing from a new joiner? *How did you communicate without exposing personal details to the wrong person?*
21. What was the structure of the Employee Master Sheet? *Which fields were most error-prone and why?*
22. Did you use formulas, filters, Pivot Tables, or VLOOKUP in actual work there? *Show a small fictional example only for tools you truly used.*
23. How did you protect banking details and identity numbers while updating the sheet? *Who could review or authorise a change?*
24. What happened when a date of joining changed after documents had been drafted? *Which downstream records needed rechecking?*
25. Explain how the New Joining Checklist was used. *Was “100% adherence” measured over every new hire, a sampled set, or only files you handled?*
26. How did onboarding differ between Infrastructure and Healthcare? *Which differences did you see personally?*
27. What were the quality checks for ID cards? *How would you handle a missing blood group rather than guessing?*
28. How were resigned employee files indexed and retrieved? *What access or retention rules were you instructed to follow?*
29. What could go wrong if an employee master record is inaccurate? *Give a concrete downstream effect without citing a confidential incident.*
30. Which GPT task had the highest stakes for accuracy, and what was your double-check method?

## IOCL — payroll, compliance exposure, employee relations, and L&D

31. Explain a hypothetical pro-rata salary calculation with attendance and leave without pay. *What assumptions about payable days must be confirmed first?*
32. How would earned leave encashment affect a calculation? *What policy inputs would you need before calculating?*
33. For the 30 employee calculations, what was the source data, output, review, and final use? *Could you reproduce a fictional example on paper?*
34. What did “audited the Labour Law Compendium” mean in your internship: compared documents, flagged clauses, or issued a formal compliance opinion? *Who reviewed your work?*
35. How did you track a legacy provision, a newer code, and a government notification without mixing their effective dates? *What would you do if applicability was unclear?*
36. Which part of Certified Standing Orders or CDA Rules did you study and how did it inform the draft chargesheet? *What procedural fairness did you learn?*
37. Was the chargesheet a learning exercise, a supervised draft, or an issued document? *What evidence and approval would an actual disciplinary notice require?*
38. What was your role in RTI processing? *What information may need review before disclosure?*
39. What was the central question of your 25+ employee digital-systems survey? *How were respondents chosen?*
40. What differences did respondents describe between SAP, ERP, ESS, and EPMS? *Which systems did you actually operate rather than only hear about?*
41. What can a small, non-random survey say about work culture? *What conclusions would overstate the evidence?*
42. What was your specific contribution to the E0 officer induction? *What would have happened if a logistical task had failed?*
43. What did you change in the TNA questionnaire and why? *How did you decide a question would produce usable information?*
44. Which IOCL task made you ask a supervisor for clarification? *How did you act after receiving it?*

## Dissertation — methods, data, and explanation to HR interviewers

45. Why did you choose bank employees and the topic of hybrid learning? *What gap did your study aim to address?*
46. What did each of the four constructs measure? *Give one non-confidential example item per construct.*
47. Why use Likert items? *How were responses coded, and were any items reverse-coded?*
48. What did “guide-approved” mean? *What were your decisions and your guide's suggestions?*
49. How did you conduct the pilot with 20 employees? *What changed after feedback, if anything?*
50. How did you avoid duplicate or ineligible Google Form responses? *What did “0 mismatches” compare, exactly?*
51. Can you reproduce the flow from 132 collected to 103 eligible and 102 regression cases? *State each exclusion rule and missing-value step.*
52. How did you combine paper and online questionnaires? *What checks caught data-entry errors?*
53. How were branches and employees approached? *What selection bias might remain?*
54. Why is Cronbach's alpha .98 worth examining further? *Which scale was tested and could similar items inflate consistency?*
55. What did each correlation tell you, and what did it not tell you?
56. Why use multiple regression with three predictors? *What is the dependent variable?*
57. Explain R²=.61 to an HR manager in one sentence. *Why does it not mean “61% improvement caused by training”?*
58. What does p<.001 in the overall model test establish? *Does it establish that every predictor is individually significant?*
59. What are F(3,98) and n=102 describing? *Can you show where these appear in the SPSS output?*
60. Did you check missing data, outliers, linearity, residuals, and overlap among predictors? *Say clearly which checks you actually completed.*
61. What practical action could a bank test next, based on your findings? *How would you measure improvement without claiming this survey proved causation?*
62. Why did you preserve versions and input fingerprints? *Explain SHA-256 in plain language without claiming it proves data quality.*
63. What would you change if you repeated the study with more time or a larger sample?

## Behavioural probes across experiences

64. Which task did you initially misunderstand? *How did you clarify it and prevent recurrence?*
65. Give an example of saying “I don't know” to a supervisor. *What did you do next?*
66. When did you have to prioritise accuracy over speed? *What was the trade-off?*
67. Tell me about a deadline when several people depended on your work. *What did you communicate and deliver?*
68. When did feedback change your approach to a document, survey, or interaction?
69. Describe a disagreement over how to handle a task. *How did you respond without claiming authority you did not have?*
70. What example best demonstrates discretion with sensitive information? *Explain your behaviour without revealing the information.*
71. Which experience most changed your view of HR work? *What assumption did it challenge?*
72. What would a supervisor say you did well? *What concrete example supports that view?*
73. What would a supervisor say you should improve? *What have you already done about it?*
74. Why did you move among banking, infrastructure/healthcare, and an energy PSU? *What transferable HR skill connects them?*
75. Which part of your resume is still mostly exposure rather than independent capability? *What would you need to learn before owning it?*

## Rapid-fire checks an interviewer may use

- “You say **maintained** a live sheet. What did you edit, how often, and who reviewed it?”
- “You say **audited** a compendium. Was this a formal audit or an intern's comparison?”
- “You say **administered** a survey. Did you design, distribute, collect, enter, and analyse it yourself?”
- “You say **supported** induction. Name your precise deliverables.”
- “You say **100% adherence**. Show the numerator and denominator.”
- “You mention SAP/ERP/ESS. Which did you log into and perform a task in?”
- “What happened to the one case between 103 eligible records and n=102?”
- “Which achievement can you verify without exposing employer or respondent data?”
