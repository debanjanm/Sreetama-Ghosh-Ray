# Grid and Matching Puzzles

## In simple words

**The idea:** A grid turns a story of who-owns-what into a table where every clue fills in a tick or a cross.

**Think of it like:** A seating chart with tick-marks for each person's role.

**Simple steps:**
1. Draw a table with people down the side and attributes across the top.
2. Put a tick for sure matches and a cross for exclusions.
3. A tick in a row or column crosses out the others.
4. Repeat until the grid is complete.

**Watch out:** Do not assume each attribute is used only once unless the puzzle says so.

## Method
Make one row per person and one column per attribute. Enter ✓ for certain matches, ✗ for exclusions. A ✓ eliminates other cells in its row/column if each attribute is unique.

## Recognise and solve
A,B,C hold HR, Finance, Sales roles (one each). A is not HR; B is Finance; C is not Sales. B=Finance, C=HR, A=Sales.

## Tips and traps
Check whether attributes can repeat; a one-to-one grid is invalid if repetition is allowed. Record “either X or Y” as a paired option rather than guessing. Process the most restrictive clue first.

## Try it
X,Y,Z work Mon,Tue,Wed once each. X not Mon; Y Wed; Z not Tue. **Answer:** Z Mon, X Tue, Y Wed.

## More worked patterns

### Pattern 1: Asha, Bela and Charu choose distinct colours red, blue, green. Asha does not choose red; Bela chooses blue; Charu does not choose green. Who chooses what?

**Solution:** Bela=blue. Remaining red/green; Asha≠red, so Asha=green and **Charu=red**.

### Pattern 2: Three interviews occur Mon–Wed, one per day. HR is before Finance; Operations is Tuesday. Find the order.

**Solution:** Operations fixes Tuesday. HR must precede Finance in the two remaining slots: **HR Monday, Operations Tuesday, Finance Wednesday**.

### Pattern 3: Three candidates score distinct ranks. A is above B, C is below B. Rank them.

**Solution:** A>B and B>C combine transitively: **A first, B second, C third**. Record relative order before assigning slots.
