# Data Sufficiency

## In simple words

**The idea:** You do not have to solve the problem. You only decide whether the information is enough to solve it.

**Think of it like:** A hiring manager deciding whether a CV has enough detail to shortlist someone.

**Simple steps:**
1. Understand exactly what the question asks.
2. Look at statement 1 alone.
3. Look at statement 2 alone.
4. Combine them only if neither is enough alone. Test with two different numbers to catch false sufficiency.

**Watch out:** When judging one statement alone, do not use information from the other.

## Method
Read what is asked: a value, or a yes/no decision. Test statement 1 alone, statement 2 alone, then together only if required. Try two values satisfying a statement to challenge sufficiency.

## Recognise and solve
1. Find x: (1) x²=16; (2) x>0. (1) allows ±4, (2) many positives; **both together** give x=4.
2. Is n even? (1) n multiple of 4; (2) n multiple of 2. **Each statement alone** gives a definite yes.
3. Is x positive? (1) x²=9; (2) x<0. Statement 2 alone gives a definite **no**, even without exact x.

## Tips and traps
Do not carry statement 1 into your evaluation of statement 2. “Can answer yes/no” does not mean the answer must be yes. Follow the options' exact code, which varies by test.

## Try it
Find y: (1) y+z=10; (2) z=4. **Answer:** both together, y=6.

## More worked patterns

### Pattern 1: Is integer x even? (1) x is divisible by 6. (2) x is divisible by 3.

**Solution:** (1) alone is sufficient because multiples of 6 are even. (2) alone is not: 3 and 6 differ. **Statement 1 only**.

### Pattern 2: What is x? (1) x+y=10. (2) y=4.

**Solution:** Neither alone fixes x; together x=10−4=**6**. Thus **both statements together** are sufficient.

### Pattern 3: Is x positive? (1) x²=9. (2) x>0.

**Solution:** (1) allows +3 and −3, so insufficient. (2) answers the yes/no question directly. **Statement 2 only**.
