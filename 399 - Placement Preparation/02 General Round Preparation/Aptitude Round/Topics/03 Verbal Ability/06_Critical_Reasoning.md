# Critical Reasoning and Verbal Logic

> **Difficulty:** Hard  ·  **Target time:** 90 s

## In simple words

**The idea:** Find the gap between the evidence and the conclusion. Questions ask you to fill it, widen it or spot it.

**Think of it like:** A friend says 'the shop is busy so the food must be great'. Busy does not prove great.

**Simple steps:**
1. Mark the evidence and the conclusion.
2. Name the missing link (the assumption).
3. To strengthen: add evidence that supports the link. To weaken: add evidence that breaks it.

**Watch out:** Two things happening together does not prove one caused the other.

## Method
Mark evidence and conclusion. Identify the missing bridge for assumptions. For strengthen/weaken, look for new evidence that changes the link without changing the subject.

## Worked pattern
“Online training drew more attendees than classroom training; therefore it improved learning.”
- Evidence: more attendees.
- Conclusion: better learning.
- Assumption: attendance reliably indicates learning, or learning was independently measured.
- Weakener: the online group performed worse on a comparable post-test.
- Strengthener: matched groups took the same assessment and the online group improved more.

## Cause and effect trap
“After a new policy, attrition fell” does not by itself prove the policy caused the fall; hiring mix, economy, or other changes may explain it.

## Tips and traps
The negation test: if negating an alleged assumption breaks the reasoning, it may be necessary. Distinguish “some” from “all” and correlation from causation.

## Try it
“The workshop had high satisfaction, so productivity improved.” What information most directly tests the claim? **Answer:** comparable pre/post productivity measures, not another satisfaction rating.

## More worked patterns

### Pattern 1: Claim: “After a wellness programme began, sick days fell; therefore the programme caused the decline.” Give one alternative explanation.

**Solution:** A mild flu season or a change in reporting could lower sick days. The before/after correlation alone does not establish causation.

### Pattern 2: Claim: “We should add evening support because 30% of tickets arrive after 6 PM.” What evidence would strengthen it?

**Solution:** Show that these tickets need prompt human resolution, remain unanswered until morning, and that evening staffing improves outcomes at a reasonable cost.

### Pattern 3: Claim: “Every surveyed intern liked the policy, so all employees will like it.” What is the main weakness?

**Solution:** Interns may differ from the whole workforce. The **unrepresentative sample** cannot justify a conclusion about all employees.

## Deeper patterns (medium to hard)

### Extra rules and tricks
- Question types: assumption, strengthen, weaken, inference, flaw, conclusion.
- Common flaws: correlation taken as cause, reverse causation, small or unrepresentative sample, overgeneralisation, false either-or.
- Weaken: add evidence that gives another cause or breaks the link. Strengthen: add evidence that rules out other causes or backs the link, such as a control group.
- Assumption test: negate it; if the argument collapses, it is a real assumption.

### Medium patterns

**M1. 'Sales rose after the new ad, so the ad caused the rise.' Which fact weakens this?**

**Solution:** 'A festival season began at the same time.' It gives **another cause**.

**M2. 'Employees who use the gym are healthier, so gyms improve health.' What is the flaw?**

**Solution:** **Reverse causation**: healthier people may be the ones who join a gym.

### Hard patterns

**H1. 'A pilot at 3 branches cut errors by 20% after training, so training cuts errors.' Which fact strengthens it?**

**Solution:** 'Branches without the training saw no change in errors.' A **control group** rules out other causes.

**H2. 'We should hire more recruiters because applications have doubled.' What is the assumption?**

**Solution:** That the **existing recruiters cannot handle the extra load**. If they can, the argument fails.

**H3. 'Everyone I asked likes the canteen, so the whole company likes it.' Name the flaw.**

**Solution:** A **small, unrepresentative sample**.
