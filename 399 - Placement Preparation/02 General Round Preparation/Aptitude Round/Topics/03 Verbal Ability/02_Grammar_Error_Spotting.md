# Grammar and Error Spotting

## In simple words

**The idea:** Find the real subject and the real verb, then check that they agree.

**Think of it like:** Matching a key to its lock: a singular subject needs a singular verb.

**Simple steps:**
1. Strip out extra phrases between the subject and the verb.
2. Check singular and plural agreement.
3. Check tense, pronouns, and parallel structure.
4. Read the corrected sentence to see if it sounds natural.

**Watch out:** Phrases such as 'of the candidates' do not change whether the subject is singular or plural.

## Check order
1. Identify main subject and verb; ignore intervening descriptive phrases.
2. Check tense sequence and pronoun reference.
3. Check parallel forms, modifiers, articles, and prepositions.
4. Read the full corrected sentence for meaning.

## Recognise and solve
1. “The list of applicants are ready” → **is**; list is singular.
2. “Each of the interns have submitted” → **has**; each is singular.
3. “She enjoys screening, interviewing, and to onboard” → **onboarding** for parallel gerunds.
4. “After reviewing the files, the report was sent” → dangling modifier; write “After reviewing the files, the analyst sent the report.”

## Tips and traps
Long phrases conceal agreement errors. “Neither A nor B” usually agrees with the nearer subject in standard test constructions. Grammar questions may test usage rather than a universal rule; read options fully.

## Try it
“One of the reports ___ missing.” **Answer:** is.

## More worked patterns

### Pattern 1: Correct: “Each of the candidates have submitted their resume.”

**Solution:** The subject **Each** is singular: “Each of the candidates **has** submitted a resume.” The plural noun after “of” does not control the verb.

### Pattern 2: Correct: “Neither the manager nor the employees was available.”

**Solution:** With “neither…nor,” the verb commonly agrees with the nearer subject “employees”: “Neither the manager nor the employees **were** available.”

### Pattern 3: Correct: “She has been working here since three years.”

**Solution:** Use **for** with a duration and **since** with a starting point: “She has been working here **for** three years.”
