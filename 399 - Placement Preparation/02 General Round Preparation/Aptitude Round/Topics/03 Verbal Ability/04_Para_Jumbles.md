# Para Jumbles

## Method
Find a sentence that introduces the subject without unresolved “this/it/they.” Pair references with antecedents, then use chronology and cause→effect. Read the complete sequence aloud.

## Worked pattern
A. This feedback led to a revised schedule.  
B. New joiners said the first week was overloaded.  
C. The revised schedule spread sessions across two weeks.  
D. HR asked new joiners about their induction.

**Order: D→B→A→C.** D introduces the survey; B is feedback; “This feedback” in A points to B; C gives the resulting revision.

## Tips and traps
An opening can sometimes begin with a pronoun if it refers to a general concept, so do not use a mechanical ban. Look for definite articles (“the policy”) versus first introductions (“a policy”), contrast words, and paired examples.

## Try it
P. It was then reviewed by the manager. Q. An intern prepared a draft. R. The approved version was sent. **Answer:** Q→P→R.

## More worked patterns

### Pattern 1: Order: A. Therefore, it revised the schedule. B. The company surveyed employees. C. Many requested flexible hours. D. The new schedule began in April.

**Solution:** **B-C-A-D**. “Many” refers to surveyed employees; “therefore” follows their request; rollout comes last.

### Pattern 2: Order: A. The automation reduced errors. B. First, the team mapped the current workflow. C. It then automated duplicate entry. D. Finally, it measured the results.

**Solution:** **B-C-A-D**. “It then” follows the first step; the benefit follows automation; measurement closes the sequence.

### Pattern 3: Order: A. As a result, attendance rose. B. The firm introduced short online sessions. C. Earlier workshops required long travel. D. Employees could now join from their desks.

**Solution:** **C-B-D-A**. Background problem → intervention → access mechanism → result.
