# Para Jumbles

> **Difficulty:** Hard  ·  **Target time:** 90 s

## In simple words

**The idea:** The sentences form a chain: introduce the topic, add detail, give the result.

**Think of it like:** Putting the steps of a recipe in the right order.

**Simple steps:**
1. Find the opening sentence that introduces the topic with no 'this' or 'it' pointing back.
2. Match words like 'this', 'it' and 'they' to what they refer to.
3. Find pairs that clearly go together (cause then effect).
4. Read the full order once to check it flows.

**Watch out:** A sentence starting with 'This' or 'Therefore' cannot be the opening sentence.

## Method
Find a sentence that introduces the subject without unresolved “this/it/they.” Pair references with antecedents, then use chronology and cause→effect. Read the complete sequence aloud.

## Worked pattern
A. This feedback led to a revised schedule.  
B. New joiners said the first week was overloaded.  
C. The revised schedule spread sessions across two weeks.  
D. HR asked new joiners about their induction.

**Order: D→B→A→C.** D introduces the survey; B is feedback; “This feedback” in A points to B; C gives the resulting revision.

## Tips and traps
An opening can sometimes begin with a pronoun if it refers to a general concept, so do not use a mechanical ban. Look for definite articles (“the policy”) versus first introductions (“a policy”), contrast words, and paired examples.

## Try it
P. It was then reviewed by the manager. Q. An intern prepared a draft. R. The approved version was sent. **Answer:** Q→P→R.

## More worked patterns

### Pattern 1: Order: A. Therefore, it revised the schedule. B. The company surveyed employees. C. Many requested flexible hours. D. The new schedule began in April.

**Solution:** **B-C-A-D**. “Many” refers to surveyed employees; “therefore” follows their request; rollout comes last.

### Pattern 2: Order: A. The automation reduced errors. B. First, the team mapped the current workflow. C. It then automated duplicate entry. D. Finally, it measured the results.

**Solution:** **B-C-A-D**. “It then” follows the first step; the benefit follows automation; measurement closes the sequence.

### Pattern 3: Order: A. As a result, attendance rose. B. The firm introduced short online sessions. C. Earlier workshops required long travel. D. Employees could now join from their desks.

**Solution:** **C-B-D-A**. Background problem → intervention → access mechanism → result.

## Deeper patterns (medium to hard)

### Extra rules and tricks
- Opening sentence: introduces the topic, uses 'a/an' for new things, and has no 'this/it/they/however' pointing backwards.
- A noun introduced with 'a' or 'an' in one sentence appears with 'the' in the next.
- Connectors: 'however' (contrast), 'therefore' and 'as a result' (effect), 'meanwhile' (parallel), 'finally' (last).
- Mandatory pairs: cause then effect, question then answer, claim then example.
- A sentence that starts with 'However', 'Therefore' or 'This' cannot open the paragraph.

### Medium patterns

**M1. Order: A. Many employees lost jobs. B. The economy slowed. C. Companies cut costs to survive. D. As a result, unemployment rose.**

**Solution:** **B-C-A-D**: slowdown, cost cuts, job losses, then the result.

**M2. Order: A. The policy allows two remote days. B. A firm has introduced a new policy. C. Employees welcomed it.**

**Solution:** **B-A-C**: 'a new policy' appears first, then 'the policy', then reactions.

### Hard patterns

**H1. Order: A. This led to higher turnover. B. Salaries in the sector had stagnated. C. Many skilled workers moved abroad. D. Firms struggled to retain talent.**

**Solution:** **B-C-A-D**: stagnation, people leave, 'this' refers to them leaving, firms struggle.

**H2. Which sentence cannot open a paragraph: 'However, costs kept rising'?**

**Solution:** A sentence starting with **'However'** cannot open, because it needs a prior statement to contrast with.
