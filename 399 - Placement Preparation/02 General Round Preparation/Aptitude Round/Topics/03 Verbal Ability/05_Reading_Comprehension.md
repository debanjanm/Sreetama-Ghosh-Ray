# Reading Comprehension

> **Difficulty:** Hard  ·  **Target time:** 3–4 min per passage

## In simple words

**The idea:** Answer only from what the passage says, not from what you already know.

**Think of it like:** Looking up information in a company policy document instead of guessing.

**Simple steps:**
1. Skim the passage for the main idea and tone.
2. Read the question, then find the matching lines.
3. Cross out answers that are too strong, too narrow or not in the passage.

**Watch out:** Outside knowledge and extreme words ('always', 'only', 'never') are the usual traps.

## Method
Map each paragraph's role (claim, evidence, counterpoint, conclusion). Read the question, find the supporting sentence, and reject answers that change the scope or strength of the passage.

## Mini passage
“A company piloted short online modules with 80 employees. Attendance was higher than in its previous classroom series. The team did not measure whether employees applied the learning at work.”

1. What improved? **Attendance** in the described comparison.
2. Did the pilot prove better job performance? **No**; application was not measured.
3. Best summary? **Attendance improved, while workplace impact remains unknown.**
4. What follows? A follow-up measure of application would help evaluate impact.

## Tips and traps
For main idea, use the whole passage, not a vivid detail. An inference can be stated or closely paraphrased; do not reject it merely because it resembles the text. “Always/only” often adds unsupported certainty.

## Try it
If the passage reports attendance only, can we infer higher productivity? **Answer:** No.

## More worked patterns

### Pattern 1: Passage: “A pilot allowed employees to choose two remote days. Commute time fell, but collaboration scores were unchanged. The firm will extend the pilot before making a permanent decision.” Can we conclude remote work improved collaboration?

**Solution:** **No.** The passage says scores were unchanged. Reduced commuting supports a different benefit; extension does not prove a permanent policy.

### Pattern 2: Using the same passage, why is the firm extending the pilot?

**Solution:** To gather more evidence before a permanent decision. This is an **inference from the stated sequence**, not a claim that the pilot failed.

### Pattern 3: Passage: “Applicants with prior experience completed the task faster on average; however, the study did not control for training hours.” What limits the conclusion that experience caused faster work?

**Solution:** **Training hours** could explain some of the difference. “On average” also does not imply every experienced applicant was faster.

## Deeper patterns (medium to hard)

### Extra rules and tricks
- Question types: main idea, detail, inference, vocabulary in context, tone, 'NOT/EXCEPT'.
- Tone words: optimistic, critical, neutral, sceptical, cautious. Pick the one the author's wording supports.
- For 'EXCEPT/NOT' questions, test each option against the passage and find the one that fails.
- Inference answers are supported by the passage but not word for word; do not pick options that add outside facts.
- Underline contrast words (but, however, although); the key idea usually follows them.

### Medium patterns

**M1. Passage A: 'Many firms now offer flexible working hours. Supporters argue that flexibility improves morale and reduces absenteeism. Critics note that it can make teamwork harder, since employees are not available at the same time. A survey of 200 firms found that productivity stayed the same in most, though communication costs rose in about a third.' What is the main idea?**

**Solution:** Flexible hours have **both benefits and drawbacks, with mixed evidence**.

**M2. According to Passage A, what do critics say?**

**Solution:** That it can make **teamwork harder** because people are not available at the same time.

### Hard patterns

**H1. From Passage A, which can be inferred: (a) Flexible hours harmed productivity in a third of firms. (b) Productivity did not clearly improve in most surveyed firms.**

**Solution:** **(b)**. The passage says productivity stayed the same in most. A third had higher communication costs, not lower productivity, so (a) is wrong.

**H2. What is the tone of Passage A?**

**Solution:** **Neutral and balanced**; it gives both sides and the evidence.
