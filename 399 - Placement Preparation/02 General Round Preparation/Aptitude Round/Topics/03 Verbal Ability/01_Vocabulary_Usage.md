# Vocabulary, Synonyms, Antonyms, and Usage

> **Difficulty:** Easy to Medium  ·  **Target time:** 30 s

## In simple words

**The idea:** The sentence usually tells you what the word means before you look at the options.

**Think of it like:** Guessing a new word from the people and events around it.

**Simple steps:**
1. Read the whole sentence and guess the meaning in your own words.
2. Look for contrast words (but, however) and clue words.
3. Test each remaining option in the sentence.

**Watch out:** Look-alike words (affect and effect, complement and compliment) are common traps.

## Method
Read the whole sentence and predict meaning, tone, and grammatical role before inspecting options. Use roots or contrast words as clues, then substitute each remaining option.

## Recognise and solve
1. “The instructions were ambiguous, so students asked for clarification.” **Ambiguous** means open to multiple interpretations, not simply difficult.
2. “Her response was candid, not evasive.” **Candid** ≈ frank; antonym in this context ≈ evasive.
3. “The new policy will ___ attendance.” **Affect** (verb) fits; “effect” is usually a noun, though it can be a verb meaning bring about.

## Tips and traps
Near-synonyms differ in intensity or connotation. A word can have multiple senses; sentence context determines which. Learn a new word with one example sentence rather than a memorised synonym only.

## Try it
“The data were inconclusive.” Closest meaning? **Answer:** they did not establish a definite conclusion.

## More worked patterns

### Pattern 1: Choose the meaning of “mitigate” in “The policy aims to mitigate risk.”

**Solution:** **Reduce the severity**. It does not mean eliminate risk entirely; use the sentence context to distinguish the two.

### Pattern 2: Choose the correct word: “The new policy will ___ employee morale.” affect / effect

**Solution:** **Affect** is the verb meaning influence. “Effect” is usually a noun here: “the effect on morale.”

### Pattern 3: Choose the opposite of “concise”: brief, succinct, verbose, compact.

**Solution:** **Verbose** means using more words than needed; the other three align with concise.

## Deeper patterns (medium to hard)

### Extra rules and tricks
- Word parts: un-, in-, dis- (not), pre- (before), post- (after), anti- (against), mal- (bad), bene- (good), -phobia (fear), -logy (study).
- Useful HR and work words: candid, diligent, prudent, meticulous, reluctant, ambiguous, mitigate, scrutinise, retain, redundant, lucrative, tenure, attrition.
- Confusing pairs: affect/effect, principal/principle, stationary/stationery, complement/compliment, advice/advise, accept/except, council/counsel, its/it's.
- Synonym and antonym questions: match tone too (formal, informal, positive, negative).

### Medium patterns

**M1. Synonym of 'meticulous': (a) careless (b) careful (c) rushed (d) vague.**

**Solution:** **Careful**: very attentive to detail.

**M2. Antonym of 'reluctant': (a) unwilling (b) eager (c) slow (d) afraid.**

**Solution:** **Eager**.

### Hard patterns

**H1. 'The company's ___ is to treat every employee fairly.' principle or principal?**

**Solution:** **Principle** means a rule or belief. Principal means head or main.

**H2. 'The manager tried to mitigate the conflict.' Choose the meaning: (a) worsen (b) lessen (c) ignore (d) create.**

**Solution:** **Lessen**.
