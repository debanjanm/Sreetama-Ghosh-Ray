# Idioms and One-word Substitutions

> **Difficulty:** Easy to Medium  ·  **Target time:** 30 s

## In simple words

**The idea:** An idiom is a phrase whose meaning is different from its separate words. A one-word substitution replaces a long phrase with a single exact word.

**Think of it like:** "It's raining cats and dogs" has nothing to do with animals. It just means very heavy rain.

**Simple steps:**
1. Do not translate an idiom word by word. Think of the situation where people use it.
2. Test each option by putting the meaning back into the sentence.
3. For one-word substitutions, look for the key idea in the phrase (knowing all, after death, favouring relatives).

**Watch out:** Learn idioms and words in a sentence, not as a list of isolated pairs. You remember them far better that way.

## Common idioms

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Break the ice | Start a conversation in an awkward situation |
| Hit the nail on the head | Say exactly the right thing |
| Burn the midnight oil | Work late into the night |
| Once in a blue moon | Very rarely |
| A piece of cake | Very easy |
| Cost an arm and a leg | Be very expensive |
| Under the weather | Slightly ill |
| Call it a day | Stop working for now |
| Bite the bullet | Face a difficulty bravely |
| Back to square one | Start again from the beginning |
| On the same page | In agreement |
| Think on your feet | Decide quickly in the moment |

## One-word substitutions

| Phrase | Word |
|---|---|
| Able to speak two languages | Bilingual |
| One who knows everything | Omniscient |
| Whose name is not known | Anonymous |
| Agreed upon by all | Unanimous |
| Happening after death | Posthumous |
| A person who walks on foot | Pedestrian |
| Favouring relatives over merit | Nepotism |
| Rule by the most talented | Meritocracy |
| No longer in use | Obsolete |
| Fit to be eaten | Edible |
| One who hopes for the best | Optimist |
| Copying another's work as your own | Plagiarism |

## Solve
1. "After the merger, the team had to start again from scratch; it was **back to square one**." Meaning: starting over.
2. "The new hire cracked a joke to **break the ice**." Meaning: ease the awkwardness.
3. "A person who knows everything" → **omniscient**.
4. "Favouring relatives in hiring" → **nepotism**, a term HR professionals meet often.

## Tips and traps
Eliminate options that are literal (cats and dogs). Group idioms by theme (work, effort, ease, cost) to remember them. Revise ten idioms and ten words each day instead of one big list.

## Try it
1. "Burn the midnight oil" means? **Answer:** Work late into the night.
2. One word for "agreed by everyone". **Answer:** Unanimous.
3. "Once in a blue moon" means? **Answer:** Very rarely.
