# Data Interpretation

> **Difficulty:** Hard  ·  **Target time:** 90 s per question (a 5-question set in about 6–7 min)

## In simple words

**The idea:** A table or chart is just numbers laid out neatly. You read them and then use percentages, ratios and averages.

**Think of it like:** Reading an HR dashboard: applications, offers and joiners by month.

**Simple steps:**
1. Read the title, units and the period first.
2. Find exactly which numbers the question needs.
3. Choose the right denominator ('of the offers' or 'of the applications').
4. If the options are far apart, round the numbers and estimate.

**Watch out:** You cannot average percentages that come from different totals. Add the numerators and denominators instead.

## Rules and formulas
- Growth%=(later−earlier)/earlier×100.
- Share%=part/total×100; conversion%=output/input×100.
- Combined percentage uses summed numerators and denominators, not the unweighted mean of percentages.

## Recognise the pattern
Read title, period, units, legend, whether categories overlap, and whether values are cumulative. Then identify the correct denominator.

| Period | Applications | Offers | Joined |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 200 | 50 | 40 |
| B | 250 | 60 | 45 |
| C | 300 | 75 | 60 |

## Solve
1. Application growth A→B=(250−200)/200=**25%**.
2. B joining rate=45/60=**75%** (not 45/250).
3. Overall joining rate=(40+45+60)/(50+60+75)=145/185≈**78.4%**.

## Tips and traps
Approximate if options are far apart, calculate precisely if close. For a pie chart, percentages sum to 100 only for mutually exclusive exhaustive slices. Never average period rates with unequal denominators.

## Try it
C offers as a percentage of C applications? **Answer:** 75/300=25%.

## More worked patterns

### Pattern 1: Sales are 80, 100 and 120 units in three years. Find the percentage increase from year 1 to year 3.

**Solution:** Increase=40 on a base of 80; 40/80×100=**50%**. Use the first year as denominator.

### Pattern 2: Departments A and B have 120 and 180 employees; 25% of A and 40% of B are women. What fraction of both departments are women?

**Solution:** Women=30+72=102 of 300; fraction=102/300=**34%**. A plain mean of 25% and 40% ignores group sizes.

### Pattern 3: A chart reports revenue ₹240 lakh and margin 15%. Estimate profit and cost.

**Solution:** Profit=0.15×240=**₹36 lakh**; cost=240−36=**₹204 lakh**. This assumes margin means profit divided by revenue.

## Deeper patterns (medium to hard)

### Extra formulas and shortcuts
- Pie chart: angle = % × 3.6; value = (angle/360) × total.
- Bar or line graph: read the scale first and check for broken axes.
- Missing data: use stated totals, percentages and ratios to rebuild the table before answering.
- Caselets: convert the text into a table first, then answer.
- Approximation: round to 1–2 significant figures when options are far apart, but never when options differ slightly.
- Percent vs percentage point: 20% to 25% is +5 percentage points but +25% change.

### Medium patterns

**M1. A pie chart sector is 72°. Total expenditure ₹5,00,000. Amount?**

**Solution:** 72/360 × 5,00,000 = **₹1,00,000**.

**M2. Revenue ₹400 cr → ₹500 cr; costs ₹300 cr → ₹350 cr. Profit growth?**

**Solution:** Profit 100 → 150, so **50%**.

### Hard patterns

**H1. In a class of 100, 40 are girls. 60% of girls and 50% of boys pass. What percent fail?**

**Solution:** Girls pass 24; boys (60) pass 30; passed 54 so **46%** fail.

**H2. Sales of 1,200 units split A 25%, B 35%, C 40%. Profit per unit A ₹50, B ₹30, C ₹20. Total profit and the biggest contributor?**

**Solution:** A 300×50=15,000; B 420×30=12,600; C 480×20=9,600. Total **₹37,200**; **A** contributes most despite lower sales.
