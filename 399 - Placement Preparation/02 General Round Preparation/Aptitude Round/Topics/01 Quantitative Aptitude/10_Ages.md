# Ages

## Rules and formulas
- Present ages x,y; n years ago x−n,y−n; n years later x+n,y+n.
- The age difference between two people stays constant.
- Ratio changes over time; do not subtract years from ratio components directly.

## Recognise the pattern
“Was twice as old,” “will be,” “sum of ages” → define present ages and form simultaneous equations.

## Solve
1. A is 10 years older than B. In five years, A will be 1.5 times B. Set A=B+10, B+15=1.5(B+5); B=**15**, A=**25**.
2. Parent is 40, child 10. How many years until parent is twice child's age? 40+t=2(10+t), so t=**20**.

## Tips and traps
Check that “years ago” does not yield negative age. Translate each tense separately, and plug your answer back into both conditions.

## Try it
Two ages sum to 50 and differ by 8. **Answer:** 29 and 21.

## More worked patterns

### Pattern 1: A father is three times his son’s age. In 10 years he will be twice his son’s age. Find present ages.

**Solution:** Let son=x, father=3x. Then 3x+10=2(x+10), so x=10. Ages: **10 and 30**.

### Pattern 2: Five years ago A:B=2:3. Today their ages sum to 60. Find present ages.

**Solution:** Five years ago ages were 2k and 3k; today sum=5k+10=60, so k=10. Ages are **25 and 35**.

### Pattern 3: A mother is 24 years older than her child. In 6 years she will be three times the child’s age. Find today’s ages.

**Solution:** Let child=x, mother=x+24. x+30=3(x+6) gives x=6; ages are **6 and 30**.
