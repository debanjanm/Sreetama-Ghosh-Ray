# Partnership

> **Difficulty:** Easy to Medium  ·  **Target time:** 60 s

## Rules and formulas
- Profit share is proportional to **capital × time** (use months for time).
- Equal time → share in the ratio of capitals. Equal capital → share in the ratio of times.
- A working partner often takes a fixed fee or percentage of profit first; the rest is split by capital × time.
- A partner who joins after t months invests for (12 − t) months in a 12-month year.

## Recognise the pattern
"Invest," "join after," "withdraw," "share of profit" → build a capital × months table, simplify the ratio, then split the profit.

## Solve
1. A ₹5,000 and B ₹7,000 for the same time; profit ₹3,600. Ratio 5:7 (12 parts of ₹300): **₹1,500 and ₹2,100**.
2. A ₹6,000 for 12 months, B ₹8,000 for 6 months: 72,000 : 48,000 = 3:2. Profit ₹5,000 → **₹3,000 and ₹2,000**.
3. A ₹4,000 for 12 months, B ₹6,000 for 8 months, C ₹12,000 for 4 months: 48,000 each, so profit ₹9,000 splits **equally, ₹3,000 each**.
4. A (working partner) gets 10% of profit as a fee and invests ₹6,000; B invests ₹4,000 for the same period; profit ₹10,000. Fee ₹1,000; the remaining ₹9,000 splits 3:2 as ₹5,400 and ₹3,600. **A = ₹6,400, B = ₹3,600**.

## Tips and traps
Convert years to months before multiplying. When someone withdraws, count their months only up to withdrawal. Check whether the question gives the ratio of capitals or the ratio of profits; they are different. Write a small table: partner, capital, months, product.

## Try it
1. A ₹10,000 for 12 months and B ₹15,000 for 8 months share ₹8,000 profit. **Answer:** products 120,000 each, so ₹4,000 each.
2. Capitals in ratio 3:4:5 for equal time share ₹6,000 profit. **Answer:** ₹1,500, ₹2,000, ₹2,500.
3. A invests ₹8,000; B joins after 4 months with ₹8,000. Profit ratio? **Answer:** 8,000×12 : 8,000×8 = 96:64 = 3:2.
