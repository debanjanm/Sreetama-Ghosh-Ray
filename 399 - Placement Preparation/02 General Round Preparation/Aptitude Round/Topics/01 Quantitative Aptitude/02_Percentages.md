# Percentages

> **Difficulty:** Easy  ·  **Target time:** 40 s

## In simple words

**The idea:** 'Percent' means 'out of 100'. Every percentage question is: part ÷ whole × 100.

**Think of it like:** A salary hike: compare what you got with what you had before.

**Simple steps:**
1. Decide which number is the whole. For a change, it is the OLD (starting) value.
2. Turn the percent into a multiplier: 20% up is ×1.2, 20% down is ×0.8.
3. For several changes in a row, multiply the multipliers.
4. To undo a change, divide by its multiplier.

**Watch out:** +20% then −20% does not return you to the start (1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96), because the second change works on a bigger number.

## Rules and formulas
- Percentage change = (new−old)/old ×100.
- New value after p% rise/fall = old ×(1±p/100).
- Successive signed changes a%, b% combine to a+b+ab/100 percent.
- To reverse a change, divide by the multiplier; never simply add back the stated percentage.

## Recognise the pattern
“What percent of” → part/whole. “Increase from” → old is denominator. “After a discount/increase, find original” → divide by remaining/growth fraction.

## Solve
1. A value rises from 80 to 100. Rise =20/80=**25%**. The fall from 100 back to 80 is 20/100=**20%**: bases differ.
2. After a 20% reduction, an amount is ₹640. Original =640/0.8=**₹800**.
3. Rise 20%, fall 10%: 1.2×0.9=1.08, an **8% net rise**.

## Tips and traps
Memorise 1/8=12.5%, 1/6≈16.67%, 1/5=20%, 1/4=25%, 1/3≈33.33%. Approximate only if answer choices are sufficiently apart.

## Try it
After a 25% increase a price is ₹500. Original? **Answer:** ₹400.

## More worked patterns

### Pattern 1: A salary rises 25% and then falls 20%. What is the net change?

**Solution:** Use multipliers: 1.25×0.80=1.00, so **no net change**. Equal percentage changes would not cancel because the bases differ.

### Pattern 2: A is 20% more than B. By what percent is B less than A?

**Solution:** Let B=100 and A=120. The decrease relative to A is 20/120×100=**16⅔%**.

### Pattern 3: A shop sold 40% of its stock and then 25% of what remained. What percentage remains?

**Solution:** Start with 100 units: 60 remain, then 0.75×60=45. **45% remains**; 40%+25% is not the sold fraction.

## Deeper patterns (medium to hard)

### Extra formulas and shortcuts
- Price up p% → cut consumption by p/(100+p)×100 % to keep spending equal. Price down p% → raise consumption by p/(100−p)×100 %.
- A is x% more than B ⇒ B is x/(100+x)×100 % less than A. A is x% less than B ⇒ B is x/(100−x)×100 % more than A.
- Population after n years = P(1 ± r/100)ⁿ; use one multiplier per year.
- Percentage points are differences between percentages; percent change divides by the old percentage.
- More fractions: 1/7≈14.29%, 1/9≈11.11%, 1/11≈9.09%, 1/12≈8.33%, 1/16=6.25%, 3/8=37.5%, 5/8=62.5%, 7/8=87.5%.

### Medium patterns

**M1. Sugar price rises 25%. By what percent must a household cut consumption to keep spending the same?**

**Solution:** 25/(100+25)×100 = **20%**.

**M2. In an exam 30% failed Maths, 25% failed English and 10% failed both. What percent passed both?**

**Solution:** Failed at least one = 30+25−10 = 45%, so **55%** passed both.

### Hard patterns

**H1. A town of 20,000 grows 10%, then falls 10%, then grows 20%. Final population?**

**Solution:** 20,000×1.1×0.9×1.2 = 20,000×1.188 = **23,760**.

**H2. A candidate needs 40% to pass, scores 180 and fails by 20 marks. Maximum marks?**

**Solution:** Pass mark = 180+20 = 200 = 40% of total, so total = **500**.
