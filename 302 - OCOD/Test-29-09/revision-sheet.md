## 1. Forces of OC (Unit 1)

- **Change (definition):** any alteration in the overall work environment of an organisation.
- **Nature of change:** results from internal + external pressure; disturbs existing equilibrium/status quo; affects whole organisation, not just one part; can be reactive or proactive.
- **Reactive change** = brought about due to pressure of external forces.
- **Proactive change** = initiated by management on its own to increase effectiveness.
- **Levels of change (3):** Individual level, Group level, Organisational level.
- **Organisational-level change types (4):** Strategic Change, Structural Change, Process-Oriented Change, People-Oriented Change.
- **Change cycle — 8 steps:** (1) Develop new goals/objectives (2) Select an agent for change (3) Diagnose the problem (4) Select methodology (5) Develop a plan (6) Strategy for implementation (7) Implementation of the plan (8) Receive & evaluate feedback.
- **External forces for change (4):** Technology, Marketing conditions, Social changes, Political forces.
- **Internal forces for change (6):** Nature of the workforce, Change in managerial personnel, Deficiencies in existing management structure, To avoid developing inertia, Human resource problems/prospects, Managerial behaviour/decisions.
- **Change agent (definition):** person who initiates change in the organisation to increase organisational effectiveness.
- **Two types of change agents:** External change agents, Internal change agents.
- External change agents = behavioural scientists/consultants; take total view of org as a system; role is process consultation.
- Internal change agents = usually managers trained by consultants; role is primarily implementation/education; role secondary to external agents.
- **Resistance to change — sources:** Individual level and Organisational/Group level (can overlap).
- **4 individual-level reasons for resistance:** Habit, Security, Selective Information Processing, Economic Factors.
- **Group-level resistance factors (2):** Group Inertia, Structural Inertia (group cohesiveness).
- **Kotter & Schlesinger (1979)** — identified **6 strategies** for overcoming resistance to change: Education + Communication, Participation + Involvement, Facilitation + Support, Negotiation + Agreement, Manipulation + Co-optation, Explicit + Implicit Coercion. (Source: "Choosing Strategies for Change," Harvard Business Review, March–April 1979.)

## 2. Basics of OD (supplementary — not in the given PDFs)

- **OD (Organisation Development)** = a planned, systematic, organisation-wide effort, managed from the top, to increase an organisation's effectiveness and health through planned interventions using behavioural science knowledge.
- Commonly attributed definition: **Richard Beckhard** — OD is "an effort planned, organisation-wide, and managed from the top, to increase organisational effectiveness and health through planned interventions in the organisation's 'processes' using behavioural-science knowledge."
- **Key characteristics of OD:** planned change; organisation-wide/whole-system focus; managed from the top; long-term process; uses behavioural science; relies on action research; involves a change agent (often external consultant).
- **OD values (humanistic roots):** respect for people, trust and support, power equalisation, collaboration, confrontation of issues openly, participation.
- OD is distinguished from ad-hoc/one-off change management by being **continuous, data-driven, and collaborative**.

## 3. Action Research (supplementary — not in the given PDFs)

- **Pioneered by Kurt Lewin** — foundational methodology underlying most OD interventions.
- **Definition:** a data-based, problem-solving process that mirrors the scientific method: gather data → feed back to client → jointly plan action → act → re-evaluate.
- **Typical Action Research steps (French & Bell):** (1) Problem identification (2) Consultation with a behavioural science expert (3) Data gathering & preliminary diagnosis (4) Feedback to the client group (5) Joint diagnosis of the problem (6) Joint action planning (7) Action (8) Data gathering after action (cycle repeats).
- Core idea: it is **cyclical/iterative**, not a one-time fix — each action generates new data for the next diagnosis.
- Action research is both a **research method** (generates knowledge) and a **change method** (produces actual improvement) at the same time.

## 4. Models of OC (Unit 2)

- **Kurt Lewin — Force Field Theory / 3-Step Model:** Unfreeze → Change (Move) → Refreeze.
  - Unfreeze = creating awareness of need for change.
  - Change/Move = movement from old state to new state.
  - Refreeze = assurance of permanent change.
- **Burke–Litwin Model** — developed by **Warner Burke & George Litwin**.
  - Distinguishes **first-order change** (transactional, evolutionary, incremental) from **second-order change** (transformational, revolutionary, radical).
  - Distinguishes **organisational climate** (people's perceptions — easy to change) from **organisational culture** (deep-seated assumptions/values — hard to change).
  - Transactional leadership → causes first-order (climate) change; Transformational leadership → causes second-order (culture) change.
- **Ralph Kilmann — 5 Track Model** (five critical leverage points/tracks for organisational change): Culture Track, Management Skills Track, Team-Building Track, Strategy-Structure Track, Reward System Track.
- **Lippitt, Watson & Westley — 7-Phase Model** (an extension of Lewin's 3 steps):
  1. Diagnose the problem
  2. Assess motivation & capacity for change
  3. Diagnosing the client system's problem (clarification)
  4. Establishing alternative routes
  5. Transforming intentions into actual efforts
  6. Stabilising change
  7. Terminal relationship (exit)
- **Kotter's 8-Step Model** (adapted from Kotter, 1996): (1) Create urgency (2) Form a powerful guiding coalition (3) Develop a vision & strategy (4) Communicate the vision (5) Remove obstacles/empower broad-based action (6) Create short-term wins (7) Consolidate gains (8) Anchor change in the corporate culture.
  - Kotter's model is criticised for being **top-down**, time-consuming, and rigid (skipping a step causes problems).
- **Marvin Weisbord — Six-Box Model:** Purposes, Structure, Relationships, Rewards, Leadership, Helpful Mechanisms. (Generic diagnostic framework to assess org functioning.)
- **Sociotechnical Systems (STS) Theory** — term coined by **Eric Trist, Ken Bamforth & Fred Emery** at the **Tavistock Institute**, based on studies of English coal mines (post-WWII era).
  - Core idea: **joint optimisation** of the social system and the technical system together.
  - Key STS concepts: **responsible autonomy** (self-regulating small groups), **adaptability**, **whole tasks**, **minimal critical specification** (precise on *what*, not on *how*).
  - Seminal paper: Trist & Bamforth, 1951.
- **Organisational Design Models** (structural, not "change" models per se, but grouped in this unit): Functional Design, Geographical Design, Product-Oriented Design, Market-Oriented Design, Matrix Structure.
  - Matrix structure = employee reports to **two bosses** at once (functional + project manager) → causes role conflict.
