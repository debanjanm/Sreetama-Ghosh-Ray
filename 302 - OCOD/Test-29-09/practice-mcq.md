## Section A: Multiple Choice (tricky — numbers/names deliberately mixed across models)

**1.** Who coined the term "sociotechnical systems"?
A) Kurt Lewin
B) Marvin Weisbord
C) Eric Trist, Ken Bamforth & Fred Emery
D) Warner Burke & George Litwin

**2.** Kotter's model has 8 steps. Which of the following is NOT one of them?
A) Creating a sense of urgency
B) Assess motivation and capacity for change
C) Forming a powerful guiding coalition
D) Anchoring change in corporate culture

**3.** How many strategies did Kotter & Schlesinger (1979) identify for overcoming resistance to change?
A) 4
B) 5
C) 6
D) 8

**4.** The Burke-Litwin model distinguishes between organisational climate and organisational culture. Which statement is correct?
A) Climate is deep-seated and hard to change; culture is easy to change
B) Climate is people's perceptions and relatively easy to change; culture is deep-seated and hard to change
C) Both are equally easy to change
D) Climate and culture are the same thing in this model

**5.** In the Burke-Litwin model, which leadership style is required to cause second-order (transformational) change?
A) Transactional leadership
B) Autocratic leadership
C) Transformational leadership
D) Laissez-faire leadership

**6.** Ralph Kilmann's model identifies 5 "tracks" for organisational change. Which of these is NOT one of his tracks?
A) Culture Track
B) Reward System Track
C) Communication Track
D) Team-Building Track

**7.** Lippitt, Watson & Westley extended Lewin's 3-step model into how many phases?
A) 5
B) 6
C) 7
D) 8

**8.** In Lippitt's 7-phase model, "Terminal Relationship" refers to which phase?
A) Phase 1
B) Phase 4
C) Phase 6
D) Phase 7

**9.** Marvin Weisbord's Six-Box Model includes all of the following EXCEPT:
A) Purposes
B) Helpful Mechanisms
C) Motivation
D) Rewards

**10.** Which model's three steps are Unfreeze, Change, Refreeze?
A) Burke-Litwin Model
B) Kurt Lewin's Force Field Model
C) Kilmann's 5-Track Model
D) Weisbord's Six-Box Model

**11.** A matrix organisational structure is best identified by which feature?
A) Employees report to a single functional head

**12.** Sociotechnical Systems theory was originally developed from studies of which industry?
A) Automobile manufacturing

**13.** Which of these is an INTERNAL force for change, not an external one?

**14.** How many external forces for change are listed in the notes?
A) 3

## Section B: One-Mark Rapid Fire (fill in the blank / short answer)

1. The process by which people become aware of the need for change is called __________.
2. __________ change goes by labels such as transformational, revolutionary, or radical (Burke-Litwin).
3. The Weisbord Six-Box Model was developed by __________.
4. Name the 4 external forces for organisational change.
5. Name the 6 internal forces for organisational change.
6. Kotter and Schlesinger's article on resistance strategies was published in which journal, and in what year?
7. In Trist & Bamforth's sociotechnical theory, what does "minimal critical specification" mean?
8. OD is described as an effort that is planned, organisation-wide, and managed from __________.
9. Name the 8 steps of Action Research (French & Bell) in order.

## ANSWER KEY

### Section A
1. C — Eric Trist, Ken Bamforth & Fred Emery (Tavistock Institute)
2. B — "Assess motivation and capacity for change" is a Lippitt phase, not a Kotter step (classic trap)
3. C — 6
4. B — Climate = perceptions, easy to change; Culture = deep-seated, hard to change
5. C — Transformational leadership
6. C — Communication Track is not one of Kilmann's 5 (real ones: Culture, Management Skills, Team-Building, Strategy-Structure, Reward System)
7. C — 7 phases
8. D — Phase 7
9. C — Motivation is part of Burke-Litwin, not Weisbord's Six-Box (real boxes: Purposes, Structure, Relationships, Rewards, Leadership, Helpful Mechanisms)
10. B — Kurt Lewin
11. B — Reports to two bosses at once
12. B — English coal mines (Tavistock Institute, post-WWII)
13. C — Change in managerial personnel (internal); Technology, Political forces, Marketing conditions are all external
14. B — 4
15. C — Selective information processing (individual level); Group inertia and Structural inertia are group-level
16. B — Can lead to future problems if people feel manipulated
17. C — Strategic Change
18. B — Role is primarily educational/implementational, secondary to external agents
19. B — Feedback to the client group
20. B — Kurt Lewin

### Section B
1. Unfreezing
2. Second-order (transformational)
3. Marvin Weisbord
4. Technology, Marketing conditions, Social changes, Political forces
5. Nature of the workforce, Change in managerial personnel, Deficiencies in existing management structure, To avoid developing inertia, Human resource problems/prospects, Managerial behaviour/decisions
6. Harvard Business Review, March–April 1979
7. Be precise about *what* has to be done, but not precise about *how* it is done
8. The top
9. Problem identification → Consultation with a behavioural science expert → Data gathering & preliminary diagnosis → Feedback to the client group → Joint diagnosis of the problem → Joint action planning → Action → Data gathering after action (cycle repeats)
