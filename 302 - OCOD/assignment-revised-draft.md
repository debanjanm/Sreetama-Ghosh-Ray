## 1. Organization Profile

Union Bank of India is a public-sector bank headquartered in Mumbai. It was established in 1919 and nationalised in 1969. The Bank provides deposit, lending, payment, digital and other banking services to retail, agricultural, MSME and corporate customers. In August 2019, the Government of India chose UBI as the anchor bank for the amalgamation of Andhra Bank and Corporation Bank. The amalgamation came into effect on 1 April 2020. This made UBI a larger institution with a wider customer base, branch network and employee base. I completed my internship at Union Bank of India, Regional Office Chennai South, Chennai. As an intern, I mainly observed office routines and the coordination involved in banking administration; I did not directly handle customer accounts or confidential information.

## 2. Nature of the Change

This was a planned, structural organisational change: a three-bank amalgamation. It was not simply a change in one policy. The three legacy banks had to function under one identity, with aligned systems, processes, reporting relationships, customer services and HR practices. The change therefore affected employees, customers, managers, technology teams and regulators.

## 3. Why the Change Was Required

The Government's stated objective was to create stronger, more efficient banks through scale, technology and operational synergies. A larger combined bank was expected to have a broader reach and lending capacity, while reducing duplication in support functions. The merger was therefore intended to improve long-term capability, not merely the Bank's size.

## 4. Internal Factors Driving Change

- The need to combine three organisations with separate operating practices and identities.
- Overlap in branches, administrative work and support functions after amalgamation.
- The need to harmonise HR procedures, reporting structures and employee roles.
- The need to integrate customer-facing and back-office technology into common processes.
- The need to build a single organisational identity across former Union Bank, Andhra Bank and Corporation Bank employees.

## 5. External Factors Driving Change

- Government policy on consolidation of public-sector banks.
- Competitive pressure from private banks, fintech firms and digital banking channels.
- Customers' growing expectation of faster, simpler and technology-enabled banking services.
- Regulatory expectations around prudent risk management, capital strength and service continuity.
- The need for public-sector banks to support economic growth through wider lending capacity.

## 6. Stakeholders Affected

The most directly affected stakeholders were employees of all three legacy banks. Branch managers and zonal offices had to implement new processes and guide staff. Customers of the former banks could be affected by changes in service channels, forms, cards, account-related processes or communication. HR and IT teams were essential for harmonising employee practices and systems. Employee associations, shareholders, the Ministry of Finance and the Reserve Bank of India were also important stakeholders because they represented employee concerns, ownership and regulatory oversight.

## 7. Employee Response and Resistance

In a merger, employees may worry about role clarity, transfers, career progression, familiar work methods and loss of organisational identity. The central challenge was helping employees from three legacy organisations feel part of one Bank rather than divided by their earlier affiliation.

# Change Management Strategy: Kotter's Eight-Step Model

Kotter's model is suitable because an amalgamation requires a sequence of planned actions: creating a case for change, coordinating many stakeholders, removing practical barriers and embedding a common culture. The following applies the model to UBI's merger rather than only explaining the theory.

### 1. Create a sense of urgency

The Government's consolidation programme and the need for scale, efficiency and technology capability created urgency. Management should explain this clearly at branch level.

### 2. Build a guiding coalition

The coalition should include senior management, HR, IT, zonal leaders, branch managers and employee representatives from all three legacy banks.

### 3. Form a strategic vision

The vision should be: “One stronger, customer-focused and digitally capable Union Bank, while respecting the useful strengths of each legacy bank.” This is more meaningful than presenting the merger only as a cost-saving exercise.

### 4. Communicate the vision

Use branch briefings, FAQs, intranet updates and town halls. Explain what is changing, where employees can seek help and how customer queries will be handled.

### 5. Remove barriers

Remove barriers through role-based training, a help desk, clear escalation routes and transparent processes for transfers, role allocation and grievances.

### 6. Generate short-term wins

Recognise branches that complete training, resolve transition queries quickly or adopt common processes successfully.

### 7. Sustain acceleration

Review branch feedback regularly and continue training where staff or customers face repeated difficulties.

### 8. Anchor the change in culture

Anchor the change through common processes, cross-legacy knowledge sharing, induction and recognition practices that reinforce collaboration.

# OD Intervention Design

### 1. Communication and grievance-support intervention

Create a monthly branch/zonal briefing, a simple FAQ and a confidential grievance channel to reduce rumours and address concerns.

### 2. Training and development intervention

Provide role-based refresher sessions on common processes, digital tools and customer-query handling, supported by peer learning.

### 3. Culture-integration and team-building intervention

Conduct cross-legacy team sessions to share good practices and agree on common service standards.

### 4. Employee well-being intervention

Offer manager-led check-ins and support for employees facing adjustment, workload or relocation stress.
