## 1. Organization Profile

Union Bank of India is a public sector bank headquartered in Mumbai, set up in 1919. It is among the older nationalised banks in the country. On 1st April 2020, it became the anchor bank in a three-way merger, taking over Andhra Bank and Corporation Bank as part of the government's PSU bank consolidation drive announced in August 2019. After the merger the bank became one of the largest public sector banks in India in terms of business size, with branches spread across almost all states and a workforce running into tens of thousands. ## 2. Nature of the Change

This was a structural change — a merger/amalgamation, not a small policy tweak. Three separate banks with their own systems, staff, culture and customer base had to be combined into one. So it touched almost everything: HR, IT, branch operations, even day to day things like account numbers and passbooks for old Andhra Bank/Corporation Bank customers.

## 3. Why the Change Was Required

The government wanted fewer but stronger public sector banks instead of many small ones struggling individually with bad loans. Union Bank, Andhra Bank and Corporation Bank were all dealing with rising NPAs on their own books, and individually none of them had the scale to compete well with private banks like HDFC or ICICI. Merging them was meant to create a bank with a bigger capital base and better ability to absorb losses.

## 4. Internal Factors Driving Change

- High non-performing assets in all three banks before the merger
- Overlapping branches in the same cities/areas, adding cost without adding value
- Three different core banking systems (Finacle versions differed) that eventually had to be unified
- Different pay scales, promotion policies and seniority lists across the three banks — this became a real headache during integration

## 5. External Factors Driving Change

- Direct government policy — Ministry of Finance announced the consolidation in August 2019
- RBI's push generally for stronger banks after the IL&FS crisis and the NPA problems of the mid-2010s
- Competition from private sector banks and newer fintech players who were growing faster
- Basel III capital requirements pushing banks to hold more and better-quality capital

## 6. Stakeholders Affected

Employees of all three banks (the biggest group affected), branch managers, customers who had to get new account numbers/cards, shareholders, the two employee unions (AIBEA and AIBOC were both vocal about this), RBI and the Finance Ministry as regulators, and IT vendors handling the system migration.

## Change Management Model — Kotter's 8 Steps

Chose Kotter because a merger fits step-by-step rollout better than Lewin's simpler freeze/unfreeze model.

1. **Create urgency** — the NPA problem and direct government order made the case for urgency; this wasn't optional for the banks.
2. **Build a coalition** — top management including the MD/CEO and a steering group coordinated the merger, working with Finance Ministry oversight.
3. **Form a vision** — the pitch was a "stronger, bigger, more competitive" bank rather than three weak ones.
4. **Communicate the vision** — done through internal circulars and some town halls, but based on the union pushback, this step seems to have been weaker at the ground level than at the top. Worth flagging this as a gap in your write-up.
5. **Remove obstacles** — HR policy harmonisation, single seniority protocol, unified core banking rollout.
6. **Generate short-term wins** — cost savings from closing overlapping branches, faster processing once systems were unified.
7. **Build on the change** — continued digital investment after the merger, things like UMobile app upgrades and more digital branches.
8. **Anchor it in culture** — new combined branding, HR fully integrated, single identity established by around 2021-22.

## OD Interventions Recommended

1. **Communication intervention** — regular town halls, a proper FAQ/grievance channel for staff instead of one-off circulars
2. **Training and development** — structured retraining for ex-Andhra Bank/Corporation Bank staff on the new core banking system, not just expecting them to pick it up
3. **Culture change workshops** — sessions to build one shared identity instead of "us vs them" between the three legacy banks
4. **Employee well-being support** — relocation assistance and some kind of counselling/support for staff anxious about transfers or job security
