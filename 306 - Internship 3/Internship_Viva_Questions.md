## A. Placement & Basics

**1. Where did you do your internship and for how long?**
Union Bank of India. Initial posting at Chennai North Zonal Office (19–25 Aug 2026), then Regional Office Chennai South, Royapettah (26 Aug – 6 Oct 2026). 30 working days, 240 hours, including a medical-leave break.

**2. Why were you relocated from the Zonal Office?**
Distance and travel difficulty made daily attendance impractical, so I was moved to Regional Office Chennai South.

**3. What was the focus of your internship?**
Human Resource Management, linked to my dissertation on Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness.

**4. What did you learn at the Zonal Office in the first week?**
How the Zonal Office coordinates with Regional Offices and branches, plus the bank's community initiatives such as The Hindu Made of Chennai Run 2026.

## B. Organisation & HR Structure

**5. Explain the bank's administrative structure.**
Head/Central Office (Mumbai) → Zonal Office → Regional Office → Branches. Policy is framed centrally; the Regional Office implements it.

**6. What does the Regional Office do?**
Administers ~75 branches: welfare, attendance, leave, intra-region transfers, salary/allowance processing, TA/conveyance bills, routine industrial-relations matters.

**7. What is decided at Head Office vs Regional Office?**
Head Office: recruitment, salary structure, benefits, IR and L&D policy. Regional Office: day-to-day implementation, e.g. processing pay and reimbursements.

**8. Why does the day start with prayer and circular reading?**
It is a communication routine: circulars cover performance, NPAs, policy instructions and mandatory training, keeping staff informed.

**9. What is an NPA?**
Non-Performing Asset — a loan on which repayment is overdue beyond the regulatory period.

**10. Which leave is sanctioned at RO level?**
Routine leave. Maternity, sabbatical and study leave need higher approval.

**11. What is the transfer policy?**
Inter-state transfers involve management, unions and Head Office. Within-region transfers are handled by the RO. Clerks must know the local language; officers become eligible after a prescribed tenure.

**12. Describe the disciplinary procedure.**
Issue reported → examined by the concerned authority → report through the proper channel → formal inquiry where necessary. I saw only the broad procedure, not individual cases.

**13. How did you handle confidential records?**
Access was through a temporary ID for a limited demonstration. No employee or customer data was accessed or recorded.

## C. Banking Operations

**14. What is RABD?**
Rural and Agriculture Business Department — agricultural lending, rural development and government-sponsored schemes.

**15. What is Priority Sector Lending (PSL)?**
Banks must lend to identified sectors — agriculture, MSMEs, education, housing, weaker sections — that may lack access to formal credit. It supports financial inclusion.

**16. What did you learn about the Currency Chest?**
Only the broad process: cash distribution, documentation, verification, security and regulatory compliance, with multiple control layers.

**17. What security arrangements did you learn about?**
Guards, CCTV, alarms, fire-safety measures and emergency-response procedures.

## D. Research & Fieldwork

**18. What was your research topic?**
Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness at Union Bank of India.

**19. How many branches did you visit, and how?**
27 branches, plus RO departments (Retail Loans, Currency Chest), administering a questionnaire to officers and clerks during working hours.

**20. Name some branches you visited.**
Triplicane 1–3, Alandur, Teynampet, Mowbrays Road, Chamiers Road, Mylapore 1–3, Adyar, Madhya Kailash, Besant Nagar 1–2, Indiranagar, Shastri Nagar, T. Nagar 1–3, West Mambalam, Nungambakkam, Koyambedu, Virugambakkam, Saligramam, Ashok Nagar 1–3.

**21. Did you collect any customer or confidential data?**
No. Only employees' general training experiences and perceptions.

**22. Was participation voluntary?**
Yes. Employees were approached respectfully, in between customer work, and could decline.

**23. What challenges did you face while collecting data?**
Busy branches, limited staff (e.g. Triplicane 2 had two officers and one cashier), and having to avoid disturbing customer service.

**24. What was your sampling approach?**
Branch-wise convenience coverage of the region — be ready to say it was not random, and name this as a limitation.

**25. What are the limitations of your study?**
One regional office, convenience sampling, self-reported perceptions, and limited time (affected by my medical leave).

## E. Hybrid Learning & Training

**26. What is hybrid learning?**
A mix of classroom, online modules and live-streamed sessions.

**27. What did employees say about it?**
They compared the three modes and how far they could apply learning at work. Digital learning helps where staff can't leave the branch; classroom/live sessions help with discussion and clarification.

**28. How does workload affect training?**
High footfall and thin staffing reduce time for training, so schedules must fit branch operations.

**29. Is training effective just because someone attends it?**
No. Effectiveness depends on retention and application at work.

**30. Why does a bank need continuous training?**
Regulations, technology, products and customer expectations keep changing.

---

## F. Merger & Change Management

**31. Which banks merged with Union Bank of India?**
Andhra Bank and Corporation Bank.

**32. What challenges did employees face after the merger?**
Learning common systems, SOPs, products and reporting requirements.

**33. What is an SOP and why does it matter?**
Standard Operating Procedure — a structured way to do routine work, covering documentation, authorisation and customer service, which keeps work accurate and compliant.

**34. How does training help in change management?**
It explains why the change is happening and helps staff apply revised procedures confidently. One-time orientation is not enough.

## G. Reflection

**35. What were your key learnings?**
HR goes beyond recruitment and pay; confidentiality matters; workload shapes training; hybrid learning works best when matched to conditions; training supports change management.

**36. How did the internship help your dissertation?**
It gave real data from 27 branches and context on how training works in practice.

**37. What would you recommend to the bank?**
Schedule training around branch workload, keep a blend of digital and interactive sessions, and give follow-up support after major changes. (Frame these as suggestions from limited observation.)

**38. What was your biggest personal learning?**
Professional communication and research ethics in a live work setting.

**39. What would you do differently?**
Plan data collection earlier, so a medical break doesn't compress fieldwork; use a larger, more structured sample.
