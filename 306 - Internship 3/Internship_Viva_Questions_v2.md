215. **If you couldn't access internal data, how can your report be called "fieldwork"?** I collected primary questionnaire responses and spoke with employees during Regional Office and branch visits.
216. **Can you claim your findings are valid?** The analysis describes associations within the selected eligible respondents. It does not prove causation or represent all Union Bank employees.
217. **Why didn't you triangulate with management interviews or training records?** Those sources were outside the study's access and design. They would be useful in a permitted follow-up study.
218. **Why did you not observe a training session?** The fieldwork record documents survey collection and orientation, not direct observation of a selected programme. The questionnaire asked employees about their own experience.
219. **How do you separate your observation from your opinion?** I identify a dated branch observation as field context, a questionnaire result as respondent data, and a recommendation as an interpretation.
220. **Why does your report say "may" and "likely" so often?** The study uses a selected cross-sectional self-report sample, so I avoid causal or Bank-wide claims.
221. **What is the strongest part of your study despite the restrictions?** It combines a 30-question instrument, responses from eligible staff, and documented statistical analysis with direct field context.
222. **What is the weakest part?** Non-probability sampling and self-reported, cross-sectional outcomes limit generalisation and causal interpretation.
223. **If you had a free hand, what would you do?** With permission, I would broaden the sample and add later assessments or supervisor-observed work application.

## N. Version 5 follow-up questions

231. **What is the dissertation's single main hypothesis?** Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, and Learner Engagement jointly predict perceived Training Effectiveness among the eligible respondents. The null hypothesis says they do not jointly predict it.
232. **What did the main regression show?** The overall model was significant, *F*(3, 98) = 50.652, *p* < .001, with *R*² = .608 in 102 valid cases. It explains 60.8% of variation in reported Training Effectiveness within this sample.
233. **How can the overall model be significant when two predictors are not individually significant?** The three predictors have overlapping relationships. Together the model predicts the outcome, but after adjusting for the others, only Learner Engagement had a statistically significant unique coefficient (*β* = .532, *p* < .001).
234. **Were merger experiences measured in your dissertation model?** No. Staff conversations about post-merger adaptation informed my internship learning; the dissertation's scored model contains only the four stated training constructs.
