### Regression

The path hypotheses (H1, H1b, H2, H3, H4) were tested using four multiple regression models, each corresponding to one stage of the hypothesized chain.

**Model 1: Learner Engagement regressed on Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence**

**Table 4.8: Regression Model 1 (H1, H1b)**

| Predictor | B | SE | t | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intercept | 2.175 | 0.540 | 4.026 | <.001 |
| Hybrid Learning | 0.357 | 0.110 | 3.250 | .0017 |
| Trainer Competence | 0.050 | 0.088 | 0.573 | .5682 |

R² = .122, F(2,77) = 5.327, p = .0068. Hybrid Learning significantly predicted Learner Engagement (β=.357, p=.0017); since p < 0.05, the null hypothesis for H1 is rejected, and the alternative hypothesis is accepted. This means Hybrid Learning is positively associated with Learner Engagement in this sample. Trainer Competence did not significantly predict Learner Engagement (β=.050, p=.5682); since p > 0.05, the null hypothesis for H1b is retained, and H1b is not supported. Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence together explained 12.2% of the variance in Learner Engagement, an effect carried almost entirely by Hybrid Learning.

**Model 2: Training Effectiveness regressed on Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, and Learner Engagement**

This is reported plainly rather than omitted: Learner Engagement did not significantly predict Training Effectiveness in this sample, a result discussed further in Section 4.3.

### Mediation

The mediation hypotheses (H5a, H5b, H5c) were tested using the Baron and Kenny (1986) causal-steps approach with a Sobel test, following the conditional approach described in Section 3.8. Table 4.12 summarizes the results for each of the three serial mediation steps.

**Table 4.12: Mediation test results**

| Mediation path | Total effect (c) | a-path | b-path | Direct effect (c′) | Indirect effect (a×b) | Sobel z | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H5a: EN mediates HL→TE | .197 (p=.108) | .352 (p=.002) | .199 (p=.114) | .127 (p=.323) | .070 | 1.431 | .152 |
| H5b: TE mediates EN→KR | .096 (p=.465) | .242 (p=.042) | .533 (p<.0001) | -.033 (p=.780) | .129 | 1.895 | .058 |
| H5c: KR mediates TE→JP | -.051 (p=.639) | .526 (p<.0001) | .367 (p=.0008) | -.243 (p=.037) | .193 | 2.831 | .0046 |

For H5a, the total effect of Hybrid Learning on Training Effectiveness was not significant to begin with (c=.197, p=.108), and the indirect effect through Learner Engagement was likewise not significant (Sobel z=1.431, p=.152); the null hypothesis for H5a is retained, and H5a is not supported in this sample.

For H5b, the indirect effect through Training Effectiveness approached but did not clear the conventional significance threshold (Sobel z=1.895, p=.058); this is reported as a marginal, borderline result rather than rounded to either "significant" or "not significant," since a larger sample may resolve it in either direction.

For H5c, the indirect effect through Knowledge Retention was significant (Sobel z=2.831, p=.0046) even though the total effect of Training Effectiveness on Job Performance was not (c=-.051, p=.639); since p < 0.05 for the indirect effect, the null hypothesis for H5c is rejected, and H5c is supported. This pattern, a significant indirect effect alongside a non-significant total effect, is known as inconsistent mediation: the direct effect (c′=-.243, significant and negative) and the indirect effect through Knowledge Retention (positive) run in opposite directions and partly cancel each other out at the total-effect level. Reading only the total effect would have suggested no relationship worth explaining between Training Effectiveness and Job Performance; the mediation analysis reveals a genuine effect operating underneath that a total-effect-only reading would have missed entirely.

The comparative hypotheses (H6, H7) were tested by comparing respondents grouped by training delivery mode (digital-only, classroom-only, blended).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Knowledge Retention (H6) | 3.347 | 3.421 | 3.587 | 1.101 | .3377 |
| Job Performance (H7) | 3.747 | 3.779 | 3.748 | 0.025 | .9757 |

Neither comparison reached significance. For H6, although the blended group's mean Knowledge Retention (3.587) was numerically higher than the digital-only (3.347) and classroom-only (3.421) groups, this difference was not statistically significant (F(2,77)=1.101, p=.3377); the null hypothesis for H6 is retained, and H6 is not supported. For H7, group means on Job Performance were nearly identical across all three delivery modes (F(2,77)=0.025, p=.9757); the null hypothesis for H7 is retained, and H7 is not supported. An independent-samples t-test comparing the blended and digital-only groups specifically on Job Performance confirmed this null result (t≈0.006, p≈.995), the two group means differing by less than one-thousandth of a scale point.

## 4.3 Interpretation of Results (dummy data)

Table 4.14 summarizes the decision reached for each hypothesis tested in Section 4.2.

**Table 4.14: Hypothesis summary**

| # | Hypothesis | Result | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Hybrid Learning → Engagement | β=.357, p=.0017 | Supported |
| H1b | Trainer Competence → Engagement | β=.050, p=.568 | Not supported |
| H2 | Engagement → Training Effectiveness | β=.196, p=.122 | Not supported |
| H3 | Training Effectiveness → Knowledge Retention | β=.537, p<.0001 | Supported |
| H4 | Knowledge Retention → Job Performance | β=.372, p=.0009 | Supported |
| H5a | Engagement mediates HL→TE | Sobel z=1.43, p=.152 | Not supported |
| H5b | Training Effectiveness mediates EN→KR | Sobel z=1.90, p=.058 | Marginal |
| H5c | Knowledge Retention mediates TE→JP | Sobel z=2.83, p=.0046 | Supported (inconsistent mediation) |
| H6 | Blended > digital-only on Knowledge Retention | F=1.10, p=.338 | Not supported |
| H7 | Blended > digital/classroom on Job Performance | F=0.03, p=.976 | Not supported |
| H8 | Integration positively influences KR and JP | — | Not computed in this dummy dataset |

Read individually, ten of this study's eleven hypotheses were addressed quantitatively in Section 4.2 (H8 excluded, since no dummy result exists for it); of these, four were supported (H1, H3, H4, H5c), one showed a marginal, borderline result (H5b), and five were not supported (H1b, H2, H5a, H6, H7). Read as a whole rather than hypothesis by hypothesis, this pattern divides the model's chain into two halves that behaved very differently from one another.

The upstream half of the chain, from Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence through Learner Engagement to Training Effectiveness, held up only partially. Hybrid Learning significantly predicted Learner Engagement, consistent with H1 and with Saroj, Sahney and Sekar's (2026) own acceptance-to-engagement finding, but Trainer Competence did not, and Learner Engagement itself did not go on to significantly predict Training Effectiveness. This means the answer to this study's central research question, whether hybrid learning contributes to training effectiveness through the mechanism of learner engagement, is only partly yes in this sample: hybrid learning is associated with engagement, but engagement is not shown here to carry that effect through to training effectiveness itself.

The downstream half of the chain told a more consistent story. Training Effectiveness strongly predicted Knowledge Retention, and Knowledge Retention in turn predicted Job Performance, exactly as the sequential model proposes. The most theoretically interesting result in this chapter is H5c: although the total effect of Training Effectiveness on Job Performance was not significant on its own, the indirect effect through Knowledge Retention was. This is worth carrying forward into Chapter 5's discussion of contribution: a study that tested only whether Training Effectiveness "affects" Job Performance directly, without testing the specific mechanism through Knowledge Retention, would have concluded there was no relationship to explain, missing this effect entirely. This is the "training mode to training mechanism" argument developed in Chapter 2's gap analysis (Section 2.4), demonstrated concretely here rather than only argued for.

The null results are reported as findings, not omissions. Neither H6 nor H7 found a significant difference between blended, digital-only, and classroom-only respondents on Knowledge Retention or Job Performance respectively; delivery mode alone did not distinguish these three groups in this dataset. Combined with the H2 non-result, this suggests that whatever benefit hybrid learning offers in this sample operates earlier in the chain, through its association with engagement specifically, rather than showing up as a simple group-level difference in outcomes further downstream. H8's status remains genuinely open pending real data, since no dummy result exists to interpret.

## 4.4 Comparison with Previous Studies (dummy data)

This study's central hypothesis, Hybrid Learning predicting Learner Engagement (H1), was supported, consistent with Saroj, Sahney and Sekar's (2026) own finding that blended-learning acceptance predicts engagement in a workplace setting; this alignment on the study's core mechanism is unsurprising, given that this study's own model is closely modelled on theirs. Their second antecedent, instructor competence, was also found to significantly predict engagement, however, whereas this study's equivalent hypothesis (H1b, Trainer Competence predicting Engagement) was not supported in this sample. Several factors distinguish the two studies at this specific path: Saroj, Sahney and Sekar's (2026) sample (n=375) is considerably larger than this study's target sample (n=80), giving it more statistical power to detect a smaller effect, and this study's Trainer Competence construct was compressed to three items during the 30-item instrument redesign (Section 3.9), which may measure the construct less precisely than their own instrument. This divergence is worth investigating further once real data exists, rather than assumed away. It is also worth noting explicitly that Trainer Competence is not tested at all in this study's actual base paper, Ye, Kuang and Liu (2022) — its inclusion here rests on Saroj, Sahney and Sekar's (2026) evidence specifically, discussed in Chapter 2 Section 2.1, a comparatively more exposed position than if the base paper itself had tested it.

This study's downstream path, Training Effectiveness predicting Knowledge Retention and Knowledge Retention predicting Job Performance (H3, H4), extends beyond anything tested in either Saroj, Sahney and Sekar (2026), whose model terminates at employee agility, or in this study's base paper, Ye, Kuang and Liu (2022), whose model terminates at use of blended learning. The closest comparison for the retention stage specifically is Wakekar et al. (2026), who found substantially higher six-month retention among community health workers receiving blended training compared with traditional or e-learning-only training. That finding concerns a delivery-mode group comparison, most directly comparable to this study's own H6 rather than the Training-Effectiveness-to-Knowledge-Retention path tested in H3; on their most comparable measure, the two studies point in different directions in this dummy run, with H6 finding no significant delivery-mode difference in retention where Wakekar et al. found a large one. Differences in population (banking employees rather than community health workers), measurement (a multi-item self-report perception scale here rather than an objective recall test), and retention interval may all contribute to such a divergence, and should be addressed explicitly once real results are available rather than left unreconciled.

No directly comparable study was identified in Chapter 2 testing a mediation chain of the specific kind examined in H5a-c, consistent with the chain-depth gap this study was designed to address (Section 2.4). The inconsistent-mediation finding for H5c therefore has no direct precedent to compare against in the literature reviewed for this study, and stands as this study's own original contribution to the mediation literature regardless of how the real data eventually resolves it.

## 4.5 Theoretical and Practical Implications (dummy data)

Theoretically, these results would qualify rather than simply confirm the explanatory and chain-depth gaps identified in Chapter 2. The explanatory gap, that few studies test engagement as a formal mechanism rather than assuming it, is addressed by this study's design regardless of outcome, but the results themselves suggest the mechanism operates only partially in this sample: Hybrid Learning predicts Learner Engagement, yet Learner Engagement does not go on to significantly predict Training Effectiveness. A future reader should not read this as evidence the engagement-mechanism argument is wrong, only as evidence that, in this particular sample, engagement's role may sit earlier in the chain than originally modelled. The chain-depth gap is addressed more decisively: H5c's inconsistent-mediation finding is a genuine methodological contribution regardless of the specific numbers, demonstrating concretely why testing a full mechanism chain, rather than a single training-mode-to-outcome comparison, can surface effects that a shorter model would miss entirely.

Practically, these results, once real, would point Union Bank of India's Learning and Development function toward the downstream half of its training pipeline as the more reliable lever: Training Effectiveness's strong prediction of Knowledge Retention, and Knowledge Retention's prediction of Job Performance, suggest that ensuring training content is well understood and immediately applicable may matter more, for retention and performance specifically, than the precise mix of digital versus classroom delivery. The non-significant result for Trainer Competence, if it held with real data, would caution against assuming trainer-focused investment alone will move engagement, without first establishing why competent trainers are not translating into stronger engagement in this specific context. As with every number in this chapter, these implications are illustrative of how the argument would be structured, not a substitute for the argument the real data will actually support.
