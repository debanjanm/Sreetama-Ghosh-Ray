Research Gap and Originality — Note for Discussion with Guide

## Research Problem Context

Existing literature confirms that hybrid or blended learning is generally associated with improved learner engagement, satisfaction, and learning outcomes. However, this literature leaves specific gaps unaddressed, summarized below, which this study is designed to address.

## The Research Gap

| # | Gap | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Explanatory gap.** Most studies examine whether hybrid learning improves outcomes, rather than the mechanism through which it does so. | Ashraf et al. (2021), an umbrella review of 57 systematic reviews, finds engagement, satisfaction, and self-regulation studied primarily as outcomes rather than as tested explanatory mechanisms. |
| 2 | **Population gap.** The literature is concentrated in higher education rather than organizational/workplace training. | Bin Mubayrik (2018) identifies only 17 qualifying workplace blended-learning studies against a substantially larger higher-education literature. |
| 3 | **Geographic and sectoral gap.** Very little research addresses hybrid learning specifically within Indian public-sector banking. | Only three India-banking-adjacent studies were identified in the literature review conducted for this study (Jain & Jain, 2015; an unnamed 2024 study in *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*; Joshi & Khan, on Union Bank of India specifically). |
| 4 | **Chain-depth gap.** No identified study tests a full sequence from hybrid learning through engagement, training effectiveness, retention, and job performance within one model. | Saroj, Sahney and Sekar (2026) test engagement as a mediator but conclude at employee agility rather than retention or performance. |

**Summary statement:** While substantial literature confirms that blended and hybrid learning are associated with improved engagement, satisfaction, and learning outcomes, this evidence is concentrated in higher education and does not adequately explain the mechanisms through which hybrid learning contributes to workplace training effectiveness, nor has it been examined within Indian public-sector banking specifically, nor has a full outcome chain from engagement through to job performance been tested. This study addresses these four gaps concurrently in a Union Bank of India employee sample.

## Contribution and Originality of This Study

This study contributes to the literature in the following ways, each corresponding to a gap identified above:

1. **Testing engagement as an explanatory mechanism, not a delivery-mode comparison.** The study models Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence as antecedents of Learner Engagement, which is hypothesized to mediate the relationship to Training Effectiveness — a mediation design rather than a simple comparison of training formats, consistent with the approach taken by Saroj, Sahney and Sekar (2026), the study's base paper.
2. **Sampling employees rather than students**, directly addressing the population gap identified in the literature.
3. **Studying Union Bank of India specifically** — a context with near-zero prior verified research directly addressing this combination of organization and topic.
4. **Testing a full sequential model** — Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence through Learner Engagement, Training Effectiveness, Knowledge Retention, and Job Performance — extending further along the outcome chain than the base paper's own model (which concludes at employee agility) and further than prior research within this department.

## Item for Discussion: Measurement of Knowledge Retention

One aspect of the instrument design would benefit from your review. No published multi-item, self-report perception scale for knowledge retention was identified in the literature reviewed for this study; existing research (e.g., Wakekar et al., 2026) measures retention as a single objective outcome, typically a delayed recall-test score, rather than a multi-item perception scale of the kind developed for this study.

Two approaches were considered:

1. Retain the current multi-item perceived-retention scale, explicitly distinguished from objective retention, on the basis that administering a delayed knowledge test across a live banking workforce was not practicable within the study's data-collection window.
2. Supplement the perception scale with a small number of objective recall items, following Wakekar et al.'s design.

The current instrument adopts the first approach. I would welcome your guidance on whether this is an appropriate scoping decision for this study, or whether the second approach should be incorporated before data collection begins.
