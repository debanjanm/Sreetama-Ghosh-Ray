# 08 — Base Paper: Selection, Search Process, and Model-Alpha Comparison

**What a base paper is, per department convention.** Not something named in the official 2026 guidelines PDF, but a convention your guide clearly expects — confirmed by checking 4 reference theses from the same department ([05-past-dissertation-summary.md](05-past-dissertation-summary.md)), every one of which names a single anchor paper and explains how the student's study narrows or extends it. A base paper is the one published study whose variables, mechanism, and (ideally) measurement approach your model is directly modeled after — not just "a paper I cite somewhere," but the specific study you can point to and say "my model is this paper, adapted."

**Update, 2026-09-10 — guide confirmed the base paper must itself contain a published questionnaire.** The guide shared an example (De Clercq, Thongpapanl & Dimov, 2011, *J Prod Innov Manag*) whose base-paper-equivalent single paper publishes a full "Constructs and Measurement Items" table — every item, every factor loading, in the paper itself — and stated one single paper should be considered for base paper, not a model paper plus a separate instrument paper. Checked directly (full open-access PDF read, not just abstract): **Saroj, Sahney & Sekar (2026), the paper originally selected below (Section 3, kept as history), does not publish its item wording anywhere** — confirmed by direct publisher fetch, not an access problem. Also checked Bahl, Kiran & Sharma (2024) — the Indian-banking Kirkpatrick paper this project already cites elsewhere — the same way: full open-access PDF *and* its supplementary data file read directly; it uses item codes only (T1-T3, L1-L4, B1-B4, R1-R2) with no wording published anywhere, main text or supplement. **Base paper changed to Ye, Kuang & Liu (2022)** — see Section 3b below. Section 3 (Saroj et al.) and Section 4 (the old narrows/extends comparison) are kept as history, not deleted, since Saroj et al. remains cited throughout the project as strong supporting evidence — it is simply no longer the base paper.

## 1. Search Process

Base-paper selection wasn't a separate search — it's the output of the literature work already done across three passes, re-examined specifically for "which one paper is closest":

1. **First-pass search** ([01-initial-literature-search.md](01-initial-literature-search.md)) — ~10 papers, Google Scholar-style web queries on hybrid/blended workplace learning, engagement, training effectiveness.
2. **Systematic matrix** ([03-literature-matrix.md](03-literature-matrix.md)) — expanded to 36 papers/instruments across 10 constructs, queries run against Google Scholar-indexed sources and cross-checked where possible directly against publisher pages (ScienceDirect, SAGE, Emerald, Frontiers, PubMed, Wiley, Taylor & Francis, DOI resolvers). Two entries — including the paper originally selected — were verified by fetching the actual publisher page, not just a search snippet, specifically because they were the strongest candidates and needed the highest confidence.
3. **Targeted follow-up** ([07-model-beta-chain-check.md](07-model-beta-chain-check.md)) — re-fetched the leading candidate directly a second time, specifically to confirm what its model does and doesn't test, before treating it as the anchor for Model-Alpha.
4. **Second round, 2026-09-10** — live re-search specifically for a workplace/blended-learning paper publishing full measurement items, after the guide's requirement above. Checked, live, with full-text fetch where possible: Deb Biswas & Sengupta (2025, Emerald, paywalled — access not available), Matosas-López & Cuevas-Molano (2022, PMC open access, has items but is a single-construct instrument-validation paper with no tested mediation model, higher-ed not workplace), Nabi et al. (2025, ScienceDirect, access blocked, item-publication status unconfirmed), Bahl, Kiran & Sharma (2024, confirmed open, no published items — see update note above).

**Selection criteria applied** (the original five questions, per [02-guide-suggestion.md](../../../chapter-1/version-0/scratchpad/03-guide-suggestion.md) and [04-literature-summary.md](04-literature-summary.md)), **plus a sixth added 2026-09-10 per the guide's explicit requirement**:

1. Closest match to your actual IV→mediator→DV mechanism (not just similar keywords).
2. Genuine workplace/organizational context — not higher education or K-12.
3. Empirical, testable model (mediation/SEM), not a review or meta-analysis.
4. Independently verifiable — fetched and confirmed, not taken on a search snippet alone.
5. Recent enough to reflect current blended-learning practice, not pre-2015 assumptions.
6. **Publishes its own full measurement-item table** — not "researcher-adapted scales" described only in the abstract, and not items available only on request.

No open-access candidate found in either search round satisfies all six at once. Criterion 6 is now weighted as a hard requirement per the guide; Ye, Kuang & Liu (2022) is the strongest candidate that satisfies it, discussed in Section 3b.

## 3. Base paper originally selected (kept as history — see update note above; superseded 2026-09-10)

**Saroj, S., Sahney, S., & Sekar, S. (2026).** "Antecedents and consequences of learners' engagement: examining moderating role of supervisor support in workplace blended learning." *VJIKMS* (Vine Journal of Information and Knowledge Management Systems), Vol. 56, No. 2, pp. 452–474.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| DOI | doi.org/10.1108/VJIKMS-12-2024-0454 |
| Verification | Fetched directly from publisher (emerald.com) — twice, in [03](03-literature-matrix.md) and again in [07](07-model-beta-chain-check.md). |
| Country / context | India — genuine workplace blended-learning training setting (organization not named in the paper) |
| Sample | 375 employees |
| Method | Survey, Structural Equation Modeling (SPSS + AMOS) |
| Variables tested | Blended-learning acceptance, Instructor competence, Metacognitive skills → **Learner Engagement** (mediator) → Employee agility (outcome); Supervisor support (moderator on engagement→agility) |
| Key findings | Acceptance, instructor competence, and metacognitive skills all positively predict engagement; engagement positively predicts employee agility; supervisor support strengthens the engagement→agility relationship |

Still the closest mechanism match found anywhere in this project's literature review, and still used throughout Chapter 2 as the primary supporting citation for the engagement-mediation logic and for Trainer Competence's inclusion (see Section 5 and the note on Trainer Competence's weakened grounding in Section 3b below).

## 3b. Base paper now selected (current)

**Ye, L., Kuang, M., & Liu, S. (2022).** "The Effects of Organizational Support on Blended Learning: The Role of Attitude and ICT Self-efficacy." *Frontiers in Psychology.*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| DOI | doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.941535 |
| Verification | Fetched directly from publisher (Frontiers, open access) — the other highest-confidence entry in the matrix alongside Saroj et al. |
| Country / context | China (Guangdong) — workplace/professional setting, English-language teachers |
| Sample | 562 |
| Method | Survey, PLS-SEM |
| Variables tested | ICT self-efficacy (ICTSE), Organizational support for blended learning (OSBL) → Attitude toward blended learning (**ABL, mediator**) → Use of blended learning (**UBL**, outcome) |
| Key findings | ICTSE and OSBL both positively predict ABL; ABL positively predicts UBL; jointly explain 54.7% of variance in UBL |
| **Published instrument** | **Full item-level scale published in the paper's own appendix** — ICTSE (4 items, α=.924), OSBL (4 items, α=.826), ABL (3 items, α=.884), UBL (6 items, α=.939). 17 items total, all with exact wording given. This is what satisfies the guide's requirement. |

### Why this paper, specifically

- **The only candidate found, across two full search rounds, that satisfies criterion 6** (publishes its own full measurement table) while still being a genuine workplace/professional population, a formally tested mediation model, and independently verified by direct fetch.
- Already the second-highest-confidence entry in the 36-paper matrix before this question even came up — not a paper found under time pressure, one already vetted.
- Already earmarked in [03-literature-matrix.md](03-literature-matrix.md) as a source to re-anchor this study's own technology-dimension items to — so adopting it as base paper is consistent with, not a reversal of, earlier instrument decisions. The actual re-anchoring, done 2026-09-11 by pulling the paper's real Appendix 1, found only HL10 genuinely matches (via ICTSE, not UBL as originally guessed) — see [questionnaire/version-2/01-model-alpha-questionnaire.md](../../../chapter-3/questionnaire/version-2/scratchpad/01-model-alpha-questionnaire.md) HL10-12 notes for the full detail.
- Directly on-topic: blended learning specifically, not training effectiveness in the abstract.

### The real cost — stated plainly, not hidden

Ye, Kuang and Liu's own tested model does **not** include a Trainer Competence construct, an Engagement construct in the sense this study defines it (their mediator is *attitude*, not *engagement*), or any Training Effectiveness / Knowledge Retention / Job Performance chain. Its outcome variable, *use of blended learning*, is a behavioural-intention-style endpoint, not a performance or retention outcome. This means:

- **Model-Alpha now extends this base paper far more substantially than it extended Saroj et al.** — the entire Engagement→Training Effectiveness→Knowledge Retention→Job Performance chain has no precedent in this paper at all, not just a partial one.
- **Trainer Competence's inclusion in Model-Alpha loses its strongest empirical grounding.** Under Saroj et al., Trainer Competence's inclusion could be justified as "tested directly in the base paper." That justification no longer holds. Trainer Competence is now grounded only in the wider literature discussed in Chapter 2 Section 2.1 (the base-paper-comparison subsection there needs updating to reflect this, see [SEQUENCE.md](../../../SEQUENCE.md)), not in the base paper itself. **This is now a more exposed point at your defense than it was before** — expect a direct question on why Trainer Competence is tested when the base paper doesn't test it.
- Saroj, Sahney and Sekar (2026) is still cited heavily as supporting evidence throughout Chapters 2 and 3 — this is not a removal, only a demotion from "base paper" to "closest supporting study."

## 4. How Model-Alpha Narrows and Extends the Base Paper — ORIGINAL comparison vs. Saroj et al. (kept as history, superseded)

| Base paper (Saroj, Sahney & Sekar, 2026) | Model-Alpha (this study) | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Blended-learning acceptance, Instructor competence, Metacognitive skills → Engagement | Hybrid Learning (5-dimension: flexibility/accessibility/interaction/technology/integration) → Engagement | **Narrowed** — single multi-dimensional IV replaces the base paper's three separate antecedents; Instructor competence and Metacognitive skills specifically dropped from formal testing |
| Engagement → Employee agility (single endpoint) | Engagement → Training Effectiveness → Knowledge Retention → Job Performance (3-step chain) | **Extended** — Model-Alpha goes further downstream than the base paper attempts |
| Supervisor support as moderator | Not tested | **Dropped** |
| n=375, unnamed Indian workplace | Target ~80-130, Union Bank of India specifically | **Narrowed context** |

## 4b. How Model-Alpha Narrows and Extends the Base Paper — CURRENT comparison vs. Ye, Kuang & Liu (2022)

| Base paper (Ye, Kuang & Liu, 2022) | Model-Alpha (this study) | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| ICT self-efficacy, Organizational support for blended learning → Attitude toward blended learning (mediator) → Use of blended learning | Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence → Learner Engagement (mediator) → Training Effectiveness → Knowledge Retention → Job Performance | **Substantially extended** — different mediator construct (engagement, not attitude), different antecedent set (adds Trainer Competence, folds technology readiness into Hybrid Learning as one dimension rather than a separate IV), and a three-step outcome chain in place of the base paper's single behavioural-use outcome |
| Mediator: Attitude toward blended learning | Mediator: Learner Engagement | **Replaced**, not extended — this is the single largest structural departure from the base paper; justified by Saroj, Sahney & Sekar (2026) and Fredricks, Blumenfeld & Paris (2004) as supporting theory, not by the base paper itself |
| Outcome: Use of blended learning (behavioural intention) | Outcome chain: Training Effectiveness → Knowledge Retention → Job Performance | **Extended and redirected** — from a usage/adoption outcome to a training-outcome chain |
| Population: 562 English teachers, China | Target ~80, Union Bank of India employees | **Narrowed context and population** — corporate banking rather than education, single Indian organization rather than a regional teacher sample |
| Items: HL10 re-anchored to ICTSE (HL11-12 have no match in Ye et al.'s 17 items, checked directly, kept researcher-developed) | Items: rest of the instrument anchored elsewhere (Boelens et al., Fredricks et al., Koopmans et al., see Chapter 3.3) | Ye et al. supplies only a fraction of this study's own instrument, not the whole thing — the "one paper for the questionnaire" requirement is satisfied by this being the base paper, not by every item in the instrument coming from it |

**One-paragraph version for Chapter 2 (replaces the Section 3 paragraph):**

> This study adopts Ye, Kuang and Liu's (2022) organizational-support and technology-readiness model as its base paper, the only paper identified across two literature-search rounds that both tests a formal mediation model in a genuine workplace setting and publishes its own full measurement instrument. The present study substantially extends the base paper's structure: it replaces the base paper's attitude-based mediator with learner engagement, adds Trainer Competence as a second antecedent, and extends the base paper's single behavioural-use outcome into a three-step Training Effectiveness–Knowledge Retention–Job Performance chain. Saroj, Sahney and Sekar (2026), whose model more closely matches Model-Alpha's own mechanism but does not publish its items, is retained as the primary supporting citation for the engagement-mediation logic and for the inclusion of Trainer Competence specifically.

## 5. The Open Question This Raises

Trainer Competence's inclusion in Model-Alpha now rests on Saroj, Sahney and Sekar (2026) as supporting literature rather than on the base paper itself — a materially weaker position than before this switch, and worth deciding how to present at the defense rather than leaving implicit. The original open item (whether to test Trainer Competence at all) was already logged in [SEQUENCE.md](../../../SEQUENCE.md); this switch reopens it in a sharper form: be ready to explain, directly, why a construct absent from the named base paper is still tested.
