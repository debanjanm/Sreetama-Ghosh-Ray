# 4. Evidence for knowledge retention

This is another very promising variable.

## Wakekar et al. (2026)

### “Blended learning approaches for community health education in tuberculosis control”

This is a **very recent empirical study** involving **248 community health workers**.

Three groups were compared:

* blended learning: 84
* traditional: 82
* e-learning: 82

The researchers measured:

* knowledge acquisition
* practical skill competency
* **knowledge retention after six months**
* satisfaction

The blended-learning group performed better on post-training knowledge, six-month retention, practical competency and satisfaction. ([ScienceDirect][6])

The reported six-month knowledge-retention rates were:

| Training mode | Retention |
| ------------- | --------: |
| Blended       | **78.2%** |
| Traditional   |     62.4% |
| E-learning    |     58.7% |

([ScienceDirect][6])

[Read the PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42362230/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

# 6. Technology readiness / self-efficacy

## Ye, Kuang & Liu (2022)

### “ICT Self-Efficacy, Organizational Support, Attitudes, and the Use of Blended Learning”

This study examined 562 teachers using PLS-SEM.

It found that:

* ICT self-efficacy predicted use of blended learning
* organizational support predicted use
* attitudes toward blended learning had a direct influence
* attitudes mediated relationships involving ICT self-efficacy and organizational support

Together, these variables explained **54.7% of the variance in blended-learning use**. ([Frontiers][8])

[Read the open-access paper](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.941535?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Important distinction

This paper supports **technology readiness/ICT self-efficacy as an antecedent**, rather than necessarily as the main mediator of training effectiveness.

That distinction matters when designing your conceptual framework.
