The engagement questions focus on the behavior of participants in a specific training program. They do not evaluate general work engagement, organisational commitment, motivation, or metacognitive skills as separate factors.
Training Effectiveness
Training effectiveness refers to how much the program helped the employee in their current job. The questions focus on understanding the material, determining its relevance, evaluating confidence in the knowledge, and assessing the ability to use the learning in everyday tasks and related situations.
Training, evaluation, and transfer literature supports this definition, which goes beyond simply asking whether employees liked a session. The evidence is based on self-reports: there is no measurement of any actual improvement in individual performance, customer service, productivity, reputation, or financial outcomes here.
Learning Management System
A Learning Management System (LMS) assists an organization in providing and overseeing learning by utilizing digital tools like modules, readings, videos, quizzes, online meetings, and records of learning activities. Union Bank has identified Union Vidya as its Learning Management System, which is part of a larger system that includes e-learning, virtual training, phased programs, and role-based learning (Union Bank of India, 2024, 2025).
Union Vidya gives background information for the Hybrid Learning questions, and it is not treated as a separate score. The questionnaire refers to it as an example when asking about the platform used in the respondent's chosen program, as programs and platform features can vary.
1.3 Need, Scope and Significance of the Study
Need for the Study
The main issue is understanding how employees' experiences with Hybrid Learning, the skill level of trainers, and the level of engagement from learners connect to their perception of how effective the training is. Bank employees gain knowledge as they handle customer interactions, follow established procedures, and carry out everyday tasks. If activities are hard to reach or not well connected, training may create more stress rather than assist in applying new knowledge.
Research on blended learning provides helpful insights for designing programs, even though a lot of it focuses on students. A bank worker experiences a unique situation: customer service and compliance tasks continue as part of their training. Corporate studies provide evidence from the workplace, but there is still limited research on how these four constructs are experienced by employees working in an Indian public-sector bank.
Union Bank has recorded digital materials and offers training sessions led by trainers. Those records define the context, not the quality of an employee's experience within a particular program. The survey addresses that narrower question.
Scope of the Study
Employees who were linked to Union Bank’s Regional Office Chennai South and had participated in a program that included both digital and in-person learning within the last 12 months were considered eligible. Each person answered about one recent programme. A person who had only experienced one method was unable to understand the connection between the two and was therefore not qualified.
The four areas that were measured are Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, and Training Effectiveness. Other matters that may be relevant, such as knowledge retention, job performance, supervisor support, digital literacy, and the technical performance of Union Vidya, are not covered by this model. A narrower instrument helps keep the analysis focused and manageable for the selected sample.
Responses were gathered once using a structured questionnaire that included a five-point agreement format. Descriptive and correlational analyses provide an overview of this group of respondents and explain the connections between their ratings. The design is not capable of accounting for later changes in work behavior or bank performance that may result from training.
Significance of the Study
For Union Bank, the results organize employees' feedback regarding the relationship between learning methods, trainer support, and level of participation. Lower-rated areas can help staff identify issues that should be reviewed, such as the timing of modules, access to learning materials, the level of guidance provided, the quality of feedback received, or the chances for students to participate actively.
Trainers and those involved in learning and development can utilize these responses to move beyond just delivering content. Employees should be informed about the reason a digital module comes before a session, how a new procedure relates to their job, and where they can get help if they have questions later. Those practical links influence whether training seems feasible to handle along with everyday responsibilities.
The academic contribution is a specific analysis based on a public-sector banking environment. The questionnaire is based on conceptual, corporate, and Indian banking literature; it was reviewed by the guide, tested through piloting, and evaluated for internal consistency in the final sample.
1.4 Statement of the Problem
Union Bank’s learning system offers both digital materials and training sessions led by instructors, providing a mix of online and in-person learning opportunities. The presence of these resources does not necessarily reflect an individual employee's experience. The main questions remain: do the activities seem related, do the trainers provide sufficient guidance, are the employees involved, and can they apply what they've learned in their current positions.
Evidence from higher education and corporate training provides context for these questions, but it does not directly look at the four constructs together within the selected Union Bank environment. In particular, published material about Union Vidya does not clarify how employees link its digital resources with trainer-led learning.
The study examines if the evaluation of Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, and Learner Engagement together influence the perception of Training Effectiveness.
1.5 Research Questions

1.6 Industry and Company Context
Banking Industry Context
Banking work involves handling regulations, digital technologies, financial products, and providing customer support. Employees are required to keep their understanding of procedures and risk controls up to date as they interact with customers on a daily basis. In a public-sector bank where staff are spread across different departments and locations, essential materials should be easily accessible while still allowing for opportunities to ask questions, practice, receive guidance, and later review the content.
Different training needs call for different activities. A brief policy update could be suitable for a digital module and a knowledge check; a new procedure might need a demonstration and hands-on practice; a customer-facing scenario may be more effectively discussed through role play. Digital material can remain available afterwards. The selection of the mode should be based on what the employees need to learn.
