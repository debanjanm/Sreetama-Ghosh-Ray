3.7 Research Instrument and Scoring
Appendix A contains the self-administered 30-question instrument. Questions 1 through 4 determine if someone is eligible and gather information about their profile.Questions 5 to 30 ask the respondent to rate their agreement on a scale from Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree. Respondents mentioned one hybrid program from the past 12 months.
The themes of the items are derived from the foundational papers and relevant measurement literature, with the adaptation process detailed in Appendix B. Union Vidya has been identified as the bank's LMS and is provided as an example of a platform that a respondent might have used.
The four question blocks cover different sections of the same programme. Questions 5 through 11 focus on how materials are delivered and accessed; questions 12 through 17 deal with the support provided by the trainer; questions 18 through 23 relate to the employee's personal participation; and questions 24 through 30 cover the learning outcomes and their usefulness in the workplace. This split gives more insight than a single general opinion about the programme.
Questions 5 through 30 are rated on a scale from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree). In SPSS, the four construct means are referred to as HLTMEAN, TCTMEAN, LETMEAN, and TETMEAN.
Each construct score is calculated as the average of the items it is assigned, and it stays on the 1 to 5 scale; higher scores reflect more positive evaluations. All the items move in the same direction, so none of them need to be reversed. Means enable the comparison of blocks that contain six or seven items. They summarize the respondents' answers, not the observed job performance or the Bank's overall results.

3.10 Questionnaire Review, Reliability and Validity
The approved guide questionnaire was tested online with 20 qualified employees as the main data collection process started. The pilot started on 15 September 2026 and was finished by 16 September; the main survey also began on 15 September using the same approved questions. Pilot feedback included comments on clarity, the amount of time required, the wording used in Union Vidya, the relevance of the content, and instances of repetition. There was no need to remove any item due to a systematic issue, and the wording used for scoring remained the same throughout the collection process. Pilot records were maintained independently from the main analysis.
The pilot also checked the time taken to complete the task: the average time was 14.3 minutes, and the middle value was 14 minutes. Sixteen people thought the length was just right, while four felt it was too long. Comments indicate that there may be some overlap between Questions 5 and 6, and between Questions 14 and 15.Additionally, one person found Question 24 challenging when considering more than one programme. The instruction to respond regarding a single chosen program dealt with that particular concern. The comments did not indicate a recurring issue within the group, and all the questions that were asked were kept.
