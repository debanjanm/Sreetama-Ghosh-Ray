3.11 Ethical Considerations and Limitations
Participation was voluntary. The questionnaire did not ask for any personal information or sensitive bank details, and participants were informed that only the combined results would be used for academic research.
Several design limits affect interpretation. The non-probability sample is not suitable for statistically representing all bank employees, and a single self-reported survey cannot establish a causal relationship. Having the same person evaluate both the programme experience and the outcome can help make the observed connections stronger. The saved SPSS output includes an overall Cronbach's alpha for 26 items, but does not provide separate alphas for individual constructs.Additionally, one record was excluded from the regression analysis because the mean score for Trainer Competence was missing.
Within those boundaries, the results offer a detailed overview of the experiences of eligible respondents regarding four key aspects of training within the Union Bank environment.

Trainer Competence responses
Trainer ratings were mostly favourable. Of the 102 respondents, 92 indicated they either agreed or strongly agreed that the trainer presented the content clearly (Q13), and 91 evaluated the trainer's subject knowledge in those areas (Q12). The numbers show the assistance provided by an individual, something that cannot be fully understood just by looking at online content.
The pattern shows a somewhat less positive trend for connections between different modes and feedback. On question 14, 87 respondents agreed or strongly agreed that the trainer explained the connection between activities, while ten were neutral. On question 17, 88 people provided helpful feedback, while eight disagreed or strongly disagreed. These small differences in the items were not subjected to significance testing, but they indicate the importance of examining how trainers discuss digital preparation and how feedback assists employees in addressing misconceptions.
Learner Engagement responses
Engagement levels were favorable in terms of attention, effort, participation, involvement, asking questions, and showing interest. On each of the questions Q20, Q21, and Q23, 93 out of 102 respondents chose either agree or strongly agree. This aligns with Learner Engagement having the highest construct mean, as it is most often described as active involvement in the program they selected.
Question-seeking showed a slightly different pattern. On question 22, 88 respondents agreed or strongly agreed that they sought clarification, while eleven responded with a neutral stance. One thing that could be neutral is when there was no need for further explanation, no opportunity to start a conversation, or when the employee paused or was unsure. One thing the questionnaire can't do is tell apart those options, so the real question is whether the programs make it simple to ask.
Positive trainer and engagement ratings come from the same sample, but they reflect different experiences. One issue relates to the support provided; the other relates to what employees claim they did. The analysis checks if the latter still has a unique link to Training Effectiveness, along with trainer competence and hybrid design.
Training Effectiveness responses
Training effectiveness includes items that cover understanding, relevance, capability, application, task support, and confidence. In Figure 4.8, 93 people said they either agreed or strongly agreed that they understood the main knowledge or skills (Q24). The total is 89 for each of the questions Q25 through Q28. These are the employees' own accounts of what they have learned and how they might apply it, rather than observations made by their supervisors regarding their performance.
