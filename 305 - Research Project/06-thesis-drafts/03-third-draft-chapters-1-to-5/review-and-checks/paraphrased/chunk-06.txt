Literature Summary
2.2 Theoretical Framework
Blended-Learning Integration
The framework starts by incorporating the learning modes outlined by Garrison and Kanuka (2004) and Graham (2006). Digital work and a live session should be arranged in a clear and logical order. When employees understand the connection, it becomes clearer why each activity is done.
For an employee, the process might begin with digital content that introduces a topic, followed by a session focused on real cases and practical application. The material might still be accessible if it needs to be reviewed again later. A program might also start with a live explanation and then use digital tools for further support. What is important is whether employees understand how their activities relate to the needs of the work.
The design of a program cannot be simplified to the platform it is built on. Posting a module does not guarantee that it will adequately prepare staff for discussions; even a well-designed classroom activity might be difficult to apply effectively in the future without a reliable reference resource. The Hybrid Learning components also inquire about both connectivity and access, not just a login or completion status.
Separate Employee-Level Predictors
The second viewpoint maintains three connected experiences as separate. Kim (2022) talks about the aspects of design, the role of instructors, how participants engage, and the importance of practice in corporate training. Ansari and others in 2023, along with Mulaudzi in 2021, provide additional evidence regarding facilitation and access in different learning modes. Employee engagement can also be influenced by situations that are outside the control of either the trainer or the program. That's why each of the three is measured individually and then all three are inputted together into the regression analysis.
An employee may receive the material and understand the trainer's instructions, yet is unable to take part due to the demands of their branch's work responsibilities. Another person might be enthusiastic about learning but struggle to locate the digital resource. Combining these situations into a single positive or negative rating would obscure which specific part of the experience requires improvement.
The predictors can still move together. Clear guidance can assist employees in completing a digital task, and engaging activities can encourage them to take part. The model enables these connections without requiring that one predictor must lead to engagement in a specific order. Its regression explores how the three ratings are connected to Training Effectiveness when looked at as a group.
Training Evaluation and Transfer
Training evaluation and transfer offer the third viewpoint. Kirkpatrick (1994) differentiates between the immediate response and subsequent results. Holton (1996), Baldwin and Ford (1988), and Blume et al.(2010) talk about how the learner, the design, and the work environment are connected to the application. The result here is the employee's ability to clearly understand, relate to, find value in, and apply the learning, rather than a result that applies to the whole organization.
“Effectiveness” can otherwise mean several different things. Enjoying a trainer's session is a reaction; grasping a concept is learning; applying it to a task is application. A change that affects the entire bank in terms of service or productivity is even more comprehensive. The questionnaire focuses on understanding, the relevance of roles, and the reported use or capability of applying the learning.
Transfer literature also highlights the events that take place following a session. An employee may know how to carry out a procedure but might not have the chance, the time, or the assistance needed to apply it right away. A single survey cannot track a skill's development over several months; it can only note if respondents perceive a link between their training and their current job responsibilities (Baldwin and Ford, 1988; Holton, 1996).

2.3 Case Study: Union Bank of India
The participants were employees associated with the Union Bank's Regional Office in Chennai South. Their job might need them to stay up to date with new information about products, processes, safety measures, digital tools, and how to handle customer interactions. Training supports ongoing work as well as personal growth.
The annual reports cover both digital and instructor-led training programs. The 2023–24 report notes the introduction of Union Vidya as the Bank's Learning Management System, along with digital resources, virtual workshops, and both classroom and on-site training programs. One of the key components of the 2024–25 report is the Master Policy on Learning and Development.This includes provisions for e-learning, virtual training, and staggered programmes.The policy outlines ten mandatory e-learning modules that all officers must complete.It also emphasizes role-based training and the implementation of a Training Management System within Union Vidya.
In 2022, Union Bank introduced nine specialized Union Learning Academies. The public training centres include the Bengaluru Staff Training College and seven other Staff Training Centres located in different locations (Union Bank of India, 2022; Union Bank of India, n.d.-b). These records outline the physical training facilities available, but they do not provide details on each individual employee's specific training program.
An internal notice dated 9 September 2026 mentions that certain classroom sessions at Union Learning Academies and Zonal Learning Centres are being broadcast live via Microsoft Teams. It stores schedules and recordings in Union Vidya and enables virtual attendees to ask questions via chat (Union Bank of India, 2026). This shows how trainer-led sessions are made longer by offering digital access.
These resources make Union Bank a suitable place to examine hybrid learning, but having good infrastructure alone doesn't explain how participants experienced the program. The survey therefore seeks input from eligible employees regarding a recent program that utilized both methods, including the access and support they encountered.
Respondents evaluate their own program instead of the whole training system. They are not required to compare different programs or share customer details, private records, or overall bank performance information.
2.4 Gaps in the Study
The gap between the workplace and academic settings occurs because a lot of the evidence and research on blended learning comes from higher education. Employees train in line with their schedules, customer requirements, and the stress of their work environment. Corporate studies do exist, but they offer less direct evidence regarding these experiences compared to the student literature.
