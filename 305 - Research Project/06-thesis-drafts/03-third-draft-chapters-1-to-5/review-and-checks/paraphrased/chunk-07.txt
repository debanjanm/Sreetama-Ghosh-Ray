The gap in the banking sector still exists, even after the study conducted by Bahl, Kiran, and Sharma in 2024 on the effectiveness of training for Indian bank employees. Their model does not cover Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, or Union Vidya. Union Bank's public documents mention the learning resources they offer but do not explain how employees felt about using different methods within a single program.
The gap between prediction and combination refers to what the three foundational papers do not address collectively. Sosnova examines hybrid approaches in education, Kim analyzes different ways corporations organize their delivery systems, and Bahl, Kiran, and Sharma assess training programs within the banking sector. No study examines the three separate constructs collectively in relation to training effectiveness at the employee level within a public-sector bank.
The gap in measurements arises due to the different scales available: one tool was created for assessing students' experiences in blended learning, while another scale focuses on workplace effectiveness and includes outcomes at the organizational level. Neither fits this short bank survey unchanged. The four sections of the questionnaire were based on existing literature, checked against the guide, and tested online with 20 qualified employees between September 15th and 16th, 2026. SPSS provides an overall measure of consistency for the 26 items, but it does not offer a separate reliability assessment for each individual construct.
The contribution is at the point where these different areas meet: the employee experience with hybrid delivery, trainer support, participation, and how effective they feel the learning process is, specifically within a public-sector bank that uses Union Vidya as part of its learning system.
CHAPTER 3 — RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
3.1 Objectives of the Study
This study explores how eligible Union Bank employees experienced a particular hybrid training program and examines the relationship between their evaluations and their perception of the training's effectiveness. They might use digital resources and participate in sessions led by trainers while still carrying out their regular banking duties. The measure is based on their understanding of that program, not just having an LMS.
The primary objective is:
To assess if Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, and Learner Engagement together influence Training Effectiveness among eligible respondents of Union Bank of India.
The specific objectives are:
1. To describe how respondents perceive Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, and Training Effectiveness. To look into the connections between the four study concepts.3. To figure out how Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, and Learner Engagement together influence Training Effectiveness.
These objectives focus on the relationships and statistical predictions observed in the chosen sample, without implying that one event causes another.
3.2 Hypothesis of the Study
One main regression hypothesis is tested at the 5 percent significance level.
The hypothesis refers to the model as a whole. Coefficients are provided on their own to demonstrate each predictor's adjusted relationship after considering how much they overlap with the other variables.
3.3 Definition of Variables
Four constructs are scored from the questionnaire.
Hybrid learning involves the integration of both digital and in-person activities within a single program. Questions 5 through 11 focus on the design, access to the platform, available materials, and subsequent revisions.
Trainer Competence includes having good subject knowledge, explaining things clearly, connecting different teaching methods, using appropriate digital tools, encouraging learners, and providing helpful feedback. It is measured by Questions 12–17.
Learner Engagement describes how much attention, effort, involvement, questions, and interest a participant shows while taking part in a specific programme. Questions 18–23 measure it. Engagement stays as an independent factor because factors like workload, available time, experience, motivation, confidence in using digital tools, and local circumstances can influence participation in ways that go beyond the design of the program or the support provided by the trainer.
Training effectiveness records measure how well learners understand the content, how relevant the training is to their job roles, their ability to apply what they've learned, their confidence in using the knowledge, and their ability to use it in related tasks. Questions 24–30 ask about these employee-level outcomes.
There are 30 questions in total, numbered from 1 to 30.The first four questions are about eligibility and personal profile, and the remaining 26 questions are five-point agreement items. Knowledge retention, job performance, organisation-level results, employee agility, supervisor support, and Union Vidya’s technical performance are not considered as additional variables that are measured.
3.4 Research Design
The design involves quantitative, descriptive, correlational, and predictive elements in terms of statistical analysis. Structured ratings explain how responses are organized; correlations indicate the direction and strength of relationships between pairs of variables; and SPSS regression evaluates how three independent variables collectively relate to Training Effectiveness.
Each participant filled out the survey once regarding a hybrid program they had attended within the last 12 months. Mentioning a single program reduces the necessity of combining memories from multiple different courses. The individual response record serves as the unit of analysis.
The model is:
The predictors might be connected, but the model does not suggest that the way a program is designed or the skill of the trainer is the main factor in determining engagement. Employee and workplace conditions that are not covered in the questionnaire may also play a role.
3.5 Population, Sample and Sampling Method
The population of interest consisted of employees from Union Bank’s Regional Office in Chennai South who had participated in a program that combined both digital and in-person learning methods within the last 12 months.
Purposive, non-probability sampling was used to select employees who had the necessary experience to answer the questions. Only individuals who had participated in both methods during the specified time frame were considered eligible, as the survey explores the relationship between the two.
The collection was restricted to offices and branches that were available for visitation during the internship period. The eligibility question then checked for relevant training experience. Respondents were not selected randomly from a complete list of all Bank employees, meaning the percentages and connections shown cannot be generalized statistically to every Union Bank employee.
The original goal was to reach around 80 people who qualified to participate. The collection resulted in 132 entries, with 70 submitted on paper and 62 submitted via Google Forms. Screening left 103 profile-complete records. SPSS utilized 102 valid cases for the multiple regression analysis because one record was missing the Trainer Competence mean in the analysis file.
No employee name or number was requested. Each qualified form was considered as a single response entry; identical answer patterns alone were not enough to confirm that the same person submitted the form more than once.
