## 5.1 Summary of Findings

The study examined the relationships among Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, Training Effectiveness, Knowledge Retention, and Job Performance among employees of Union Bank of India. The model was inspired by Uddin et al. (2026), which links blended learning with employee performance through learning-related development. The present study extended this logic by examining Trainer Competence and Learner Engagement before Training Effectiveness and by treating Knowledge Retention as the link between effective training and Job Performance.

The illustrative results support all five proposed relationships. Hybrid Learning had a significant positive relationship with Learner Engagement (β = .39, p < .001). Trainer Competence also had a significant positive relationship with Learner Engagement (β = .31, p = .001). The first regression model explained 32 per cent of the variation in Learner Engagement. This illustrative result suggests that employees may engage more actively with training when the online and classroom parts are accessible, connected, and supported by a competent trainer.

The descriptive results provide useful context for this illustrative pattern. All six construct means were above the neutral midpoint of the five-point scale. Training Effectiveness had the highest illustrative mean (3.75), while Knowledge Retention had the lowest (3.56). The reliability values for all six constructs were above .70. In a final study, these tables would show whether employees report a generally positive training experience and whether the questionnaire provides sufficiently consistent construct scores for further analysis.

Learner Engagement had a significant positive relationship with Training Effectiveness (β = .54, p < .001). The simulated result indicates that employees who pay attention, participate in learning activities, and make an effort to understand the training are more likely to consider the training useful and relevant to their work. Training Effectiveness then had a significant positive relationship with Knowledge Retention (β = .58, p < .001). This suggests that clear, relevant, and applicable training may help employees remember and use important content later.

The strongest illustrative relationship was between Knowledge Retention and Job Performance (β = .61, p < .001). Employees who reported stronger recall of training content also reported better accuracy, quality, efficiency, and application of learning in their work. The result illustrates the importance of treating retention as a separate part of the learning-to-performance pathway. The final study must determine whether the same pattern is present in the actual survey data.

## 5.2 Contributions and Limitations

The study contributes to the literature in three ways. First, it applies workplace blended-learning research to an Indian public-sector banking setting. Second, it uses Uddin et al.’s blended-learning-to-performance logic as a base and extends it through Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, Training Effectiveness, and Knowledge Retention. Third, it examines a connected sequence from the employee’s training experience to perceived task performance. This approach helps distinguish immediate training effectiveness from the employee’s ability to retain and apply learning in the workplace.

The study also has practical relevance for Union Bank of India. If the actual findings support the illustrative pattern, the bank can improve hybrid learning by ensuring that online resources are easy to access, classroom sessions build on digital content, and trainers are able to guide participation in both modes. Refresher materials, follow-up quizzes, and job aids may also help employees retain important training content after a programme ends.

The final findings should be interpreted with several limitations in mind. The study uses convenience sampling and therefore represents the selected respondents rather than every employee of Union Bank of India. It relies on employees’ self-reported perceptions of training, retention, and job performance. The cross-sectional design identifies relationships between constructs at one point in time and does not establish causation. Finally, the 30-question limit requires a concise instrument, so the constructs are measured through a limited number of items. These limitations should be considered when interpreting the actual results and applying them to wider organizational practice.

The study also focuses on employees who have experienced hybrid training. Its conclusions therefore concern the quality of an integrated digital and face-to-face learning experience within the selected setting. They should not be extended automatically to all training programmes, all bank locations, or all organizations. The final report should state any practical constraints encountered during data collection, including the response rate and the period in which the questionnaire was administered.

## 5.3 Future Research Directions

Future research can examine the model with a larger sample drawn from more than one region or public-sector bank. A broader sample would help determine whether the relationships found in one organizational setting are also present in other banking contexts.

Further studies can use a longitudinal design. Measuring knowledge immediately after training and again after several weeks or months would provide stronger evidence on retention. Supervisor ratings, objective knowledge tests, and relevant work-performance indicators could also be used alongside employee perceptions.

Researchers can also examine different kinds of hybrid training, such as compliance training, product training, customer-service training, and system training. These programmes may require different combinations of self-paced digital learning, trainer-led explanation, practice, and follow-up support. Studying the design of specific programmes may help identify which hybrid-learning features are most useful for different work tasks.

Future research may also examine other workplace factors that affect the use of learning after training. Organizational support, supervisor support, workload, and opportunities to apply new knowledge may help explain why employees with similar training experiences report different outcomes. These factors can be added to a larger model after the present six-construct pathway has been tested with an adequate sample.

## Conclusion

This study examines how digital and face-to-face learning can work together in a corporate training setting. The illustrative results show a positive pathway from Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence to Learner Engagement, from engagement to Training Effectiveness, from effective training to Knowledge Retention, and from retention to Job Performance. The central practical idea is that a hybrid programme should connect online learning, trainer support, active participation, and later opportunities to recall and apply learning. The final conclusion will be based solely on the actual survey findings from employees of Union Bank of India.
