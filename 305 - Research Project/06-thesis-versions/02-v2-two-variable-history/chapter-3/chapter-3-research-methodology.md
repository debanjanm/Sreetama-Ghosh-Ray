# Chapter 3 — Research Methodology

## 3.1 Objectives of the Study

This study examines whether employees who report a stronger hybrid-learning experience also report more effective training outcomes. It focuses on a hybrid-training programme as one integrated experience rather than treating digital learning and classroom learning as competing formats. The study is conducted among eligible employees of Union Bank of India connected with Regional Office Chennai South.

The primary objective is:

**To examine the relationship between Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness among employees of Union Bank of India.**

The supporting objectives are:

1. To assess employees’ perceptions of Hybrid Learning in their most recent hybrid-training programme.
2. To assess employees’ perceptions of Training Effectiveness following their most recent hybrid-training programme.
3. To identify the aspects of hybrid delivery that employees experience most positively and least positively.
4. To examine whether Hybrid Learning has a significant positive relationship with Training Effectiveness.

## 3.2 Hypothesis of the Study

| Null hypothesis | Alternative hypothesis |
|---|---|
| **H0:** There is no significant relationship between Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness among employees of Union Bank of India. | **H1:** There is a significant positive relationship between Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness among employees of Union Bank of India. |

The alternative hypothesis is directional because the literature reviewed in Chapter 2 suggests that a hybrid programme is more likely to be effective when its digital and face-to-face parts are connected, accessible, and supported by meaningful interaction.

## 3.3 Definition of Variables

The study has two variables. Hybrid Learning is the independent variable, and Training Effectiveness is the dependent variable.

### Hybrid Learning

Hybrid Learning refers to a planned training programme in which digital learning and face-to-face learning are connected as parts of the same experience. The digital element may include an LMS, online modules, e-learning content, virtual sessions, quizzes, or downloadable materials. The face-to-face element may include classroom sessions, workshops, demonstrations, practice, or trainer-led discussions.

The present study measures Hybrid Learning through 13 statements covering four related aspects:

- integration of digital and face-to-face activities;
- access, usability, availability, and reliability of the LMS or digital platform;
- flexibility and the ability to revisit learning content; and
- trainer guidance, feedback, interaction, and participation across both modes.

The reference to an LMS includes Union Vidya where it was used for the programme. The questionnaire does not assume that every hybrid programme uses the same platform.

### Training Effectiveness

Training Effectiveness refers to the extent to which employees consider a training programme useful and capable of improving their work-related learning and application. It is not limited to whether an employee liked the programme. It includes whether the employee understood the content, found it relevant to the role, could use the learning in work situations, and perceived improvement in relevant work tasks.

The present study measures Training Effectiveness through 13 statements covering:

- understanding and use of the knowledge or skills taught;
- relevance of the programme to work needs;
- transfer and application of learning in day-to-day work; and
- perceived improvement in accuracy, efficiency, work quality, and confidence.

This definition follows workplace training-evaluation logic that distinguishes learning and individual work application from immediate satisfaction alone. It is informed particularly by Kirkpatrick’s evaluation framework, the training-transfer work of Baldwin and Ford, and Aziz’s workplace Training Effectiveness Scale.

## 3.4 Research Design

The study follows a quantitative, descriptive, and correlational research design. It is quantitative because responses are collected through fixed-response statements and analysed numerically. It is descriptive because it reports employees’ views of hybrid learning and training effectiveness. It is correlational because it examines the direction and strength of the relationship between the two construct scores.

The study is cross-sectional. Each respondent completes the questionnaire once and answers with reference to one hybrid-training programme attended during the previous 12 months. The design can identify whether the two variables are associated in the selected respondent group. It cannot establish that hybrid learning causes training effectiveness, because no experimental comparison or repeated measurement is used.

The model is shown below:

```text
Independent Variable                         Dependent Variable
Hybrid Learning               ───────────▶    Training Effectiveness
```

The study does not test trainer competence, learner engagement, knowledge retention, job performance, organizational support, or LMS use as separate variables. These ideas either describe aspects of the hybrid-learning experience or remain outside the present model.

## 3.5 Sampling Methods

The population comprises employees of Union Bank of India who are connected with the selected study setting and who have attended at least one training programme in the preceding 12 months that combined a digital or online component with a face-to-face component.

The study uses non-probability purposive sampling. This method is appropriate because only employees with direct experience of a hybrid-training programme can answer the questionnaire meaningfully. Within the eligible group, questionnaires may be shared with employees who are available and willing to participate after organizational permission has been obtained.

The proposed sample is approximately 80 eligible respondents. The final dissertation will record the actual number of questionnaires distributed, returned, and retained for analysis. The findings will describe the selected respondent group and will not be treated as statistically representative of all employees of Union Bank of India or the wider banking sector.

The eligibility criterion is important for data quality. Employees who have not attended a programme combining digital and face-to-face learning cannot provide an informed response about the integration of modes, the digital platform, or the connection between online and trainer-led activity. The questionnaire therefore begins with a screening question. It does not ask respondents to compare one programme with another or to remember all training attended during their employment.

The profile questions are limited to age group, length of service, and job level. These questions help describe the selected sample but are not included in the hypothesis test. The study has one independent variable and one dependent variable. It does not test whether profile groups differ in their perceptions because such comparisons are outside the focused two-variable design.

## 3.6 Data Collection Procedures

Data collection is planned at Union Bank of India Regional Office Chennai South, 38 and 39 Whites Road, Express Estate, Royapettah, Chennai, Tamil Nadu 600014, subject to academic and organizational approval.

Eligible employees receive a short explanation of the study’s academic purpose, the voluntary nature of participation, and the confidentiality of responses. They are asked to consider their most recent hybrid-training programme and not to provide their name, employee number, customer details, or confidential bank information. The questionnaire may be shared through an approved online form, a printed form, or both, depending on the permission and practical arrangements available at the study site.

Question 1 confirms eligibility. Respondents who have not attended a programme combining digital and face-to-face learning during the last 12 months do not continue with the scale questions. The remaining responses are checked before analysis. Responses that do not contain enough completed items to create a construct score are excluded and the number excluded is reported.

Before distribution, the researcher seeks approval from the academic guide and the appropriate organizational authority. Participation is voluntary. Employees may decline to participate or stop before submitting the form without any effect on their employment, training access, or relationship with the Bank. The questionnaire is designed to avoid sensitive personal information. Results are reported in aggregate form so that an individual respondent cannot be identified.

The researcher stores completed forms and the analysis dataset securely. Access is limited to the researcher and, where required for academic review, the research guide. The final report presents combined tables and does not identify an individual employee, team, branch, training programme, or manager. These procedures are particularly important because respondents are commenting on an organizational learning experience.

## 3.7 Research Questions

1. How do employees perceive Hybrid Learning in their most recent hybrid-training programme?
2. How do employees perceive the Training Effectiveness of their most recent hybrid-training programme?
3. Is there a significant relationship between Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness among the selected employees of Union Bank of India?

## 3.8 Data Analysis Techniques

The questionnaire has 30 numbered questions. Questions 1–4 confirm eligibility and describe the respondent group. Questions 5–17 measure Hybrid Learning, and Questions 18–30 measure Training Effectiveness. All scale items use a five-point response format ranging from Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree.

Responses are coded from 1 to 5. A Hybrid Learning score is calculated from the mean of the completed responses to Questions 5–17. A Training Effectiveness score is calculated from the mean of the completed responses to Questions 18–30. Higher scores indicate a more positive perception of the relevant variable.

The analysis uses the following techniques:

| Technique | Purpose in the study |
|---|---|
| Frequency and percentage | To describe age group, length of service, and job level. |
| Mean and standard deviation | To describe the overall level of Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness and to identify higher- and lower-rated items. |
| Cronbach’s alpha | To assess internal consistency separately for the Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness item sets. |
| Pearson correlation | To examine the direction and strength of the relationship between the two construct scores. |
| Simple linear regression | To examine whether Hybrid Learning significantly predicts Training Effectiveness in the selected sample. |

The regression model is expressed as:

```text
Training Effectiveness = β0 + β1(Hybrid Learning) + error
```

The null hypothesis is rejected when the statistical result is significant at the selected level of significance, ordinarily p < .05, and the relationship is positive. If the result is not significant, the study reports that there is insufficient evidence to reject the null hypothesis.

Before interpreting correlation or regression, the researcher checks the dataset for obvious entry errors, duplicate records where identifiable, and missing responses. Descriptive results are reviewed first. The final report states the number of valid cases used in each analysis. Because the study uses a modest, non-probability sample and self-reported measures, a statistically significant result is interpreted as evidence of an association in the selected respondent group, not as a universal estimate for all Bank employees.

The item-level means are also reviewed as practical feedback. For example, lower Hybrid Learning scores on LMS access or connection between online and face-to-face activities may identify areas that employees find difficult. Lower Training Effectiveness scores on relevance or application may indicate that a programme needs stronger work examples, practice, or follow-up. These descriptive observations do not create extra hypotheses; they help explain the two broad construct scores.

The analysis does not use a comparison between digital-only and classroom-only programmes. Such a comparison would require clearly defined groups and a different research design. It also does not use mediation or multiple-predictor models because the present questionnaire measures only the two broad variables approved for the study. Keeping the analysis aligned with the instrument reduces the risk of making claims that the data cannot support.

## 3.9 Pilot Study, Reliability and Validity

Before the main survey, the questionnaire is piloted with approximately 20 eligible employees who are not included in the final sample. The pilot checks whether the instructions are clear, whether employees understand the phrase “hybrid training,” whether the LMS wording is appropriate, and whether the questionnaire can be completed within a practical amount of time.

Reliability is assessed through Cronbach’s alpha separately for the 13 Hybrid Learning items and the 13 Training Effectiveness items. The pilot results are reviewed alongside employee feedback. An item may be revised when respondents find it unclear, repetitive, or irrelevant to their actual training experience. Any revision is recorded before the main survey so that the final questionnaire and the analysis plan remain consistent.

Content validity is supported by the separate literature review and the questionnaire evidence map. Hybrid Learning items are based on published work on blended-learning integration, digital access, flexibility, interaction, and facilitation. Training Effectiveness items are based on workplace learning, training-transfer, and individual work-improvement literature. The study supervisor reviews the instrument before its use. Face validity is checked during the pilot by asking participants whether the statements are understandable and relevant to a recent hybrid-training experience.

The pilot also checks the wording about the LMS. Employees may know the platform as Union Vidya, by the name of a particular module, or simply as the online training system. The introductory wording therefore refers to the digital platform used for the selected programme and names Union Vidya only as an example. If pilot participants identify an unfamiliar or ambiguous term, the researcher revises the wording without changing the construct being measured.

The final report states the pilot size, the reliability results, and any wording changes made before the main survey. If the pilot shows that the two broad item sets do not have adequate internal consistency, the researcher discusses the result transparently and reviews the items with the guide before interpreting the main analysis. A satisfactory alpha value supports the consistency of the relevant item set, but it does not by itself prove that the questionnaire measures every aspect of hybrid learning or training effectiveness.

The study relies on employee perceptions. It does not use examination scores, supervisor ratings, system log data, customer data, or confidential performance records. This limitation is acknowledged when findings are interpreted. The questionnaire provides a focused account of how employees experience hybrid learning and how they relate that experience to the effectiveness of their training.
