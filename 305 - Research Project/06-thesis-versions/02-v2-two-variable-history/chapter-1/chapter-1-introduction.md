# Chapter 1 — Introduction

## 1.1 Background and Context

Employee learning has become an important part of organizational change. Banks need employees to work with evolving digital systems, understand products and procedures, meet regulatory requirements, and serve customers consistently. Training is expected to support this work. The question is no longer limited to whether an organization provides training. It also concerns whether employees can access the training, understand it, and use it in their daily responsibilities.

One response to this requirement is hybrid learning. Hybrid learning combines digital learning with face-to-face learning in the same training programme. The digital component may include an LMS, e-learning modules, online assessments, videos, virtual sessions, or downloadable material. The face-to-face component may include classroom teaching, workshops, demonstrations, group discussion, role play, and supervised practice. Hybrid learning is useful when each part has a clear purpose and the parts are connected. It is less useful when an online module and a classroom session are offered without a visible relationship between them.

For employees, hybrid learning can provide access to material before a session, allow revision after a programme, and reduce the difficulty of learning at a fixed time and place. A trainer-led session can then be used for clarification, examples, discussion, and practice. This combination is particularly relevant when employees need both factual knowledge and the confidence to apply it in work situations. At the same time, a hybrid programme can create difficulties when the digital platform is unreliable, material is hard to find, online activities are poorly connected with classroom sessions, or employees cannot obtain guidance when they need it.

The quality of the employee’s hybrid-learning experience may influence how effective the training is perceived to be. Training effectiveness in this study is not treated as satisfaction alone. It refers to whether employees consider the training understandable, useful, relevant to their role, and capable of supporting the application of learning at work. This approach follows established training-evaluation and training-transfer literature. A programme may receive a positive reaction from participants, yet still fail to improve understanding or work practice. Conversely, a demanding programme may be effective when it helps an employee perform relevant tasks with greater accuracy, efficiency, or confidence.

The topic is relevant to the banking sector because employees often need to learn new systems, products, compliance requirements, process changes, and service practices. A large public-sector bank also needs to make learning available to employees in different locations and job roles. The use of a digital platform can make training material more widely available, while face-to-face or trainer-led sessions can provide context and practice. The important issue is whether employees experience these elements as one useful training programme.

This study examines the relationship between Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness among employees of Union Bank of India. It does not compare digital-only learning with classroom-only learning. It asks eligible employees about a recent programme that included both digital and face-to-face components. Hybrid Learning is the independent variable, and Training Effectiveness is the dependent variable.

```text
Hybrid Learning → Training Effectiveness
```

The study measures Hybrid Learning through the integration of learning modes, LMS access and usability, flexibility, learning content, interaction, trainer guidance, and feedback. It measures Training Effectiveness through understanding, relevance to work, application of learning, and perceived individual work improvement. The two variables are broad enough to reflect how employees experience a connected training programme, while the model remains manageable for a short survey and the available sample.

The term hybrid learning should not be confused with hybrid work. Hybrid work describes how employees divide their work between office and remote locations. Hybrid learning describes how a training programme combines learning modes. An employee may attend hybrid learning while working entirely from the office, and an employee who works partly from home may still receive classroom-only training. This distinction is important because the present questionnaire concerns the design and experience of training, not an employee’s work location or remote-work policy.

The study also does not assume that a learning platform itself makes training effective. An LMS is a delivery and support system. Its value depends on whether employees can access and navigate it, whether materials are relevant and available, and whether the digital activities are connected to trainer-led learning. The study therefore treats LMS access and usability as parts of the broader Hybrid Learning experience. It does not evaluate the technical performance of Union Vidya or any other platform as a separate organizational system.

The study uses employee perceptions because employees are the people who complete digital modules, attend trainer-led sessions, and decide whether a newly learned procedure can be used in their routine work. Their responses can show whether the programme felt connected and useful from the learner’s point of view. The same method has limits. An employee’s response cannot establish the exact quality of a platform, the quality of every trainer, or an objective change in the Bank’s performance. These distinctions guide the scope and interpretation of the study.

## 1.2 Key Concepts and Definitions

### Hybrid Learning

Hybrid Learning is the planned combination of digital and face-to-face learning within one training programme. It is sometimes described as blended learning. The central feature is integration. The digital and face-to-face activities should complement one another instead of functioning as separate, unrelated tasks.

In the present study, Hybrid Learning includes four connected aspects. The first is the integration of the online and face-to-face components. The second is the employee’s access to and experience of the LMS or other digital platform. The third is flexibility, including the ability to complete digital material at a suitable time and revisit it for revision. The fourth is interaction, trainer guidance, participation, and feedback across both modes.

The study does not treat every form of digital learning as hybrid learning. A digital platform used without a linked face-to-face component does not meet the study definition. Similarly, a classroom programme with an unrelated online notice or document does not meet the definition. The respondent must have attended a programme in the past 12 months that used both connected components.

### Learning Management System

A Learning Management System, or LMS, is a digital platform used to organize, deliver, monitor, or support learning. It can host modules, reading material, videos, quizzes, virtual sessions, announcements, and progress information. An LMS can support hybrid learning by giving employees access to material before or after a face-to-face session.

Union Bank of India identifies Union Vidya as its LMS in its annual report for 2024–25. The report describes the platform as part of the Bank’s learning and development system, with structured learning pathways and access to mandatory and specialized learning. The questionnaire refers to the digital platform used in the respondent’s selected programme. It mentions Union Vidya only where relevant, because the study does not assume that every programme uses the same system or features.

### Training Effectiveness

Training Effectiveness is the extent to which a training programme achieves useful employee learning and supports its application at work. The concept includes understanding the content, seeing its relevance to work, using the knowledge or skill in a real work situation, and perceiving an improvement in the way relevant work tasks are performed.

The study uses an employee-perception measure. It does not use test scores, supervisor ratings, customer information, audit records, or productivity data. Therefore, its findings show how employees relate their training experience to their learning and work, rather than proving an objective organizational result.

### Relationship Between the Variables

The study expects that employees who report a more positive Hybrid Learning experience will also report higher Training Effectiveness. A programme that links digital preparation with face-to-face practice, gives employees practical access to learning material, and provides guidance and feedback may help them understand and use what is taught. This expectation is tested as a relationship, not as proof of causation.

## 1.3 Need, Scope and Significance of the Study

### Need for the study

Organizations increasingly combine digital and in-person learning, but the presence of both modes does not automatically make a programme effective. Employees may find online material convenient but disconnected from their work. They may value a classroom session but be unable to access the digital content that is meant to support it. They may also complete a module without receiving the explanation, feedback, or practice needed to use the learning later.

The need for this study arises from this practical question: when employees experience hybrid training, is the quality of that experience associated with the effectiveness of the training? The question is important for Union Bank of India because the Bank has a documented digital learning system, Union Vidya, and uses a broader Learning and Development approach that includes e-learning, virtual sessions, and staggered programmes. The study can provide focused employee feedback on the connected experience of digital and face-to-face training.

The academic need is also clear. The literature on blended learning is extensive, but much of it concerns higher education. Student learning environments and employee training environments are different. Employees must learn alongside work targets, customer responsibilities, organizational procedures, and role-specific demands. Workplace studies are available, but fewer studies examine hybrid learning and training effectiveness in the setting of an Indian public-sector bank. A focused study among Union Bank employees can contribute evidence from this underrepresented setting.

The study is especially timely because workplace learning increasingly takes place through multiple channels. Employees may receive a notification or module through an LMS, attend a virtual briefing, join a classroom session, consult a job aid, and use a digital refresher later. These activities can help an organization reach a distributed workforce, but they can also make training feel fragmented if their purpose is not clear. By asking about one actual recent programme, the study avoids asking employees to make a general judgement about every training activity offered by the Bank.

### Scope of the study

The study is limited to eligible employees connected with Union Bank of India Regional Office Chennai South. It includes only employees who attended at least one training programme during the previous 12 months that combined digital or online learning with face-to-face learning.

The study examines two variables only: Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness. It does not measure learner engagement, trainer competence, knowledge retention, job performance, organizational support, digital literacy, or supervisor support as separate statistical variables. Some of these matters are reflected within the employee’s Hybrid Learning experience, while others remain outside the present model.

The research uses a structured questionnaire and self-reported responses. It does not compare different groups of employees by mode of training. It does not make a judgement about the quality of Union Vidya as a technical product. It also does not collect confidential operational data or individual performance records.

### Significance of the study

The study may be useful to three groups. First, it can help the Bank understand how employees experience the connection between digital and face-to-face learning. Results may indicate whether employees find the learning platform accessible, whether online material is linked to trainer-led sessions, and whether the programme provides appropriate flexibility and interaction.

Second, the study can support practical discussion of training effectiveness. Employees’ views on understanding, relevance, application, and work improvement can help identify the aspects of a hybrid programme that appear useful and the aspects that need further attention. The findings do not provide an audit of employee performance, but they can offer a starting point for improving programme design and follow-up support.

Third, the study contributes to academic discussion of workplace hybrid learning. It applies a clear two-variable model in an Indian public-sector banking context. It also uses a transparent literature-informed questionnaire that brings together blended-learning environment concepts and workplace training-effectiveness concepts without presenting either as a ready-made bank-specific scale.

## 1.4 Statement of the Problem

Union Bank of India uses digital learning through its Learning Management System and also provides trainer-led and face-to-face learning activities. These resources can support employees who need current work-related knowledge and skills. However, access to a digital platform alone does not show whether employees experience a connected hybrid programme or whether the programme is effective for their work.

The problem addressed by this study is that limited verified evidence is available on the relationship between Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness among employees of Union Bank of India. The available literature includes higher-education studies, general workplace studies, and studies of training transfer. These sources are useful, but they do not directly show how employees in the selected public-sector banking context experience a hybrid programme that uses digital and face-to-face learning together.

The study therefore examines whether employees who report a more positive Hybrid Learning experience also report higher Training Effectiveness. Hybrid Learning is assessed through integration, LMS experience, flexibility, access to learning material, interaction, trainer guidance, and feedback. Training Effectiveness is assessed through learning, work relevance, application, and perceived individual work improvement.

## 1.5 Industry Profile and Company Profile

### Banking industry context

Banks operate in an environment where employees must update their knowledge regularly. New digital services, system changes, regulatory requirements, fraud-prevention practices, product information, and customer-service processes create continuing learning needs. Training must often be delivered to employees in different locations and job roles. A hybrid approach can help an organization provide common learning material through digital channels while retaining opportunities for direct explanation, discussion, and practice.

Training effectiveness is particularly relevant in banking because inaccurate or incomplete application of learning can affect service quality, process compliance, and employee confidence. Employees need more than access to information. They need to understand how the material relates to their role and be able to use it when a relevant work situation occurs. A study of hybrid learning in this setting should therefore consider both the design of the connected programme and the employee’s perceived ability to use what was learned.

### Union Bank of India profile

Union Bank of India is a public-sector bank with a large and geographically distributed workforce. Its annual report for 2024–25 describes Learning and Development as an important organizational activity. The report identifies Union Vidya as the Bank’s Learning Management System and notes that the platform supports structured pathways for mandatory and specialized learning. It also reports the use of e-learning, virtual sessions, and staggered programmes.

This learning environment makes Union Bank of India an appropriate setting for the present study. The Bank’s training system contains the digital and trainer-led elements required for a hybrid-learning context. The study does not assume that all employees use Union Vidya in the same manner or have identical training needs. Instead, it asks eligible respondents about one recent programme that combined digital and face-to-face learning.

The planned study setting is Regional Office Chennai South at 38 and 39 Whites Road, Express Estate, Royapettah, Chennai, Tamil Nadu 600014. Findings will be interpreted as evidence from the selected respondents in this setting. They will not be generalized automatically to all Union Bank employees, all public-sector banks, or all organizations.
