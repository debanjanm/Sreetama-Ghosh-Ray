# Base-Paper Rationale

## Purpose

Version 4 does not force one paper to supply every part of the study. The three selected papers have different and complementary roles. This is more accurate than presenting one study as the source of a model, a corporate context, and a respondent-ready questionnaire when it does not provide all three.

## Selected Papers and Roles

| Paper | Role in the present study | What it contributes | What it does not contribute |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sosnova et al. (2025) | Guide-suggested conceptual anchor | The direct proposition that hybrid education methods can be examined in relation to learning effectiveness. | A corporate sample, bank setting, or employee questionnaire. |
| Kim (2022) | Corporate training-effectiveness anchor | A real corporate comparison of traditional, fully digital, and hybrid training, evaluated through a training-effectiveness framework. | A causal employee-perception path model or a bank-specific instrument. |
| Bahl, Kiran and Sharma (2024) | Indian banking training-effectiveness anchor | Open-access evidence from 402 employees across public, private, and foreign-sector banks in India. It evaluates training through Kirkpatrick’s Reaction, Learning, Behaviour, and Results framework. | Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, or a Union Bank-specific questionnaire. |

## Justification for the Four-Construct Model

The three papers support the following focused model:

    Hybrid Learning ─┐
                     ├──→ Learner Engagement ───→ Training Effectiveness
    Trainer Competence ┘

Sosnova et al. support the broad Hybrid Learning–Effectiveness proposition. Kim shows why that proposition is important in corporate training and why hybrid delivery must be evaluated rather than assumed to be effective. Bahl, Kiran and Sharma establish that Training Effectiveness can be evaluated among bank employees in India. Trainer Competence and Learner Engagement are retained as separate theory- and literature-informed constructs, supported by Kim, Ansari et al., Mulaudzi, and Schaufeli et al., rather than attributed to Bahl.

The model does not reproduce any base paper verbatim. It adapts their compatible ideas for an employee-perception survey in a public-sector banking setting. The study tests relationships through regression. It does not claim to reproduce a controlled experiment, establish causation, or test formal mediation.

## Supporting Measurement Sources

The selected base papers are not treated as complete questionnaire sources. Item development is supported separately by:

- Han and Ellis (2020) for the structure of a blended-learning environment.
- Aziz (2015) for workplace Training Effectiveness measurement.
- Mulaudzi (2021) and Kumar and Pande (2017) for workplace blended-learning wording and delivery conditions.
- Kirkpatrick (1994), Holton (1996), Baldwin and Ford (1988), and Blume et al. (2010) for training evaluation and application at work.
- Union Bank of India (2025) for the documented Union Vidya/LMS and organisational context.
