Union Bank of India — Study Organization: Introduction

## Why This Organization

The study is set at Union Bank of India, a public-sector bank. This note summarizes the organization's background and training infrastructure as context for the research problem — hybrid learning and training effectiveness — for your review.

## Organizational Background

Union Bank of India was registered as a limited company on 11 November 1919 by Seth Sitaram Poddar, head office in Mumbai. Nationalized in 1969, one of the original 14 banks under that wave. On 1 April 2020, Andhra Bank and Corporation Bank were amalgamated into Union Bank of India under the Government's four-bank consolidation programme, making it the fifth-largest public-sector bank in India by scale.

This merger is directly relevant to the study: it required integrating employees from three previously separate organizations into one set of systems, processes, and culture — exactly the kind of large-scale reskilling situation where training delivery effectiveness has real organizational consequences.

## Scale

- ~8,650-8,700 domestic branches
- ~73,900-74,100 employees (FY ending 31 March 2025, per the bank's own disclosures and annual report; a lower figure appears in some secondary/aggregator sources but could not be reconciled with primary sources — treated as unverified)
- Total business (deposits + advances) over ₹23.8 lakh crore as of mid-2026; total assets ~₹14.9 lakh crore for FY2025
- Listed on NSE and BSE; Government of India holds 74.76% equity stake
- Two overseas branches plus a UK banking subsidiary

## Training Infrastructure (why this organization fits a hybrid-learning study)

- **Union Bank Knowledge Centre** — residential staff college at Bannerghatta, Bengaluru
- **Learning and Development Division** at central office, plus a network of regional learning centres
- **Union Vidya** — the bank's mobile e-learning app, providing training content, interactive scenarios, and progress tracking independent of location
- **Union Prerna** — the bank's HR-transformation initiative, described in the bank's own communications as combining "digitalization of processes" with a "learning revolution"
- Golden Peacock National Training Award, awarded ten times (Institute of Directors) — recognition for training-system quality among Indian public-sector banks

Taken together, the bank already runs the combination of centralized classroom training, regional learning centres, and mobile digital learning that constitutes hybrid learning as defined for this study — making it a substantively appropriate setting for the research question, not an arbitrary choice of employer.
