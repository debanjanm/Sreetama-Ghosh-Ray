## Industry Profile: Indian Public-Sector Banking

India's public-sector banking industry traces its modern structure to two nationalization waves — 14 major banks in 1969 and 6 more in 1980 — which brought the bulk of Indian banking under government ownership to direct credit toward national development priorities (Union Bank of India itself was nationalized in 1969) [Union Bank of India — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Bank_of_India). Over the following five decades the sector was gradually liberalized alongside continued state ownership, and in the most recent major structural shift the Government of India consolidated 10 public-sector banks into 4 through amalgamation, effective 1 April 2020 — the transaction that folded Andhra Bank and Corporation Bank into Union Bank of India (below) [Union Bank of India — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Bank_of_India).

The sector today is in the midst of a technology-driven transformation: core banking systems, mobile and internet banking, UPI-based payments, and centralized customer-relationship platforms have become standard infrastructure across public-sector banks, and this shift extends into the workforce-facing side of the business — employee training is increasingly delivered through learning-management systems, mobile learning apps, and blended (classroom-plus-digital) formats rather than classroom-only programmes, driven by the same forces reshaping customer-facing banking: regulatory complexity, cybersecurity requirements, and the pace of product and technology change (this general banking-sector transformation narrative is already documented in [01-introduction-notes.md](01-introduction-notes.md) sections 1.1/1.4, sourced from the guide-provided synopsis). This document adds the piece that was still missing: what this transformation looks like specifically inside Union Bank of India.

## Company Profile: Union Bank of India

### Founding and History

Union Bank of India was registered as a limited company on 11 November 1919, with its head office in Mumbai; the bank was founded by Seth Sitaram Poddar, and its head office building was inaugurated by Mahatma Gandhi in 1921 [Union Bank of India — About Us](https://www.unionbankofindia.bank.in/en/common/profile). Like the rest of the major private banks of its era, it was nationalized by the Government of India in 1969 [Union Bank of India — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Bank_of_India).

In August 2019 the Government of India named Union Bank of India as the "anchor bank" for a three-way amalgamation, and effective **1 April 2020**, Andhra Bank and Corporation Bank were merged into Union Bank of India as part of the government's broader public-sector-bank consolidation programme — the merger made Union Bank the fifth-largest public-sector bank in India by scale [Union Bank of India — About Us](https://www.unionbankofindia.bank.in/en/common/profile); [Union Bank of India — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Bank_of_India). This is directly relevant to a training-effectiveness study: a three-bank merger of this size means Union Bank's current workforce includes large numbers of ex-Andhra Bank and ex-Corporation Bank employees who had to be integrated into a single systems, process, and culture — historically one of the biggest L&D challenges Indian PSU bank mergers create.

### Current Scale

**Branch and ATM network:** the bank's own "About Us" page states 8,650+ domestic branches and 8,700+ ATMs [Union Bank of India — About Us](https://www.unionbankofindia.bank.in/en/common/profile); Wikipedia's more granular figure (also dated 2026) gives 8,697 domestic branches, 2 overseas branches, and 8,656 ATMs [Union Bank of India — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Bank_of_India). These are close enough to be the same underlying figure reported with different rounding/snapshot dates — **use "approximately 8,650-8,700 domestic branches"** rather than a single precise number.

**Employee count — figures genuinely conflict, flagged rather than resolved:**
- Union Bank's own "About Us" page: 74,100+ employees [Union Bank of India — About Us](https://www.unionbankofindia.bank.in/en/common/profile)
- Wikipedia (citing the FY25 annual report): 73,945 employees as of 31 March 2025 [Union Bank of India — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Bank_of_India) — this is close to the bank's own figure and is likely the most reliable anchor point
- A separate Wikipedia-sourced figure surfaced in search put the workforce at ~57,960 as of March 2026, described as a 1.4% increase — this contradicts both figures above and could not be independently verified against a primary source in this research pass

**Recommendation for the thesis: cite the ~73,900-74,100 range, dated to FY2025 (year ended 31 March 2025), and note in a footnote that a lower and internally-inconsistent figure also appears in some aggregator sources** — this is a case where the student (or her internship contact at the bank) confirming the current headcount directly with the bank's HR department would resolve the discrepancy far more reliably than further web search.

**Business size:** total business (deposits + advances) stood at Rs. 23,79,697 crore as of 30 June 2026 (Rs. 12,83,366 crore deposits + Rs. 10,96,331 crore advances) per the bank's own site [Union Bank of India — About Us](https://www.unionbankofindia.bank.in/en/common/profile); Wikipedia separately cites total assets of ₹14,90,323 crore (~US$150 billion) for FY25 [Union Bank of India — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Bank_of_India). Either figure is defensible for a one-line "the bank is a large-scale, systemically significant institution" statement in Chapter 1; precision beyond that isn't necessary for this thesis.

**Geographic presence:** operations across every Indian state, plus 2 overseas branches (Dubai and Sydney), a UK banking subsidiary (Union Bank of India (UK) Limited, London), and four domestic para-banking subsidiaries [Union Bank of India — About Us](https://www.unionbankofindia.bank.in/en/common/profile); Wikipedia additionally lists Hong Kong among overseas locations [Union Bank of India — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Bank_of_India). Notable subsidiaries include Union Asset Management and Star Union Dai-ichi Life Insurance [Union Bank of India — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Bank_of_India).

### Ownership Structure

Union Bank of India is a public-sector bank listed on both the NSE and BSE, with the Government of India holding a **74.76% equity stake** [Union Bank of India — About Us](https://www.unionbankofindia.bank.in/en/common/profile); [Union Bank of India — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Bank_of_India) (both sources agree on this figure).

### Organizational Structure Relevant to HR/Training

Union Bank of India has a dedicated, named training infrastructure — this is the single most useful finding for a thesis on hybrid learning, since it means the bank already runs formal, large-scale, structured employee training rather than ad hoc arrangements:

- **Union Bank Knowledge Centre (UBKC) / Staff College**, located at Bannerghatta, Bengaluru — the bank's central residential training campus, with smart classrooms, an auditorium, conference facilities, an executive hostel for trainees, and a guest house for visiting faculty/management [Union Bank of India — Around the College (UBKC)](https://www.unionbankofindia.bank.in/en/common/around-union-bank-knowledge-centre-ubkc).
- A wider search-engine summary (not independently re-confirmed via direct page fetch, so treat as moderately-confident rather than fully verified) describes the training system as structured around a **Learning & Development Division at Central Office**, supported by **9 Union Learning Academies (ULAs)** and **9 Zonal Learning Centers (ZLCs)** spread across the bank's zones — i.e., training delivery is regionally distributed, not centralized only at Bengaluru. The bank also operates named Staff Training Centres in other cities, including Visakhapatnam and Bhopal [Union Bank of India — Staff College / training centre address list (PDF)](https://www.unionbankofindia.bank.in/pdf/address%20and%20contact%20details%20of%20training%20centres.pdf). **This ULA/ZLC count should be treated as needing re-verification** — e.g. the student confirming the current structure and count directly with her internship/HR contact — before being stated as a precise fact in the final thesis text, since this research pass could not load the specific page carrying those numbers to re-confirm them against a live source.
- The bank's HR careers page frames its training system explicitly as central to its HR strategy, stating that the bank's "training systems have become essential for organizations to shape attitudes, enhance skills, and ignite the knowledge of their workforce" [Union Bank of India — Human Resources](https://www.unionbankofindia.bank.in/en/common/human-resources).
- Union Bank's training system has won the **Golden Peacock National Training Award** — for the **10th time** as of the 2020 award cycle, given by the Institute of Directors, New Delhi, and described by the bank as recognition of having "the best training system among the PSBs in the country" [Union Bank of India — Human Resources](https://www.unionbankofindia.bank.in/en/common/human-resources); [Union Bank of India wins Golden Peacock National Training Award 2020 — Everything Experiential](http://everythingexperiential.businessworld.in/article/Union-Bank-of-India-wins-Golden-Peacock-National-Training-Award-2020/17-07-2020-298564/). At the award ceremony, the bank's then-General Manager (HR–L&D), Vinayak V Tembhurne, stated the bank's training system focuses on "creating successful bankers by using the latest teaching pedagogy and socialising employees into the organisation's culture" [Everything Experiential](http://everythingexperiential.businessworld.in/article/Union-Bank-of-India-wins-Golden-Peacock-National-Training-Award-2020/17-07-2020-298564/) — note this is now a 2020-dated (6-year-old) recognition, not a 2023-2026 one; still useful as evidence of a longstanding, externally-validated training capability rather than a recent one-off.

These are enterprise-technology (not training-specific) awards, but they corroborate that the bank is currently, actively investing in technology adoption broadly — useful supporting context for why a hybrid/digital-learning study is a timely fit for this organization.

## How This Ties to the Thesis

The thesis studies whether well-designed blended (hybrid) learning improves knowledge retention and job performance in an Indian public-sector bank, and through what mechanism (learner engagement, trainer competence). Union Bank of India is a strong fit for this specific question for reasons the profile above establishes directly, not generically:

1. **It already runs the exact training mode the thesis is about.** The bank has a formal, mixed-delivery training system — a large residential Staff College (UBKC, Bengaluru) for in-person/classroom training, a regionally distributed network of Learning Academies and Zonal Learning Centers, *and* a live mobile e-learning platform (Union Vidya) for anywhere/anytime digital delivery. That combination — centralized classroom + distributed centers + always-on digital app — is a real-world instance of the classroom-plus-digital hybrid model the thesis's conceptual model (Model-Alpha: Hybrid Learning + Trainer Competence → Learner Engagement → Training Effectiveness → Knowledge Retention → Job Performance) is built to test, not a hypothetical.
2. **Its 2020 three-way merger makes training effectiveness a live organizational stake, not just an abstract research variable.** Absorbing Andhra Bank's and Corporation Bank's employees into one workforce, one systems environment, and one culture is precisely the kind of large-scale reskilling challenge where the difference between effective and ineffective training delivery has real, measurable consequences — a natural real-world justification for why "how well hybrid training works" is a question worth answering at this specific bank.
3. **The bank's own HR strategy already names "digitalization + learning" as one combined transformation initiative** (Union Prerna), and its training system carries an external, independently-judged track record (10-time Golden Peacock National Training Award winner) — both indicate the bank is a credible, active site to study training effectiveness, not one where digital/blended training is a marginal or token practice.
4. **What still needs the student's own input, not invented here:** which specific branch(es)/department(s)/zone she is drawing her ~80 respondents from; whether her internship placement gives her access to confirm the exact current employee count, ULA/ZLC structure, and Union Vidya adoption details directly with HR rather than relying on public web sources; and any internship-specific observations about how classroom vs. digital training is actually used day-to-day at her specific site — none of that is available from public sources and all of it would meaningfully strengthen this section's specificity.
