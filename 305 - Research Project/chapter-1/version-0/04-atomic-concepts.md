# 1. Start with the atomic concepts

Your topic is:

> **Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness: Making Digital and In-Person Learning Work Together**

Break it into five basic concepts:

**Learning → Hybrid Learning → Training → Training Effectiveness → Workplace Performance**

---

## 2. What is “learning”?

At the most basic level:

> **Learning = a relatively lasting change in what a person knows, can do, or is able to apply, as a result of experience or instruction.**

Think of an employee before and after training.

**Before training:**

Employee doesn't know a new banking procedure.

↓ Training

**After training:**

Employee understands the procedure.

But that's not enough.

If the employee forgets it after two weeks, did the training really work?

This gives us the first important distinction:

**Learning ≠ temporary exposure to information.**

Good learning should produce some degree of **retention and ability to use the knowledge**.

---

# 3. What is “training”?

Training is learning that is deliberately organized to improve someone's **knowledge, skills, abilities, or work behaviour** for a particular purpose.

For your study:

> **Employee training = an organizational intervention intended to improve employees' knowledge, skills and/or job performance.**

For example, a bank might train employees on:

* Digital banking
* Cybersecurity
* Customer service
* Compliance
* New banking software
* KYC procedures
* Financial products
* Risk management

The organization doesn't train employees simply so they can complete a course.

The ultimate objective is:

**Training → better employee capability → better work performance**

---

# 4. What is digital learning?

Digital learning means learning delivered or supported through digital technologies.

Examples:

* Online courses
* LMS modules
* Recorded videos
* Webinars
* Mobile learning
* Online quizzes
* Virtual classrooms
* E-learning modules

Its major advantages are:

**Flexibility + accessibility + self-paced learning + scalability**

But it can have weaknesses:

**Lower face-to-face interaction + distractions + technology problems + reduced personal interaction**

---

# 5. What is in-person learning?

This is traditional face-to-face learning where the trainer and learners interact physically.

Examples:

* Classroom lectures
* Demonstrations
* Role plays
* Group discussions
* Simulations
* Practical exercises
* Question-and-answer sessions

Its major strengths are:

**Interaction + immediate feedback + social learning + practical activities**

But it can have limitations:

**Fixed schedule + location dependence + higher delivery cost + less flexibility**

---

# 6. Now we reach “hybrid learning”

This is the **core concept of your dissertation**.

Hybrid/blended learning essentially means combining technology-mediated learning with face-to-face learning.

But there is an important principle:

> **Hybrid learning is NOT simply “online + classroom.”**

The important word is **integration**.

Imagine this:

### Poor hybrid model

Employee watches three hours of videos online.

Then attends a classroom session where the trainer repeats exactly the same information.

That's technically a combination, but the two modes aren't really working together.

### Better hybrid model

**Before classroom**

Employee completes online modules.

↓

**Classroom**

Trainer discusses difficult concepts, demonstrates procedures and conducts role plays.

↓

**After classroom**

Employee completes digital quizzes/revision.

↓

**Workplace**

Employee applies the learning.

↓

**Follow-up**

Digital assessment/reinforcement checks retention.

That's a genuinely integrated hybrid model.

This distinction is going to be **very important in your literature review**.

---

# 7. What is training effectiveness?

Now ask the fundamental question:

> **How do we know whether training worked?**

That's what **training effectiveness** is about.

At a basic level:

**Training effectiveness = the extent to which training achieves its intended learning and work-related outcomes.**

It can include several levels:

### Level 1 — Reaction

Did employees like the training?

> “I found the training useful.”

### Level 2 — Learning

Did they actually learn?

> “I understand the new banking procedure.”

### Level 3 — Retention

Did they remember it later?

> “I can still recall the procedure after several weeks.”

### Level 4 — Behaviour/application

Can they use it at work?

> “I apply what I learned during my daily work.”

### Level 5 — Performance

Did their work improve?

> Fewer errors, better customer service, faster processing, improved productivity, etc.

This gives you an important chain:

**Training → Learning → Retention → Application → Performance**

# 12. A very important distinction: mediator vs moderator

This will become important later.

### Mediator

A mediator explains **how/why** X affects Y.

For example:

**Hybrid Learning → Learner Engagement → Training Effectiveness**

The question is:

> Does hybrid learning improve engagement, which then improves training effectiveness?

That's mediation.

### Moderator

A moderator explains **when/for whom** X has a stronger or weaker effect.

For example:

**Hybrid Learning → Training Effectiveness**

but the relationship may be stronger for employees with high:

**Digital Literacy**

So:

> Does digital literacy change the strength of the relationship between hybrid learning and training effectiveness?

That's moderation.

You don't need to decide which variables are mediators/moderators yet. **The literature should guide that decision.**

# 14. What I recommend we do next

Now that the concepts are clear, **don't start collecting questionnaires yet**.

The next step should be a proper literature investigation.

For each candidate variable, we need to find:

**Definition → theoretical basis → previous studies → relationship with hybrid learning → relationship with training effectiveness → measurement scale → questionnaire items → reliability (Cronbach's α) → factor analysis → research gap**

Then we can make a decision such as:

> **IV:** Hybrid Learning
> **Mediator:** Knowledge Retention
> **Mediator/Moderator:** Learner Engagement
> **DV:** Training Effectiveness / Workplace Performance
> **Controls:** demographic and job characteristics, if required

—but **only after the literature supports it**.

That approach follows your madam's advice much more closely than simply adding variables to the model.
