The illustrative pattern supports all five relationships in the proposed model. The strongest example is the relationship between Knowledge Retention and Job Performance. The example does not prove that retention causes performance; it only demonstrates the correct way to report a significant association in the final study.

## 4.4 Comparison with Previous Studies

The illustrative pattern is consistent with the general logic of Uddin et al. (2026), the base paper for this study. Uddin et al. link blended learning with employee performance through learning-related development. The present model uses Training Effectiveness and Knowledge Retention to examine a similar learning-to-performance pathway in the Union Bank of India context.

The illustrative positive relationship between Trainer Competence and Learner Engagement is also consistent with Saroj, Sahney and Sekar (2026), who identify instructor competence as a workplace condition associated with learner engagement. The illustrative finding for Hybrid Learning and Learner Engagement reflects the idea that employees are more likely to participate actively when digital and face-to-face activities are accessible, integrated, and supported by interaction.

The positive illustrative association between Training Effectiveness and Knowledge Retention is particularly relevant to workplace training. It suggests that employees may retain learning more effectively when the training is relevant, understandable, and usable in their jobs. The final discussion must compare actual findings carefully with the reviewed studies and explain any relationships that are not statistically significant.

## 4.5 Practical Implications

If the actual findings follow the illustrative pattern, Union Bank of India could focus on the conditions represented by the strongest relationships. The bank could connect online content with classroom practice, ensure that trainers can use digital tools confidently, and create opportunities for employees to ask questions and participate in training activities.

The bank could also use short refresher materials after training, such as digital job aids, quizzes, or follow-up learning modules. These supports may help employees recall training content when they face related tasks at work. The final recommendations should be based only on the actual survey findings and the practical conditions reported by employees.
