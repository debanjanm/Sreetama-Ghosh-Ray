# 06 — Research Gap, Originality, and Honest Tensions

**Purpose.** This file is the single place that answers three questions a panel will ask in sequence: *"What's missing in the literature?"* → *"What are you doing about it?"* → *"Where does your own instrument still fall short of that claim?"* Sections 1-2 make the case for you; Section 3 is deliberately self-critical, because a panel that finds these gaps on its own (without you naming them first) reads worse than you naming them yourself.

## 1. The Research Gap (four distinct gaps, not one)

Don't present this as "not enough studies exist" — that's weak and untrue (dozens of blended-learning studies exist). Present it as four specific, stacked gaps:

| # | Gap | Evidence it exists | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Explanatory gap** — most studies ask *does* hybrid learning work, not *why/how* | Ashraf et al. (2021) umbrella review of 57 systematic reviews finds engagement/satisfaction/self-regulation are studied as outcomes, rarely as explanatory mechanisms in a tested model | [01](01-initial-literature-search.md) sec 15; [03](03-literature-matrix.md) row 5 |
| 2 | **Population gap** — literature concentrated in higher education/students | Same umbrella review; Bin Mubayrik (2018) systematic review of workplace blended learning found only 17 studies (~3,250 participants) vs the much larger higher-ed literature | [03](03-literature-matrix.md) rows 4-5 |
| 3 | **Geographic/sector gap** — almost no Indian-banking-specific blended-training studies | Only 3 India-banking-adjacent papers found in the entire 36-paper matrix (Jain & Jain 2015; unnamed 2024 HSSC paper; Joshi & Khan on Union Bank of India directly) — all three flagged "verify before citing," none independently fetched in full | [03](03-literature-matrix.md) rows 13-15 |
| 4 | **Chain-depth gap** — no study (published or departmental) tests the full engagement→effectiveness→retention→performance chain together in a hybrid-*learning* workplace context | Saroj, Sahney & Sekar (2026) tests engagement as mediator but stops at employee agility, not the retention/performance chain; dept precedent (Hari Kishore 2017) only reached Kirkpatrick Levels 1-2, explicitly could not test 3-4 given a short study window | [03](03-literature-matrix.md) row 1; [05](05-past-dissertation-summary.md) sec 9.2 |

**One-paragraph version for Chapter 1/2:**

> While substantial literature confirms blended/hybrid learning improves engagement, satisfaction, and learning outcomes broadly, this evidence is concentrated in higher education and lacks (a) mechanism-level explanation of *why* hybrid learning works, (b) workplace/organizational samples, (c) Indian banking-sector context specifically, and (d) a tested full outcome chain from engagement through to job performance. This study addresses all four gaps simultaneously in a Union Bank of India employee sample.

## 2. What You're Doing Extra (originality claims)

Four concrete things your project does that the reviewed literature and department precedent do not, mapped directly to the four gaps above:

| Gap closed | What you're doing | Why this counts as "extra," not just "different" |
|---|---|---|
| Explanatory gap | Testing Hybrid Learning → Learner Engagement → Training Effectiveness as a **mediation model**, not a simple format comparison (Model A/B, [03](03-literature-matrix.md) sec 4) | Most department precedent ([05](05-past-dissertation-summary.md) sec 9.1) uses correlation/regression on endpoints only — you're testing a mechanism path, matching the 2026 Saroj et al. workplace design rather than a simpler compare-two-groups study |
| Population gap | Sampling actual **bank employees**, not students | Directly targets the underrepresented workplace population Bin Mubayrik (2018) and Ashraf et al. (2021) both flag as thin |
| Geographic/sector gap | Studying **Union Bank of India specifically** | Near-zero direct precedent exists ([03](03-literature-matrix.md) row 15 — the one Union Bank-specific paper found is itself unverified); you'd be one of the first verifiable studies on this exact organization and topic combination |
| Chain-depth gap | Measuring **four sequential constructs** (Hybrid Learning, Training Effectiveness, Knowledge Retention, Job Performance) in one instrument, reaching further up the Kirkpatrick ladder than any prior MSSW department thesis attempted | [05](05-past-dissertation-summary.md) sec 9.2 confirms no prior dept thesis — including the closest precedent, Hari Kishore (2017) — tested past Kirkpatrick Level 2 due to time constraints; your design is structurally more ambitious |

**The single sentence that ties it together, for your defense:** *"This is the first study in this department's recent cohort — and one of very few in the published literature — to combine hybrid learning delivery (not hybrid work arrangement) with a full Kirkpatrick-chain outcome model, tested in an Indian public-sector banking workforce."*

## 3. Honest Tensions — Say These Before the Panel Finds Them

This section exists because claiming originality without naming your own instrument's weak spots is how a panel catches you off guard. Two tensions, both already logged in [04-literature-summary.md](04-literature-summary.md), restated here because they directly qualify the originality claims in Section 2.

### 3.1 Knowledge Retention (KR1-9) has no published multi-item match

Your KR section is the part of your own instrument with **zero backing from a validated, multi-item, self-report scale** anywhere in the 36-paper matrix. The literature measures retention as a single objective outcome (e.g., Wakekar et al. 2026's 6-month recall percentage: 78.2% blended vs 62.4% traditional), not as a 9-item perception scale like yours.

**This cuts both ways and you should present it as such, not hide it:**
- Read negatively: KR1-9 is "researcher-developed with no external validation" — a weakness if unacknowledged.
- Read positively (and this is the honest framing): **because no one has published a multi-item perceived-retention scale for this context, your KR section is itself a novel contribution**, not just a gap-filler. State explicitly that you chose a self-report perception measure over an objective delayed test because administering a knowledge test across a live banking workforce isn't practically feasible in your data-collection window — this is a scoping decision, not an oversight.

Two paths forward (per [04](04-literature-summary.md) sec 4), pick one and say so in Chapter 3:
1. Keep KR1-9 as-is, explicitly labelled *perceived* retention, distinct from *objective* retention.
2. Add 3-5 short factual recall questions (scored correct/incorrect) alongside KR1-9, following Wakekar et al.'s design logic, if you want to strengthen validity.

### 3.2 Your literature review's own top mechanism picks are missing from your actual questionnaire

This is the sharper tension. Sections 1-2 above argue your project's originality rests on *explaining the mechanism* behind hybrid learning's effect — but your literature review ([01](01-initial-literature-search.md), [03](03-literature-matrix.md)) independently concludes the three strongest mechanism variables are **learner engagement, trainer competence, and organizational/supervisor support** (per Saroj, Sahney & Sekar 2026 — your single best-verified source). None of the three currently appear as a section in your drafted 42-item questionnaire ([01](../../../chapter-3/questionnaire/version-0/scratchpad/01-base-questionnaire-and-calculation.md)/[02](../../../chapter-3/questionnaire/version-0/scratchpad/02-refined-questionnaire.md)).

**Put bluntly: your Chapter 1/2 gap argument says "we need mechanism variables," and your Chapter 3 instrument doesn't measure any.** A panel that reads both chapters back-to-back will catch this immediately — better you name it first.

**This is a decision, not an oversight, and it's yours + your supervisor's to make** (already flagged as an open decision in [03](03-literature-matrix.md) sec 4 and [04](04-literature-summary.md) sec 6):

- **Option 1 — Close the gap:** add short sections for engagement (5 items, STE-S — training-session-specific, or 9 items, UWES-9 — cross-nationally validated) and optionally trainer competence (8-item short form) and org support (8-item SPOS). This fully aligns Chapter 3 with your own Chapter 2 argument and lets you test Model B as designed.
- **Option 2 — Narrow the originality claim instead:** if time doesn't allow adding sections, reframe Section 2's claim to *"testing the outcome chain"* (Hybrid Learning → Effectiveness → Retention → Performance) rather than *"testing the mechanism"* (which needs engagement/trainer competence/org support data you don't have) — a smaller but fully defensible claim that matches what your instrument actually measures.

**Do not submit Chapter 2 and Chapter 3 without picking one of these two options and making the language consistent between them.** This single inconsistency is more likely to draw a panel objection than any of the four gaps in Section 1.
