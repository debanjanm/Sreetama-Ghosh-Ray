## 1. Joanna Evangeline Crissy F (2022) — Implications of Hybrid Workplace on Employee Engagement

- **Full title / author / year:** "Implications of Hybrid Workplace on Employee Engagement," Ms. Joanna Evangeline Crissy F, Register No. 2115782022012, November 2022.
- **Degree program:** M.A. HRM, P.G. Department of Human Resource Management, Madras School of Social Work (Autonomous), Chennai. Guide: Dr. E.T. Evangeline.
- **Organization studied:** No single company — employees across IT, Manufacturing, and Service Sector companies in Chennai (cross-organizational convenience sample).
- **Research problem / objectives:** Whether a hybrid work arrangement (as opposed to fully remote or fully in-office) affects employee engagement, and how. Primary objective: study the implications of the hybrid work model on employee engagement. Secondary objectives: compare engagement between hybrid and non-hybrid models, compare engagement by gender, and compare engagement across primary communication modes.
- **Variables:** Independent — hybrid vs. non-hybrid work model, gender, primary communication mode. Dependent — employee engagement. No mediators/moderators tested.
- **Hypotheses:** Three H0/H1 pairs — (1) hybrid vs. non-hybrid engagement difference, (2) male vs. female engagement difference, (3) hybrid work model vs. work-life balance relationship.
- **Theoretical framework:** No single named base paper; draws on Kahn's (1990) definition of engagement and a wide range of blog/magazine sources (Forbes, HubSpot, McKinsey) rather than peer-reviewed theory. No Kirkpatrick or engagement-model citation comparable to file 03's foundational papers.
- **Methodology:** Exploratory framing but effectively a cross-sectional survey. Non-probability convenience sampling. Sample size = **80 respondents**. Data collected via Google Forms questionnaire. Statistical tools: percentage analysis, independent-samples t-test, Pearson correlation. **Reliability:** used the Meyer & Allen (1997) 24-item organizational commitment instrument for the engagement section; overall Cronbach's alpha reported = **0.569** (explicitly flagged in-text as below the usual 0.60 threshold but "deemed acceptable... when the research is descriptive in nature").
- **Key findings:** Significant difference in engagement between hybrid and non-hybrid workers (independent t-test, p < .001, hybrid mean 4.08 vs. non-hybrid 2.81); no significant gender difference (p = .532); significant positive correlation between hybrid work model and work-life balance (r = .631, p < .001, "moderate" strength).
- **Limitations noted:** Sample size may not be fully representative; no industry-based distinction made; only hybrid vs. non-hybrid (not remote-only vs. office-only) was compared in depth.
- **Questionnaire:** Two-part — demographics/work-mode items (researcher-developed, mostly categorical/multiple-choice) + a borrowed 24-item Meyer & Allen (1997) organizational commitment scale adapted for engagement, 5-point Likert. Full questionnaire reproduced in appendix.
- **Relevance to Sreetama's project:** This is the department's closest **topic-word** match ("hybrid") but is about hybrid *work arrangements* (where people physically work), not hybrid *training delivery* — the constructs, hypotheses, and literature here do not transfer to a hybrid-learning study and should not be cited as topical precedent, only as a structural/statistical-writeup example. Useful takeaways: how the department writes up an independent-samples t-test and a bivariate correlation in Chapter 4, and how a below-threshold Cronbach's alpha (0.569) was handled transparently rather than hidden — a precedent for flagging (not concealing) a weak reliability score if Sreetama's pilot produces one.

## 2. Sakthipriya R (2025) — Linking Employee Recognition and Extra-Role Behaviour: A Study Across Hospitality Industry

- **Full title / author / year:** "Linking Employee Recognition and Extra Role Behaviour: A Study Across Hospitality Industry," Ms. Sakthipriya R, Reg. No. 2415782022030, September 2025.
- **Degree program:** M.A. HRM, MSSW (Autonomous). Guide: Prof. M. Hemakumar.
- **Organization studied:** Hospitality sector broadly (hotels/resorts), not one named company; sample skewed toward "foreign" (86.8%) hospitality organizations operating in India.
- **Research problem / objectives:** Whether employee recognition programs drive extra-role (organizational citizenship) behaviour in hospitality, and whether the effect varies by functional area (kitchen, housekeeping, front desk, etc.) and by gender.
- **Variables:** Independent — employee recognition programs (ER). Dependent — extra-role behaviour / OCB. Moderating/grouping variables tested — functional department, gender. No formal mediator tested.
- **Hypotheses:** Four objective-linked H0/H1 pairs: (1) ER–OCB relationship, (2) ER impact varies by functional area, (3) gender moderates ER–OCB relationship, (4) gender differs OCB directly.
- **Theoretical framework:** No single named base paper; literature review synthesizes ~20 recent (2005–2024) empirical papers on recognition, OCB, and extra-role behaviour across sectors (public sector, healthcare, banking, family business, Gen Z).
- **Methodology:** Quantitative, descriptive, cross-sectional. Convenience sampling. Sample size = **129 respondents** (target stated as 100–150). Data via structured Google Forms questionnaire in three sections. **Pilot study** with 20 respondents conducted before main survey. Statistical tools: Pearson correlation, simple linear regression, one-way ANOVA, independent-samples t-test — the most statistically comprehensive of the 8 documents.
- **Key findings:** Strong positive correlation between recognition and OCB (r = .837, p < .001); regression R² = .701 (recognition explains 70.1% of variance in OCB); one-way ANOVA shows recognition's impact differs significantly by department (F(8,120) = 7.71, p < .001); no significant gender difference in either recognition's effect (t(127) = -0.47, p = .64) or OCB directly (t(127) = 0.45, p = .65).
- **Limitations noted:** Responses depend on self-reported perception (bias risk); sample size (100–150) may not represent the whole industry.
- **Questionnaire / instrument:** Fully validated, published scales — **Lee & Allen (2002) OCB scale** (16 items, split OCB-Individual / OCB-Organization, 8 items each) and **Cannon (2015) Employee Recognition Scale** (8 items), both 5-point Likert. Reliability reported via pilot: Cronbach's alpha reported as 0.979 for both DV and IV in one table and 0.960 described in prose — an internal inconsistency in the document, but both values are comfortably high, evidencing strong internal consistency of validated instruments.
- **Relevance to Sreetama's project:** This is the strongest department-internal model for **fully validated-scale adoption + full inferential battery** (correlation, regression with R², ANOVA, t-test) — a template for how Chapter 4 should read once Sreetama has her HL/TE/KR/JP data, and for citing exact scale sources (author, year, item count) the way [03-literature-matrix.md](03-literature-matrix.md) already recommends for her own HL10-12 and JP items. The topic (recognition → OCB) is conceptually unrelated to hybrid learning, so no content transfers — only method and write-up style.

## 3. Hari Kishore S (2017) — A Study on the Effectiveness of Training on Employees' Performance at TAFE-PSD

- **Full title / author / year:** "A Study on the Effectiveness of Training on Employees' Performance at TAFE-PSD," Hari Kishore S, Reg. No. MHRM/16/15, October 2017.
- **Degree program:** M.A. HRM, MSSW (Autonomous). Guide: Mr. Hemakumar.
- **Organization studied:** TAFE — Power Source Division (battery manufacturing), Maraimalai Nagar, Chennai (subsidiary of Tractors and Farm Equipment Ltd.). Study focused on employees who attended a behavioural/technical training program at TAFE-PSD.
- **Research problem / objectives:** Understand the effectiveness of a behavioural training program on employee performance at TAFE-PSD. Secondary objectives: evaluate training effectiveness by native place, assess whether trainee performance increased post-training, gauge trainee opinion of the program, and determine trainer competency.
- **Variables:** Independent variables (as explicitly labelled in the thesis, somewhat unconventionally) — Preparation, Content, Trainer effectiveness, Facilities, General Satisfaction, Session Enjoyed, Learnings from Program, Overall Effectiveness. Dependent variable labelled as "Age" (an unusual/likely erroneous variable assignment in the original document — flagged here rather than silently corrected). No mediators/moderators tested.
- **Hypotheses:** Single H0/H1 pair — "There is no impact in the performance of employees after the training" vs. "There is an impact."
- **Theoretical framework:** Explicitly grounds its evaluation design in **Kirkpatrick's (1994) Four-Level Training Evaluation Model** (Reaction, Learning, Behaviour, Results) and Hamblin's five-level model (reactions, learning, job behaviour, organization, ultimate value) — this is the only one of the 8 documents to name and structure itself around Kirkpatrick explicitly, making it Sreetama's closest topical and theoretical precedent in this set.
- **Methodology:** Exploratory research design (self-labelled). Non-probability convenience sampling. Universe = ~600 employees; **sample size stated as 80 in the methodology narrative and 70 elsewhere in the same chapter — an internal inconsistency in the original document**, worth noting as a caution for consistency-checking one's own methodology chapter. Data collected via questionnaire (personal interview technique) plus 4 open-ended questions. Statistical tools: percentage analysis, one-sample test, **chi-square test**, confidence interval — notably, **no regression, ANOVA, correlation, or Cronbach's alpha reliability test appears anywhere in this 2017 thesis**, making it the least statistically rigorous of the full theses reviewed and reflecting how department norms have visibly tightened since 2017.
- **Key findings:** Descriptively, 60–70%+ of respondents strongly agreed the training was effective across all sub-dimensions (preparation, content, trainer, facility, satisfaction). However, the **chi-square test on the core hypothesis found no significant relationship (p = .924) between training and performance, and the null hypothesis was accepted** — a notable contradiction between strongly positive descriptive/satisfaction results and a null inferential result, which the thesis does not fully reconcile in its conclusion (Chapter 5 conclusion instead asserts training was effective, glossing over the accepted null in Chapter 4).
- **Limitations noted:** Small sample; short study period meant Kirkpatrick Levels 3–4 (behaviour/results) could not be evaluated, only Levels 1–2 equivalent (reaction/learning); results subject to respondent bias.
- **Questionnaire:** Fully reproduced in appendix — **26 Likert items (5-point: strongly agree=5 to strongly disagree=1) across five researcher-developed sub-dimensions (Preparation, Content, Trainer, Facility, General Satisfaction)**, plus 4 open-ended questions. Entirely researcher-developed; no validated published scale cited, and no reliability (Cronbach's alpha) reported at all.
- **Relevance to Sreetama's project:** This is her single closest topical precedent in the department (training effectiveness, Kirkpatrick-based). Three lessons stand out: (a) it validates a Kirkpatrick-anchored, researcher-developed instrument as departmentally acceptable even without a published scale — consistent with what [03-literature-matrix.md](03-literature-matrix.md) and [04-literature-summary.md](04-literature-summary.md) already concluded about her TE items; (b) it is a cautionary example of a mismatch between glowing descriptive statistics and a null inferential test that the discussion chapter did not honestly address — Sreetama's Chapter 5 should reconcile such tensions explicitly, not paper over them; (c) it shows a department thesis from 2017 with materially thinner statistical apparatus (no regression/correlation/reliability) than the 2024–2025 theses (files 2, 4, 7), indicating rising methodological expectations over time that her own plan should meet or exceed, not match to the 2017 baseline.

## 4. Ravina Narayanan (2021) — Digital Workplace Management in a Hybrid Working Environment Across Service Sector

- **Full title / author / year:** "Digital Workplace Management in a Hybrid Working Environment Across Service Sector," Ms. Ravina Narayanan, Reg. No. 2015782022035, November 2021.
- **Degree program:** M.A. HRM, MSSW (Autonomous). Guide: Dr. Prabakaran P.
- **Organization studied:** No single company — service-sector employees in India generally (COVID-era hybrid-work study).
- **Research problem / objectives:** How hybrid working environments affect digital workplace management, specifically infrastructure, communication, data management, and team management. Primary objective: study digital workplace management in a hybrid environment across the service sector.
- **Variables:** Independent — infrastructure (in a hybrid setting), age, years of experience. Dependent — communication, data management, team management (three separate DVs, each regressed on infrastructure). No mediators/moderators tested.
- **Hypotheses:** Three H0/H1 pairs: (1) infrastructure's effect on communication/data management/team management, (2) age-group differences on those three DVs, (3) experience-group differences on those three DVs.
- **Theoretical framework:** No single named foundational paper; literature review is a curated set of ~18 practitioner articles/blogs (Forbes, HBR, McKinsey-adjacent) on hybrid work management, plus a few academic citations (Maznevski & Chudoba 2000 on virtual teams; Saatci et al. 2019 on hybrid meetings).
- **Methodology:** Analytical research design (self-labelled). Non-probability convenience sampling. Sample size = **100 respondents**. Data via Google Forms questionnaire. Statistical tools: percentage analysis, **linear regression** (three separate models, one per DV), **one-way ANOVA** (for age and experience group comparisons). **Reliability:** overall Cronbach's alpha = **0.930** across 28 items, reported in a clean one-line reliability table.
- **Key findings:** Infrastructure significantly predicts all three DVs — communication (R²=.193, p<.001), data management (R²=.356, p<.001), team management (R²=.305, p<.001). No significant age-group or experience-group differences on any of the three DVs (all ANOVA p > .05, including one borderline p = .050 for experience × team management, treated as accept-H0).
- **Limitations noted:** Pandemic-era access constraints limited physical data collection; voluntary participation meant not all organizational levels were represented; 100-respondent cap; one-month time frame.
- **Questionnaire:** Used the **standardized "Digital Trust in Workplace" questionnaire by Dave E. Marcial and Markus A. Launer** — 28 items (of 30 total, first 2 being demographics), 5-point Likert, explicitly cited as a validated/tested instrument (test-retest reliability study referenced in bibliography) rather than researcher-developed.
- **Relevance to Sreetama's project:** Like file 1 (Joanna), this is about hybrid *work arrangements* (infrastructure/communication/data/team management under WFH-office mixes), not hybrid *training delivery* — despite the superficially similar "hybrid" keyword, the constructs, hypotheses, and cited literature do not conceptually transfer to hybrid learning and training effectiveness, and citing this paper as topical support would be a category error. What does transfer: (a) the department precedent of running **three parallel simple-regression models**, one per dependent variable, cleanly tabulated — a viable alternative write-up style to a single multi-mediator SEM if Sreetama's Model B (Hybrid Learning → Engagement → Training Effectiveness, with Trainer Competence and Organizational Support) proves too complex for SPSS-only regression; (b) confirmation that adopting one clean, single validated instrument (28 items, α = 0.930) rather than assembling multiple partial scales is an accepted, well-executed departmental approach.

## 5. Cross-Functional Collaboration and Innovation at Bonfiglioli, Chennai (proposal stage)

- **Full title:** "The Impact of Cross-Functional Collaboration and Innovation at Bonfiglioli, Chennai." Author name not given in the extracted document (proposal-stage note, not a submitted thesis).
- **Degree program:** MSSW HRM (proposal format matches the department's other project files).
- **Organization studied:** Bonfiglioli, Chennai (industrial gearmotor/drive systems manufacturer).
- **Research problem / objectives:** Whether cross-functional collaboration (among engineering, R&D, production, quality, supply chain, sales & marketing, maintenance, management) has a significant positive impact on innovation. Primary objective: determine whether collaboration significantly impacts innovation; secondary objectives assess collaboration level, innovation level, and their relationship.
- **Variables:** Independent — cross-functional collaboration. Dependent — innovation (performance). No mediator/moderator formally built into the model, though the literature review repeatedly flags innovation climate, trust, and organizational context as candidate mechanisms.
- **Hypotheses:** Single H0/H1 pair, 5% significance level: "no significant impact" vs. "significant positive impact" of collaboration on innovation.
- **Theoretical framework / base paper:** **Jassawalla & Sashittal (2006), "Collaboration in Cross-Functional Product Innovation Teams,"** explicitly named as base paper. Literature review is unusually extensive for a proposal — 20 cited empirical papers (1999–2025) spanning cross-functional team research from Jassawalla & Sashittal through recent (2025) Chennai auto-component-sector studies.
- **Methodology:** Not yet specified (proposal stage — no research design, sample size, or data collection method given).
- **Key findings / limitations:** Not applicable (pre-data-collection proposal).
- **Questionnaire:** Not yet developed; literature review flags likely construct dimensions (communication, trust, shared goals, leadership support, knowledge exchange) as candidate questionnaire content, but no items are drafted.
- **Relevance to Sreetama's project:** Topically unrelated (industrial cross-functional collaboration vs. hybrid learning), but structurally useful as a model of **how to write a tight one-page proposal**: named base paper with an explicit justification paragraph, single primary hypothesis stated with significance level up front, and a literature review organized as short one-paragraph-per-paper annotations rather than full prose synthesis — a format Sreetama could reuse for interim submissions before her full Chapter 2 draft.

## 6. Dressed to Perform: Enclothed Cognition, Self-Esteem, Psychological Safety, and Work Engagement Across Sectors (proposal stage)

- **Full title:** "Dressed to Perform: Enclothed Cognition, Self-Esteem, Psychological Safety, and Work Engagement Across Sectors." Author not named in extracted document (proposal stage).
- **Degree program:** MSSW HRM (proposal format).
- **Organization studied:** No single company — professionals and interns across sectors, predominantly IT (via the author's consultancy internship), with education-sector expansion planned.
- **Research problem / objectives:** Whether work attire (formality/flexibility of dress code) influences state self-esteem, psychological safety, and work engagement. Explicitly flagged as testing a **novel link (attire → psychological safety) not previously studied directly** — the study's core originality claim.
- **Variables:** Independent — work attire. Three parallel dependent variables — state self-esteem (DV1), psychological safety (DV2), work engagement (DV3). No formal mediator, though the literature review discusses self-esteem as a mediator in adjacent studies (Saeed et al. 2022).
- **Hypotheses:** Three H-numbered (not H0/H1 paired) hypotheses, one per DV: H1 (attire → self-esteem, positive), H2 (attire → psychological safety, non-directional — explicitly noted as the paper's core novel/untested claim), H3 (attire → work engagement, positive).
- **Theoretical framework / base paper:** **Kim, Holtz & Vogel (2023), "Wearing Your Worth at Work,"** Academy of Management Journal — named as base paper, with a transparent note that the full text is paywalled and the author has emailed the corresponding author directly for a copy while using a secondary reference in the interim. Also cites Adam & Galinsky (2012) "Enclothed Cognition" as the originating theory.
- **Methodology:** Population — professionals/interns across sectors (IT-heavy currently). Purposive + snowball sampling. Target sample size **150–200 respondents** (stated as a target, not yet achieved — proposal stage).
- **Key findings / limitations:** Not applicable (pre-data-collection).
- **Questionnaire:** Multi-instrument design fully specified even at proposal stage — IV (Work Attire): **7 researcher-constructed items**, 5-point Likert (all 7 items given verbatim); DV1 (State Self-Esteem): **Heatherton & Polivy (1991) SSES, 20 items**; DV2 (Psychological Safety): **Edmondson (1999) Team Psychological Safety Scale, 7 items**; DV3 (Work Engagement): **Schaufeli & Bakker (2006) UWES-9, 9 items, 7-point frequency scale** (note the scale-format mismatch — 5-point for IV/DV1/DV2 but 7-point frequency for DV3, an internal inconsistency worth flagging if adapted).
- **Relevance to Sreetama's project:** Topically unrelated to hybrid learning, but methodologically instructive on two points: (a) it shows a department-acceptable model of **combining one researcher-developed IV scale with three separate validated DV scales**, each cited with author/year/item-count/scale-type in a clean table — precisely the citation granularity [03-literature-matrix.md](03-literature-matrix.md) recommends for Sreetama's own HL/TE/KR/JP sourcing; (b) its transparent handling of a paywalled base paper (email the author, cite a secondary source in the interim, disclose this openly) is a reusable practice if any of her own cited papers (e.g., the gated 2024 *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* Indian-banking Kirkpatrick paper flagged in file 03) remain inaccessible by submission time.

## 7. Palak Pandey (2024) — A Study on the Perception of Women Employees Towards Leadership Roles Within the Organisational Hierarchy

- **Full title / author / year:** "A Study on the Perception of Women Employees Towards Leadership Roles Within the Organisational Hierarchy," Ms. Palak Pandey, Reg. No. 2315782022026, October 2024.
- **Degree program:** M.A. HRM, MSSW (Autonomous). Guide: Dr. P. Mohana.
- **Organization studied:** No single company — corporate organizations across sectors in Chennai (manufacturing, construction, finance emphasized), ~1,200-employee population estimate.
- **Research problem / objectives:** How male and female employees perceive women in leadership roles, what barriers women face advancing to leadership, and what impact women leaders have on team performance, morale, conflict resolution, and creativity.
- **Variables:** Independent/grouping — gender, organizational culture, work-life balance, years of experience. Dependent — perception of women's leadership representation; leadership opportunity; barriers/challenges; impact ratings (team performance, morale, conflict resolution, creativity). No formal mediator tested, though correlational relationships are examined between culture/work-life balance and leadership-related perceptions.
- **Hypotheses:** Four H0/Hα-style hypotheses: (1) significant difference in perceptions of women's representation, (2) organizational culture significantly impacts women's advancement, (3) work-life balance issues significantly affect leadership opportunities, (4) significant impact of women's leadership on team performance/morale/conflict resolution/creativity.
- **Theoretical framework:** Draws on multiple named theories — **Role Congruity Theory** (Eagly & Karau), **Glass Ceiling Theory**, **Queen Bee Syndrome**, **Tokenism Theory** (Kanter), **Stereotype Threat Theory** (Steele & Aronson), and **Social Role Theory** (Eagly) — the most theory-dense literature review of the 8 documents, citing ~20 empirical studies (1977–2022).
- **Methodology:** Descriptive research design, quantitative approach. Non-probability convenience sampling. Population ≈1,200; sample size = **126 respondents**. **Pilot study with 30 participants** conducted before the main survey (largest pilot of any file reviewed). Statistical tools: percentage/frequency analysis, **Spearman's rank correlation**, **one-way ANOVA**. Data collected via a fully self-constructed 23-item questionnaire (no validated instrument used). **Reliability:** overall Cronbach's alpha reported as **0.839** in prose, but the accompanying reliability table reports overall alpha = 0.754 across 18 items with four named sub-scale alphas (Perception of Leadership Roles = 0.732 [7 items], Leadership Opportunity = 0.699 [3 items], Barriers and Challenges = 0.758 [5 items], Work-Life Balance = 0.825 [3 items]) — **an internal inconsistency between the prose value and the table value**, worth noting as a proofreading lesson.
- **Key findings:** Significant difference in perception of male vs. female leaders (ANOVA-style test, p = .042); strong negative correlation between unfavorable cultural norms and perceived leadership opportunity (Spearman's ρ = -.67, p = .02); strong positive correlation between organizational work-life-balance support and women's leadership representation (ρ = .732, p = .015); but **no significant impact of women's leadership on team performance, morale, conflict resolution, or creativity** (all ANOVA p > .05) — a genuine null result reported honestly alongside the significant correlational findings.
- **Limitations noted:** Geographic limitation (India only); sample size/diversity constraints; subjectivity of self-reported perception; cross-sectional (no longitudinal data).
- **Questionnaire:** 23 items, entirely researcher-developed/self-constructed (explicitly stated "originated from an original research study," not adapted from a published scale), mixing Likert-type, semantic-differential (1–5/1–7 anchored), multiple-choice, and one open-ended comment item. Four named sub-scales as shown in the reliability table above.
- **Relevance to Sreetama's project:** Topically unrelated to hybrid learning, but this is the most recent (2024) full thesis in the set and shows the **current department norm most clearly**: sample size ~126 with a formal 30-person pilot, mixed correlation + ANOVA inferential battery, and a fully researcher-developed instrument justified purely by theory (no borrowed scale) — directly comparable to how Sreetama's own KR1-9 section may need to be defended per [04-literature-summary.md](04-literature-summary.md)'s note that no published KR scale was found. It is also a caution: the alpha-value inconsistency (0.839 vs. 0.754) shows how easily a reliability write-up can become internally contradictory — Sreetama should cross-check her own SPSS output against her prose narrative before submission.

## 8. Valli — Influence of Talent Development Practices on the Intention of High-Potential Employees to Stay at Hindustan Unilever Limited, Chennai (proposal stage)

- **Full title:** "Influence of Talent Development Practices on the Intention of High-Potential Employees to Stay at Hindustan Unilever Limited, Chennai." Author referred to as "Valli" per the file naming; not separately named inside the extracted text (proposal stage).
- **Degree program:** MSSW HRM (proposal format).
- **Organization studied:** Hindustan Unilever Limited (HUL), Chennai — named single-company target, focused specifically on **high-potential employees**.
- **Research problem / objectives:** Whether talent development practices (development planning, training, learning & development programmes, mentoring/coaching, e-learning, job rotation) influence high-potential employees' intention to stay. Primary objective tests this relationship directly; secondary objectives assess each variable's level and their relationship.
- **Variables:** Independent — Talent Development Practices (multi-dimensional: Learning & Training, Mentoring & Coaching, Career Advancement, Strengths/Skills Development). Dependent — Intention to Stay. No mediator built into this study's own model, though its base paper (Chami-Malaeb & Garavan) tested affective commitment as a mediator — explicitly noted as a variable this study deliberately excludes to keep the model direct/parsimonious.
- **Hypotheses:** Single H0/H1 pair: "no significant influence" vs. "significant positive influence" of talent development practices on intention to stay.
- **Theoretical framework / base paper:** **Chami-Malaeb & Garavan (2013), "Talent and Leadership Development Practices as Drivers of Intention to Stay in Lebanese Organisations: The Mediating Role of Affective Commitment"** — explicitly named as base paper, with a clear paragraph justifying the choice (high-potential-employee focus, provides a reusable 7-item TDP index) and explicitly stating how the present study narrows the base paper's scope (excludes leadership development and affective commitment as separate variables). Six empirical papers reviewed in total (2009–2022), each ending with an explicit "how this differs from the present study" sentence — a research-gap-by-comparison technique used consistently across all six review entries.
- **Methodology:** Not yet specified in the extracted document (proposal stage — no sample size, design, or sampling technique given).
- **Key findings / limitations:** Not applicable (pre-data-collection).
- **Questionnaire:** Already fully drafted at proposal stage — **9-item Talent Development Practices scale** (4 dimensions: Learning & Training [4 items], Mentoring & Coaching [1 item], Career Advancement [2 items], Strengths/Skills Development [2 items]) and **6-item Intention to Stay scale** (including one reverse-coded item) — each item individually source-attributed to a specific paper (Chami-Malaeb & Garavan 2013; Kyndt, Dochy, Michielsen & Moeyaert; Trevisan, Veloso & Silva 2014; Škerháková et al. 2022; Govaerts, Kyndt, Dochy & Baert; John & Kavatekar) in a two-column item-to-source table.
- **Relevance to Sreetama's project:** Topically unrelated (talent development/retention vs. hybrid learning), but this is **the single best structural template in the set for item-level scale justification**: every questionnaire item is traced to a specific named source paper in a table, exactly matching the granularity [04-literature-summary.md](04-literature-summary.md) already uses for Sreetama's own HL1-15/TE1-9 decision log. It also models good practice for explicitly stating, per reviewed paper, "how the present study narrows/differs from this" — a discipline Sreetama's own Chapter 2 could adopt paper-by-paper rather than only in a summary gap paragraph.

**Sanity-check for Sreetama's own plan:** a five-chapter structure and convenience sampling are universal across every full thesis in this set — her plan is consistent with department norms on both counts. **Sample size of ~80–130** is the realistic department range (not the hundreds sometimes assumed from published journal papers); Sreetama's own target sample size (check against [PLAN.md](../../../PLAN.md) and file 01/05) should be benchmarked against this range rather than against the larger samples seen in the external published literature in file 03. A **pilot study (20–30 respondents)** appears in the two most recent, most rigorous theses (Sakthipriya 2025, Palak 2024) — this is worth adopting given it strengthens the reliability write-up and is now visibly becoming a department norm, not just a nice-to-have. **Reliability reporting is inconsistent even in finished, guide-approved theses** (three of five full theses have either a flagged-low alpha, an internally contradictory alpha, or no alpha at all) — this is a real, demonstrated bar, but Sreetama should aim higher: report one clean, internally consistent Cronbach's alpha value (matching in prose and table) for each of her four constructs (HL, TE, KR, JP), since this is achievable and would exceed rather than merely meet precedent.

### 9.2 Kirkpatrick / training-effectiveness instrument comparison

Only **one** of the eight documents — **Hari Kishore S (2017), TAFE-PSD** — is a training-effectiveness study, and it is the only one to explicitly structure itself around the **Kirkpatrick Four-Level Model**. It is directly comparable to Sreetama's TE construct: both use a Kirkpatrick-anchored, entirely researcher-developed (not published/validated) instrument. Two comparison points worth carrying into her methodology chapter:

1. **Structural comparison:** Hari Kishore's 26-item instrument groups items into 5 sub-dimensions (Preparation, Content, Trainer, Facility, General Satisfaction) that map loosely onto Kirkpatrick Levels 1–2 (reaction/learning) only — it explicitly could not reach Levels 3–4 (behaviour/results) due to the short one-month study window, a limitation it names outright. Sreetama's own TE1-9/KR1-9/JP1-9 split (per [03-literature-matrix.md](03-literature-matrix.md) §3, already mapped to Kirkpatrick Levels 2/3/4 by the current model) is structurally more ambitious than this department precedent — it reaches further up the Kirkpatrick ladder (through job performance/Level 4-adjacent territory) than any prior department thesis in this set attempted, which is worth stating explicitly to a supervisor or panel as a point of methodological ambition, not just novelty.
2. **Statistical rigor comparison:** Hari Kishore's 2017 chi-square test produced a **null result that contradicted its own strongly positive descriptive statistics**, and the conclusion chapter did not fully reconcile this tension. If Sreetama's own hypothesis testing (once she has data) produces a similarly mixed picture — strong descriptive agreement but a non-significant formal test — this precedent shows that being explicit and honest about the discrepancy (rather than the discussion drifting toward the descriptive story alone) is the more defensible path, especially since her instrument and statistical toolkit (correlation/regression per [03-literature-matrix.md](03-literature-matrix.md)'s recommended Model B) are considerably more rigorous than this 2017 baseline.

No other document in this set (files 1, 4 — hybrid *work*, not hybrid *learning*; files 2, 7 — recognition/OCB and gender-leadership perception; files 5, 6, 8 — proposals on unrelated topics) offers a Kirkpatrick-style or training-effectiveness-style instrument for direct comparison. This confirms what [03-literature-matrix.md](03-literature-matrix.md) already found from the external literature search: **Sreetama's dissertation is the first attempt in this specific department cohort to combine hybrid *learning* (not hybrid *work*) with a full Kirkpatrick-chain outcome model (engagement → effectiveness → retention → performance)** — a genuine, citable point of originality within the department's own recent output, not only relative to the broader published literature.

### 9.3 Files 1 and 4 — the "hybrid" naming trap

Both Joanna (2022) and Ravina (2021) use "hybrid" in their titles and are the two documents in this set most likely to be mistakenly cited as precedent by title-matching alone. Both studies are about **hybrid work arrangements** (where/how employees physically work — office vs. remote vs. mixed) — a completely different construct from Sreetama's **hybrid learning/training delivery** (blended digital + classroom instruction). None of their variables (work-mode preference, infrastructure, communication, team management, work-life balance under WFH) map onto Sreetama's HL/TE/KR/JP model, and citing either as topical support in her Chapter 2 would conflate two unrelated literatures. Their only transferable value is structural/statistical (see sections above) — this distinction should be stated explicitly in Sreetama's own literature review if she encounters either paper again during her search, to preempt a supervisor's or panel's objection that she has confused "hybrid" as a keyword with "hybrid" as a construct.
