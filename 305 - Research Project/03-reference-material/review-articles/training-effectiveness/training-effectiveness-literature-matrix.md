# Training Effectiveness Literature Matrix

| Source | Setting and evidence | Questionnaire or measurement status | Use in the current study |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kirkpatrick (1994) | Four evaluation levels: reaction, learning, behaviour, and results. | Framework, not a single questionnaire. | Provides the general evaluation logic. The present study focuses on self-reported learning and individual work application. |
| Holton (1996) | Explains that evaluation levels alone do not explain why training produces later outcomes. | Framework, not a respondent scale. | Supports measuring learning and work application rather than satisfaction alone. |
| Aziz (2015) | Malaysian workplace scale-development study. | Validated 10-item General Training Effectiveness Scale with learning, individual performance, and organizational performance components. | Main measurement foundation. The study adapts learning and individual-performance logic and does not ask employees to judge organization-wide reputation or productivity. |
| Baldwin and Ford (1988) | Foundational training-transfer model. | Framework, not a questionnaire. | Supports job relevance, opportunities to use learning, and application to work tasks. |
| Blume et al. (2010) | Meta-analysis of training transfer. | Review, not a questionnaire. | Supports transfer and work application as meaningful training outcomes. |
| Uddin et al. (2026) | Corporate blended-learning study. | Model-based study; not used as a complete item bank. | Supports the broader workplace relevance of blended learning and work-related outcomes. |
| Bahl, Kiran and Sharma (2024) | Open-access Indian banking study; 402 managerial and non-managerial employees from public, private, and foreign-sector banks; Kirkpatrick’s Reaction → Learning → Behaviour → Results framework. | Structured questionnaire within a Kirkpatrick evaluation model. It is not a Hybrid Learning or Trainer Competence/Learner Engagement item bank for the present study. | Third base paper. Anchors Training Effectiveness in an Indian banking context and supports the banking-sector research gap: it does not examine hybrid learning, Union Vidya, trainer competence, or learner engagement. |

## Training Effectiveness Dimensions Used for Item Design

1. **Learning and clarity:** understanding, recall of important content, and confidence in using the taught skill.
2. **Work relevance:** relevance of content to the employee's daily banking work and work problems.
3. **Transfer and application:** use of knowledge or skills in actual work tasks.
4. **Individual work improvement:** perceived improvement in work quality, accuracy, efficiency, and ability to solve relevant work problems.

The questionnaire measures a single Training Effectiveness score. These dimensions ensure adequate content coverage and do not create separate dependent variables.
