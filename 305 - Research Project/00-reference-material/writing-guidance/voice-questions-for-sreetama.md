# Voice & Tone Questionnaire — for Sreetama

**Consent / scope.** Only Sreetama should answer this. The resulting style profile is for her own writing only (this thesis, and any of her own future drafts) — not for imitating anyone else or for any use beyond her own work.

**Purpose.** Every thesis chapter so far has gone through heavy AI drafting/editing, so none of it is a reliable sample of your own natural writing voice. These questions exist to collect that sample — answers here become the basis for a "write like Sreetama" style profile used to keep the thesis (and other writing) sounding like you instead of like an AI.

**How to answer — read this first:**
- Type your own first-draft answer. No AI, no grammar-checker, no polishing pass. Typos and rough sentences are fine and useful — they show real patterns.
- Answer as fully as feels natural — don't cap yourself at a couple of sentences. Total across everything, aim for roughly 800–1,200 words; some questions will pull more out of you than others, that's fine.
- For the casual questions (Part 1–3): answer in whatever mix of English/Hinglish/Bengali-English you'd normally use if no one was going to grade it.
- For the paraphrase task and the formal-assignment prompt (Part 5 and Q11): write in the plain, careful English you'd actually want in your thesis — no code-switching there, since those two answers teach the profile what your formal register looks like.
- There are no wrong answers — the goal is to see how *you* naturally explain, argue, and transition between ideas, plus your actual vocabulary and English comfort level.
- **Questions 1–8 and the paraphrase task (Part 5) matter most** — they're the real evidence. Questions 9–10 are useful context but shouldn't outweigh what your actual writing shows.

## Part 1 — Explaining things, different audiences

1. Explain your research topic to a friend who has never heard of "hybrid learning" or HR — like you're texting them, not writing a report.

2. Now explain the same topic to your guide/HOD in a quick hallway conversation — a bit more careful, but still spoken, not written-for-submission.

3. A junior student in your program asks: "how do I pick a good thesis topic?" What do you actually tell them?

## Part 2 — Personal / motivation

4. Why did you pick this specific topic (hybrid learning at Union Bank) instead of something else? What made it interesting to you personally?

5. What was the most annoying or difficult part of doing this research so far (data collection, questionnaire, literature review, anything)? Describe what actually happened.

6. What do you hope someone reading your finished thesis takes away from it?

## Part 3 — Opinions / debate

7. Do you personally think hybrid learning actually works better than fully classroom or fully digital training? Why or why not — your honest take, not the "objective" academic answer.

8. Someone argues that digital-only training is cheaper so companies should just drop classroom training entirely. How would you respond to them?

## Part 4 — Same idea, two formalities (shows how you shift register)

9. Pick one idea from your research (any of the constructs — Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, Training Effectiveness — or the overall point of your study). Write it two ways:
   - **First**, as a WhatsApp message to a friend.
   - **Then**, as an assignment answer — same idea, formal version.
