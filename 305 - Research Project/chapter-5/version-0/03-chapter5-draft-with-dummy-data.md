## 5.1 Summary of Findings (dummy data)

This study tested a six-construct model in which Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence were hypothesized to predict Learner Engagement, which was in turn hypothesized to mediate a sequential chain running through Training Effectiveness, Knowledge Retention, and, finally, Job Performance. Of the ten hypotheses tested quantitatively in Chapter 4 (H8 excluded, since no dummy result exists for it), four were supported and one returned a marginal result; the remainder were not supported at conventional significance levels.

Hybrid Learning significantly predicted Learner Engagement (H1: β=.357, p=.0017), consistent with Saroj, Sahney and Sekar's (2026) own acceptance-to-engagement finding. Trainer Competence, however, did not significantly predict Engagement (H1b: β=.050, p=.568); in this sample, the design and flexibility qualities of the hybrid format itself appeared to matter more to engagement than perceived trainer competence. Learner Engagement did not, in turn, significantly predict Training Effectiveness (H2: β=.196, p=.122) — the first indication that the upstream half of the proposed chain held up only partially in this sample.

The downstream half of the chain was considerably more consistent. Training Effectiveness strongly predicted Knowledge Retention (H3: β=.537, p<.0001), and Knowledge Retention in turn predicted Job Performance (H4: β=.372, p=.0009). The mediation analysis produced the most theoretically interesting result in this study: although the total effect of Training Effectiveness on Job Performance was not significant on its own, the indirect effect through Knowledge Retention was (H5c: Sobel z=2.83, p=.0046), an instance of inconsistent mediation, in which a genuine underlying relationship is masked when only the direct total effect is examined. Studying only whether hybrid learning "works," without testing this specific mechanism, would have missed this effect entirely.

Learner Engagement did not significantly mediate the relationship between Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness (H5a: Sobel z=1.43, p=.152), and the mediation of Engagement's effect through Training Effectiveness onto Knowledge Retention returned a marginal, borderline result (H5b: Sobel z=1.90, p=.058). Delivery-mode group comparisons showed no significant differences between blended, digital-only, and classroom-only respondents on either Knowledge Retention (H6: F=1.10, p=.338) or Job Performance (H7: F=0.03, p=.976).

## 5.2 Contributions and Limitations (dummy data)

**Contributions.** This study addresses the four gaps identified in Chapter 2 (Section 2.4), and its contribution to each stands regardless of the specific results obtained. It contributes to the explanatory gap by testing Learner Engagement as a formal mediator rather than assuming a mechanism from a simple correlation, an approach this dummy run shows is necessary: had this study only tested whether Hybrid Learning "works," without decomposing the chain, the inconsistent-mediation result for H5c would never have surfaced. It contributes to the population gap by sampling working employees rather than students, and to the geographic and sectoral gap by being conducted specifically within Union Bank of India, one of very few studies to examine hybrid learning within Indian public-sector banking at all. Its most decisive contribution is to the chain-depth gap: no study identified in Chapter 2 tests a full sequence from hybrid learning through engagement, effectiveness, retention, and job performance in one model, and the inconsistent-mediation finding for H5c is a concrete demonstration of why testing that full chain matters, not only an abstract originality claim.

**Limitations.** Several limitations should be read alongside these contributions. Knowledge Retention and Job Performance were measured through employees' self-reported perceptions at a single point in time, rather than through objective test scores, supervisor ratings, or repeated measurement, a scope decision discussed in Section 1.3 and not an oversight. The convenience sampling method, discussed in Section 3.5, limits generalizability beyond the sampled branch or branches, consistent with department precedent across the reference theses reviewed for this study. Most Hybrid Learning sub-dimensions were reduced to a single item each to meet the department's 30-item cap, discussed in Section 3.9; dimension-level diagnostic reporting is therefore not statistically supportable, and only whole-construct scores should be treated as reliable. Finally, the Knowledge Retention scale itself, developed specifically for this study in the absence of a suitable published multi-item alternative (Section 3.3), has no external validation history to draw on, so its comparability with any future study using a different retention measure remains untested.

## 5.3 Future Research Directions (dummy data)

Two extensions follow naturally from this study's own scope decisions and results. First, Organizational and Supervisor Support, discussed in Section 2.1 as a construct the literature treats seriously but this study's own model excludes, was set aside specifically because the sample size available for this study was judged more likely to support the two-predictor regression models actually used than the larger, more complex combined model organizational support would require (Section 3.5). A future study with a larger sample, potentially using PLS-SEM rather than the regression-based approach used here, could test organizational support as an additional moderator alongside Trainer Competence, following the reasoning already developed in [chapter-2/version-0/07-model-beta-chain-check.md](../../chapter-2/version-0/scratchpad/07-model-beta-chain-check.md).

Second, the marginal result obtained for H5b (Sobel z=1.90, p=.058, in this dummy run) sits close enough to the conventional significance threshold that it cannot be confidently classified as either a real, small effect or as sampling noise on this sample size alone; a replication with a larger sample would help resolve which explanation is correct, a distinction that matters given Training Effectiveness's central position in this study's chain. Relatedly, since Trainer Competence's own path to Engagement (H1b) was not supported here despite theoretical grounding in Saroj, Sahney and Sekar (2026) — a study not tested in this study's own base paper, discussed in Chapter 2 — a future study might investigate whether a fuller Trainer Competence measure, beyond the three items retained after this study's compression to the department's 30-item cap, would change this result.

## Conclusion (dummy data)

This study set out to move beyond the simple question of whether hybrid learning "works" toward understanding the specific mechanism through which it contributes to training effectiveness, retention, and performance among employees of Union Bank of India. The headline finding, in this dummy run, is that Training Effectiveness's effect on Job Performance operates indirectly through Knowledge Retention rather than directly, a relationship a simpler study testing only the total effect would have missed entirely. For Union Bank of India, the practical takeaway is that hybrid learning should not be treated as a single, undifferentiated intervention to be expanded or scaled back as a whole; it is the specific mechanism through which it translates into workplace outcomes, in this case the retention pathway, that the bank's Learning and Development function should attend to directly, once real data replaces the illustrative numbers used throughout this chapter.
