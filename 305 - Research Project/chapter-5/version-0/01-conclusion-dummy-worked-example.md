## 5.1 Summary of Findings

Write this as a narrative walk through the model, not just a repeat of the hypothesis table. Worked example using the dummy Chapter 4 results:

> This study tested a six-construct model — Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence as antecedents to Learner Engagement, which was hypothesised to drive Training Effectiveness, in turn predicting Knowledge Retention and finally Job Performance. Of the ten hypotheses tested, four were supported (H1, H3, H4, H5c) and one showed a marginal, borderline result (H5b); the remainder were not supported at conventional significance levels.
>
> Hybrid Learning significantly predicted Learner Engagement (H1: β=.357, p=.002), consistent with the base paper's [chapter-2/version-0/08-base-paper.md](../../chapter-2/version-0/08-base-paper.md) acceptance→engagement finding. Trainer Competence, however, did not (H1b: β=.050, p=.568) — in this sample, the design/flexibility qualities of the hybrid format itself mattered more to engagement than perceived trainer skill.
>
> The downstream half of the chain held up more consistently: Training Effectiveness strongly predicted Knowledge Retention (H3: β=.537, p<.0001), and Knowledge Retention in turn predicted Job Performance (H4: β=.372, p=.001). The mediation analysis produced the most theoretically interesting result of the study: although the *total* effect of Training Effectiveness on Job Performance was not significant on its own, the *indirect* path through Knowledge Retention was (H5c: Sobel z=2.83, p=.005) — an instance of inconsistent mediation, where a genuine underlying relationship was masked when only the direct total effect was examined. This finding itself supports the core premise of the dissertation (per [chapter-2/version-0/06-gap.md](../../chapter-2/version-0/06-gap.md)'s "training mode to training mechanism" gap): studying only whether hybrid learning "works" would have missed this effect entirely; studying the mechanism revealed it.
>
> Learner Engagement did not significantly mediate the Hybrid Learning → Training Effectiveness relationship (H5a: Sobel z=1.43, p=.152), and delivery-mode group comparisons (H6, H7) showed no significant differences between blended, digital-only, and classroom-only respondents on Knowledge Retention or Job Performance.

## 5.2 Contributions and Limitations

### Contributions (worked example wording)

> This study contributes to the literature in three ways. First, per the originality argument developed in [chapter-2/version-0/06-gap.md](../../chapter-2/version-0/06-gap.md), it is among the first to test a full explanatory chain — from hybrid-learning design through engagement to training effectiveness, retention, and job performance — in an Indian public-sector banking context specifically, addressing a gap the literature review identified as thin. Second, the inconsistent-mediation finding for Knowledge Retention (Section 5.1) demonstrates empirically why mechanism-level research matters more than simple mode-comparison research, directly answering the guide's original steer ([chapter-1/version-0/03-guide-suggestion.md](../../chapter-1/version-0/03-guide-suggestion.md)) toward explanatory rather than descriptive variables. Third, the study extends the base paper's model ([chapter-2/version-0/08-base-paper.md](../../chapter-2/version-0/08-base-paper.md)) beyond its single "employee agility" endpoint into a full Kirkpatrick-aligned retention/performance chain.

## 5.3 Future Research Directions

> Two extensions follow directly from this study's scope decisions. First, **Model-Beta** — adding Organizational/Supervisor Support as a moderator alongside Trainer Competence — was considered and explicitly set aside during this study's design phase ([chapter-2/version-0/07-model-beta-chain-check.md](../../chapter-2/version-0/07-model-beta-chain-check.md)) due to lack of published precedent for the full combination and statistical power constraints at this sample size. A future study with a larger sample (n>150) and access to PLS-SEM could test this fuller model directly. Second, the marginal result for H5b (Sobel z=1.90, p=.058) suggests the Engagement→Retention mediation pathway may be real but underpowered here; a replication with a larger sample would help resolve whether this is a true small effect or sampling noise.

## Conclusion

> This study set out to move beyond the simple question of whether hybrid learning "works" toward understanding the mechanisms through which it contributes to training effectiveness, retention, and performance among Union Bank of India employees.
