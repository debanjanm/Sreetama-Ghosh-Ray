## 3.1 Objectives of the Study

**Primary objective.** To examine the influence of hybrid learning on training effectiveness among employees of Union Bank of India, through the mechanism of learner engagement.

**Specific objectives.**

1. To examine the impact of blended learning on employee knowledge retention.
2. To evaluate the effect of blended learning on workplace performance.
3. To compare the effectiveness of digital-only, classroom-only, and blended training approaches.
4. To examine learner engagement as the mechanism linking hybrid learning and trainer competence to training effectiveness.
5. To propose strategies for optimizing the balance between digital and in-person training delivery.

## 3.2 Hypothesis of the Study

The study tests a serial multiple-mediation model in which Learner Engagement, Training Effectiveness, and Knowledge Retention each mediate the relationship between the constructs on either side of them, with Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence positioned as parallel antecedents to Learner Engagement.

**Path hypotheses**

| # | Hypothesis |
|---|---|
| H1 | Hybrid Learning has a significant positive effect on Learner Engagement. |
| H1b | Trainer Competence has a significant positive effect on Learner Engagement. |
| H2 | Learner Engagement has a significant positive effect on Training Effectiveness. |
| H3 | Training Effectiveness has a significant positive effect on Knowledge Retention. |
| H4 | Knowledge Retention has a significant positive effect on Job Performance. |

**Mediation hypotheses**

| # | Hypothesis |
|---|---|
| H5a | Learner Engagement mediates the relationship between Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness. |
| H5b | Training Effectiveness mediates the relationship between Learner Engagement and Knowledge Retention. |
| H5c | Knowledge Retention mediates the relationship between Training Effectiveness and Job Performance. |

**Comparative hypotheses**

| # | Hypothesis |
|---|---|
| H6 | Employees receiving blended training demonstrate higher Knowledge Retention than employees receiving digital-only training. |
| H7 | Employees receiving blended training demonstrate higher Job Performance than employees receiving digital-only or classroom-only training. |
| H8 | The integration of digital and classroom training positively influences Knowledge Retention and Job Performance. |

Trainer Competence is tested as a parallel antecedent alongside Hybrid Learning (H1b) rather than as a further step in the mediation chain, consistent with its treatment in the base paper (Saroj, Sahney & Sekar, 2026).

## 3.3 Definition of Variables

The study's conceptual model comprises six constructs. **Hybrid Learning** and **Trainer Competence** serve as independent variables, each hypothesized to predict **Learner Engagement**, which functions as the first mediator in the model. Learner Engagement is hypothesized to predict **Training Effectiveness**, which in turn predicts **Knowledge Retention**, which finally predicts **Job Performance**, the study's ultimate dependent variable. Training Effectiveness and Knowledge Retention therefore each occupy a dual role, functioning simultaneously as an outcome of the constructs preceding them and a mediator of the relationship between those constructs and the outcomes that follow.

Hybrid Learning is operationalized across five dimensions — flexibility, accessibility, interaction, technology, and integration — reflecting the design-quality framework established by Boelens, De Wever and Voet (2017). Learner Engagement draws on the dedication and absorption components of engagement identified by Fredricks, Blumenfeld and Paris (2004). Job Performance is operationalized primarily at the task-performance level, following the distinction between task and contextual performance established by Williams and Anderson (1991) and operationalized in the Individual Work Performance Questionnaire (Koopmans et al., 2011, 2014). Knowledge Retention, measured as a multi-item self-report perception scale rather than an objective recall test, represents this study's most original contribution to measurement, as no validated multi-item retention scale of this kind was identified in the literature reviewed for this study.

## 3.4 Research Design

This study adopts a descriptive and explanatory quantitative research design, appropriate given its aim of both describing the current level of hybrid learning practice among the sampled employees and testing the hypothesized explanatory relationships between constructs. Data were collected at a single point in time, making this a cross-sectional rather than longitudinal design; this limits the study's ability to draw strong causal conclusions and is addressed further in the Limitations section of Chapter 5.

## 3.5 Sampling Methods

The population for this study consists of employees of Union Bank of India who have participated in training programmes combining digital and face-to-face delivery. Given the practical constraints of accessing a complete sampling frame within a public-sector banking organization, this study employs non-probability convenience sampling, consistent with the sampling approach adopted across comparable prior studies within this department. The target sample size is approximately 80 respondents, the minimum specified by departmental research norms.

## 3.7 Research Questions

1. How does blended learning affect knowledge retention compared with digital-only and classroom-only training?
2. Does blended learning improve employee performance after training?
3. Does learner engagement mediate the relationship between hybrid learning and training effectiveness, and does trainer competence also predict engagement?
4. How should organizations divide learning activities between digital and classroom formats?
5. What blended learning strategies can improve both retention and workplace application?

## 3.8 Data Analysis Techniques

Data were analyzed in the following sequence. Descriptive statistics (means, standard deviations, and frequency distributions) were computed to characterize the sample and summarize construct-level responses. Internal consistency reliability was assessed using Cronbach's alpha for each of the six constructs. Bivariate relationships between constructs were examined using Pearson correlation. The path hypotheses (H1, H1b, H2, H3, H4) were tested using a series of four multiple regression models corresponding to each stage of the hypothesized chain. The mediation hypotheses (H5a, H5b, H5c) were tested using Hayes' PROCESS macro (Model 6, serial multiple mediation), with the Baron and Kenny (1986) causal-steps approach and Sobel test applied as a supplementary check. The comparative hypotheses (H6, H7) were tested using one-way ANOVA and independent-samples t-tests comparing respondents grouped by training delivery mode (digital-only, classroom-only, blended).

## 3.9 Pilot Study, Reliability and Validity

A pilot study of approximately 20-30 respondents was conducted prior to full data collection, consistent with departmental precedent, to assess the reliability of the instrument and identify any items requiring revision. Reliability was assessed using Cronbach's alpha, with a threshold of .70 applied as the criterion for acceptable internal consistency. Content validity was established through expert review of the instrument by the research supervisor; face validity was assessed during the pilot study. Given the compressed nature of several constructs within the final 30-item instrument (see Appendix), dimension-level reliability could not be assessed for sub-dimensions represented by a single item; reliability was therefore assessed at the construct level only.
