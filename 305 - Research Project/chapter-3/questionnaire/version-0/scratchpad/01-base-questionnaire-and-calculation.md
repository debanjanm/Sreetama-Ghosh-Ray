# 3. Now, how do you calculate the results?

This is much easier than it sounds.

You are basically going to turn people's answers into **scores**.

Suppose one employee gives these answers for Hybrid Learning:

| Item | Answer |
| ---- | -----: |
| HL1  |      4 |
| HL2  |      5 |
| HL3  |      4 |
| HL4  |      3 |
| HL5  |      4 |
| ...  |    ... |

You calculate an average.

### Hybrid Learning score

**Average of HL1 to HL15**

For example:

> HL score = 4.07

That means this employee has a relatively positive view of hybrid learning.

Do the same for the other variables.

---

# 4. You can also calculate dimension scores

This is useful because you don't just want to know:

> “Is hybrid learning good?”

You want to know **what part of hybrid learning is good or weak**.

For example:

### Flexibility

HL1 + HL2 + HL3 ÷ 3

### Accessibility

HL4 + HL5 + HL6 ÷ 3

### Interaction

HL7 + HL8 + HL9 ÷ 3

### Technology

HL10 + HL11 + HL12 ÷ 3

### Integration

HL13 + HL14 + HL15 ÷ 3

Then perhaps you get:

| Dimension     | Mean |
| ------------- | ---: |
| Flexibility   | 4.10 |
| Accessibility | 3.90 |
| Interaction   | 3.45 |
| Technology    | 3.20 |
| Integration   | 4.25 |

Now your result is much more useful.

You can say:

> Employees view the integration of digital and classroom training positively, but technology and interaction are weaker areas.

That immediately gives you a **strategy**.

---

# 5. What does “mean” actually tell you?

With a 1–5 scale:

| Mean      | Simple interpretation |
| --------- | --------------------- |
| 1.00–1.80 | Very low              |
| 1.81–2.60 | Low                   |
| 2.61–3.40 | Moderate              |
| 3.41–4.20 | High                  |
| 4.21–5.00 | Very high             |

These cut-offs are a **simple interpretation guide**, not a universal statistical rule. Your university or supervisor may prefer another approach.

For example:

> Technology = 3.20

You might describe this as:

> “Employees reported a moderate level of satisfaction with the technology used for hybrid training.”

# 6. What statistical tests should you use?

Don't use 20 different tests just because they exist.

For a master's project, a sensible basic analysis could be:

## Test 1: Descriptive statistics

Calculate:

* Mean
* Standard deviation
* Minimum
* Maximum
* Frequency/percentage for demographics

This tells you **what your respondents are saying**.

---

## Test 2: Reliability — Cronbach's Alpha

This answers:

> **“Are the questions within a variable working together consistently?”**

For example, you have 15 questions for Hybrid Learning.

Cronbach's alpha tells you whether these items are reasonably consistent as a scale.

You might get:

> Cronbach's alpha = 0.88

That would generally indicate good internal consistency.

Your actual acceptance criterion should follow the methodology guidance used by your university/supervisor and the literature supporting your instrument.

---

## Test 3: Correlation

This answers:

> **“Are two variables related?”**

For example:

**Hybrid Learning ↔ Training Effectiveness**

**Training Effectiveness ↔ Knowledge Retention**

**Knowledge Retention ↔ Job Performance**

You might get something like:

| Relationship                                 | Correlation |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------: |
| Hybrid Learning ↔ Training Effectiveness     |        0.62 |
| Training Effectiveness ↔ Knowledge Retention |        0.58 |
| Knowledge Retention ↔ Job Performance        |        0.55 |

A positive correlation means that higher scores on one variable tend to go with higher scores on the other.

**Important:** Correlation does not by itself prove that one variable causes another.

---

# 7. Test 4: Regression

This is where your study becomes more interesting.

Regression asks:

> **“Does one variable help predict another variable?”**

For example:

### Model 1

**Training Effectiveness = Hybrid Learning**

This examines whether hybrid learning predicts training effectiveness.

### Model 2

**Knowledge Retention = Hybrid Learning + Training Effectiveness**

This examines whether those variables predict retention.

### Model 3

**Job Performance = Hybrid Learning + Training Effectiveness + Knowledge Retention**

This examines which variables are associated with performance when considered together.

Your statistical software can give you:

* **Beta (β)** = direction/strength of predictor
* **p-value** = whether the relationship is statistically significant
* **R²** = how much of the variation in the outcome is explained by the predictors

For example, purely as an illustration:

> R² = 0.36

You could say:

> “The model explains 36% of the variation in job performance.”

Don't put these example numbers in your dissertation. They are only showing you what the analysis means.

# 8. What about age, experience, etc.?

You can use demographic variables for comparison.

For example:

> Do employees with different years of experience view hybrid learning differently?

You could compare groups using:

* **t-test** → when comparing two groups
* **ANOVA** → when comparing three or more groups

Example:

> Employees with less than 5 years' experience
> versus
> Employees with 5–10 years' experience
> versus
> Employees with 11–15 years' experience
> etc.

But don't automatically run every possible test.

Your research questions should decide what comparisons are useful.

# 9. Your analysis can therefore be very simple

Think of your analysis in this order:

### Step 1

**Who answered?**

Demographic frequencies and percentages.

↓

### Step 2

**What do they think?**

Means and standard deviations.

↓

### Step 3

**Are my questions reliable?**

Cronbach's alpha.

↓

### Step 4

**Are the variables related?**

Correlation.

↓

### Step 5

**Which variables predict the outcome?**

Regression.

↓

### Step 6

**Do different employee groups respond differently?**

t-test / ANOVA, where appropriate.

That's enough to build a solid master's-level analysis, assuming it fits your final research design and university requirements.

# 10. Now the important part: how do results become strategies?

This is where your questionnaire becomes useful to the bank.

Don't just write:

> “The mean score for technology was 3.20.”

Ask:

> **“Why is this low, and what should the bank do?”**

For example:

| Finding                   | Possible problem                               | Strategy                                              |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Technology = low          | Employees face technical problems              | Improve platform, connectivity and technical support  |
| Interaction = low         | Online training feels isolated                 | Add live discussions, Q&A and trainer interaction     |
| Flexibility = high        | Employees value online access                  | Keep self-paced digital modules                       |
| Integration = high        | Employees like digital + classroom combination | Keep the blended structure                            |
| Knowledge retention = low | Employees forget training after completion     | Add refresher modules, quizzes and follow-up sessions |
| Application = low         | Employees struggle to use learning at work     | Add job-based examples and post-training practice     |
| Job performance = high    | Training appears useful                        | Continue and expand effective modules                 |

This is how you move from:

**Data → Finding → Problem → Strategy**

# 11. Your strategies could eventually cover 5 areas

### 1. Better digital platform

For technology problems:

> Improve ease of use, access, technical support and connectivity.

### 2. Better trainer interaction

For low interaction:

> Include live Q&A, discussion sessions, case studies and practical activities.

### 3. Better integration

Instead of having online and classroom training as two separate things:

> Digital module → classroom discussion → practical activity → follow-up digital refresher.

### 4. Better retention

For low knowledge retention:

> Introduce short revision modules, quizzes, reminders and refresher training after the main programme.

### 5. Better transfer to the job

For low performance/application:

> Use banking-related cases, simulations, practice exercises and manager follow-up after training.
