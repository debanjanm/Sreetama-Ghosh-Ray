# Decision Record — Version 3

## Purpose and Status

This note records the main academic decisions behind Version 3 of the study, **Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness at Union Bank of India**. It is an internal project and guide-review document, not a thesis chapter. The current Version 3 model is:

```text
Hybrid Learning + Trainer Competence
→ Learner Engagement
→ Training Effectiveness
```

The earlier [Version 1](../01-current-thesis/) and [Version 2](../01-current-thesis-v2/) drafts are retained as history. They are not to be overwritten or treated as the current study design.

## 1. The Initial Broad Model

The early study was sometimes described in discussion as a “five-variable” learning-to-performance pathway. That wording was a shorthand for the five proposed relationships. The written Version 1 model in fact measured **six constructs**:

1. Hybrid Learning
2. Trainer Competence
3. Learner Engagement
4. Training Effectiveness
5. Knowledge Retention
6. Job Performance

Its logic was that Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence could relate to Learner Engagement; engagement could relate to Training Effectiveness; Training Effectiveness could relate to Knowledge Retention; and Knowledge Retention could relate to Job Performance.

This model was useful for exploring the full path from training delivery to later work outcomes. However, it was not suitable as the final design for a cross-sectional self-report study with an expected sample of about 80 eligible employees. It required too many constructs and a long sequence of relationships. It would also have required stronger evidence about time order to make credible claims about retention, performance, or an indirect pathway. A short questionnaire could not give every construct enough depth while remaining practical for respondents.

The decision was therefore to preserve Version 1 as research history rather than use it as the final study.

## 2. The Earlier Two-Variable Decision

The guide initially advised a simpler model. Version 2 was therefore developed around two broad variables:

```text
Hybrid Learning → Training Effectiveness
```

This was a sensible and defensible design. It required one direct relationship, a short 30-question instrument, and correlation and simple regression analysis. It also kept the study close to the broad topic of hybrid learning and training effectiveness.

Version 2 is retained because it is a complete and valid earlier draft. Its limitation was not that the model was wrong. The limitation was that Trainer Competence and Learner Engagement were absorbed into the employee’s overall Hybrid Learning experience. The study could therefore show whether the two broad variables were associated, but it could not examine whether trainer support and employee engagement helped explain the learning process.

## 3. Why Version 3 Uses Four Constructs

The guide later allowed a more considered model: up to two or three complementary base papers could be used when their roles were clearly justified, and a base paper did not need to provide a complete questionnaire or measurement scale.

Version 3 keeps the study focused while restoring the two factors that matter most for an HRM and workplace-learning study:

- **Trainer Competence** is measured separately because trainers connect digital and face-to-face activities, explain content, encourage participation, and give feedback.
- **Learner Engagement** is measured separately because attention, effort, participation, and clarification-seeking describe how employees take part in training.

The four-construct model is more informative than the two-variable model, but far more manageable than the original six-construct model. It supports practical recommendations about programme design, trainer facilitation, and employee participation. With an expected sample of about 80 employees, it can be analysed with standard descriptive statistics, reliability testing, correlation, a two-predictor regression for Learner Engagement, and a simple regression for Training Effectiveness.

The arrows show relationships to be examined in the selected sample. They do not establish causation or a formal mediation effect. Version 3 does not test Knowledge Retention, Job Performance, organisation-level results, supervisor support, or a separate evaluation of Union Vidya as additional variables.

## 4. Base-Paper Selection Decision

At first, the project aimed to select one base paper that would meet all of the following requirements: a corporate or banking population, hybrid or blended learning, training effectiveness, and a suitable published questionnaire or measurement scale. The literature review did not identify one suitable paper that met all of these requirements together.

Forcing a single paper in that situation would have created a weak match between the paper, the Union Bank setting, and the proposed questionnaire. The later guide direction allowed the study to use complementary papers with transparent roles instead of treating one partially relevant paper as the source of every decision.

Version 3 therefore uses three base papers. Their roles are deliberately different:

| Base paper | Reason for selection and role in this study |
| --- | --- |
| **Sosnova et al. (2025), _Enhancing the effectiveness of learning through hybrid education methods_** | This was suggested by the guide. It provides the conceptual starting point that hybrid learning can be examined in relation to learning effectiveness. Its student population means it is used as a conceptual anchor, not as a corporate or bank questionnaire source. |
| **Kim (2022), _Innovating workplace learning: Training methodology analysis based on content, instructional design, programmed learning, and recommendation framework_** | This open-access study provides direct corporate evidence by examining traditional, fully digital, and hybrid training among automotive sales trainees through a training-effectiveness framework. It establishes why hybrid training should be evaluated in a workplace setting rather than assumed to be effective. |
| **Saroj, Sahney and Sekar (2025), _Antecedents and consequences of learners’ engagement: Examining moderating role of supervisor support in workplace blended learning_** | This employee-survey study provides the workplace mechanism for keeping Trainer Competence and Learner Engagement as separate constructs. It supports examining how instructor competence and blended-learning experience relate to learner engagement. It is not used as a direct Training Effectiveness measure or a respondent-ready questionnaire. |

The three papers are complementary. They do not reproduce the Version 3 model word for word, and the study does not claim that they provide a single validated bank-specific instrument. The detailed record is available in [Base-Paper Rationale](chapter-2/base-paper-rationale.md).

## 5. Questionnaire Decision

The questionnaire is a **literature-informed, context-adapted instrument**. It is not copied verbatim from any one base paper and is not described as a ready-made Union Bank scale.

It contains 30 numbered questions:

| Part | Questions | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Eligibility and profile | 1–4 | Confirms a recent hybrid-training experience and describes the selected respondent group. |
| Hybrid Learning | 5–11 | Measures connected digital and face-to-face design, platform access, material availability, and revision access. |
| Trainer Competence | 12–17 | Measures trainer knowledge, clarity, digital facilitation, participation support, and feedback. |
| Learner Engagement | 18–23 | Measures attention, effort, participation, involvement, clarification-seeking, and interest. |
| Training Effectiveness | 24–30 | Measures employee-reported learning, role relevance, capability, application, task support, and confidence. |

Supporting measurement and theory sources are used for item development, including Han and Ellis for the blended-learning environment; Aziz for workplace Training Effectiveness; Mulaudzi and Kumar and Pande for workplace blended-learning conditions; and Kirkpatrick, Holton, Baldwin and Ford, and Blume et al. for training evaluation and transfer. The full item basis is recorded in the [Questionnaire Evidence Map](chapter-2/questionnaire-evidence-map.md).

Union Vidya is included only as an example of an authorised digital platform that may have been used in the respondent’s selected programme. The questionnaire does not assume that all Bank programmes use identical systems or that all employees have the same access.

### Decision on Union Vidya

Union Bank’s 2023–24 and 2024–25 annual reports identify Union Vidya as the Bank’s LMS and document digital resources, e-learning, virtual training, staggered programmes, role-based training, and the Training Management System. This information is used to establish the local learning context. It does not create a fifth construct or turn the study into an evaluation of Union Vidya’s technology quality. The four constructs, three hypotheses, and 30-question instrument therefore remain unchanged. The source record is available in [Union Vidya LMS Context](../03-reference-material/review-articles/hybrid-learning/union-vidya-lms-context.md).

## 6. Final Method and Scope Decisions

Version 3 is a quantitative, descriptive, correlational, cross-sectional survey of eligible Union Bank of India employees. Each respondent answers about one programme attended during the previous 12 months that combined digital and face-to-face learning.

The final study tests three relationship hypotheses:

1. Hybrid Learning is positively related to Learner Engagement.
2. Trainer Competence is positively related to Learner Engagement.
3. Learner Engagement is positively related to Training Effectiveness.

The analysis will use descriptive statistics, Cronbach’s alpha for the four constructs, Pearson correlation, a two-predictor regression for Learner Engagement, and a simple regression for Training Effectiveness. A pilot and guide review will be used to improve clarity and assess the fit of the questionnaire before main data collection.

The study will report associations among the selected employees. It will not claim causal effects, formal mediation, retention over time, job-performance improvement, customer outcomes, or Bank-wide organisational results. The current operational details are stated in [Chapter 3: Research Methodology](chapter-3/chapter-3-research-methodology.md).
