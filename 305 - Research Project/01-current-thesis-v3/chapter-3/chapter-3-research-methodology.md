# Chapter 3 — Research Methodology

## 3.1 Objectives of the Study

The study examines how eligible employees of Union Bank of India experience hybrid training and how that experience is related to trainer competence, learner engagement, and training effectiveness.

The primary objective is:

**To examine the relationships among Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, and Training Effectiveness among employees of Union Bank of India.**

The specific objectives are:

1. To assess employees’ perceptions of Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, and Training Effectiveness.
2. To examine the relationship of Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence with Learner Engagement.
3. To examine the relationship between Learner Engagement and Training Effectiveness.

These objectives follow the four-construct framework developed in Chapter 2. They are expressed as relationships because the study uses a single cross-sectional employee survey and does not establish causal effects.

## 3.2 Hypotheses of the Study

The study tests three relationship hypotheses at the 5 per cent level of significance.

| No. | Null hypothesis (H₀) | Alternative hypothesis (H₁) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | There is no significant relationship between Hybrid Learning and Learner Engagement among employees of Union Bank of India. | There is a significant positive relationship between Hybrid Learning and Learner Engagement among employees of Union Bank of India. |
| H2 | There is no significant relationship between Trainer Competence and Learner Engagement among employees of Union Bank of India. | There is a significant positive relationship between Trainer Competence and Learner Engagement among employees of Union Bank of India. |
| H3 | There is no significant relationship between Learner Engagement and Training Effectiveness among employees of Union Bank of India. | There is a significant positive relationship between Learner Engagement and Training Effectiveness among employees of Union Bank of India. |

H1 and H2 examine whether the connected design of hybrid learning and the quality of trainer support are associated with employee engagement. H3 examines whether active engagement is associated with employees’ perception that the training was understandable, relevant, useful, and applicable to their work.

The hypotheses do not test a formal mediation effect. The study does not state that Hybrid Learning or Trainer Competence causes Training Effectiveness through Learner Engagement. Such a claim would require a more demanding design, a larger sample, and stronger evidence about time order than is available in a cross-sectional self-report survey.

## 3.3 Definition of Variables

The study uses four constructs.

**Hybrid Learning** is the planned combination of digital and face-to-face learning within one programme. It is measured through connected design, access to the digital platform, availability of learning material, and the ability to revisit digital learning. Questions 5–11 measure this construct.

**Trainer Competence** is the trainer’s ability to support learning through subject knowledge, clear explanation, connected facilitation across learning modes, effective use of relevant digital tools, encouragement of participation, and feedback. Questions 12–17 measure this construct.

**Learner Engagement** is the employee’s active involvement in the selected training programme. It includes attention, effort, participation, involvement, clarification-seeking, and interest in completing the programme. Questions 18–23 measure this construct.

**Training Effectiveness** is the employee’s perception that the programme produced useful learning for the current role. It includes understanding, work relevance, capability, application, support for related work tasks, and confidence. Questions 24–30 measure this construct.

The questionnaire has 30 numbered questions. Questions 1–4 confirm eligibility and describe the respondent group. Questions 5–30 are five-point agreement items. The distribution is:

| Construct | Questions | Number of items |
| --- | ---: | ---:|
| Hybrid Learning | 5–11 | 7 |
| Trainer Competence | 12–17 | 6 |
| Learner Engagement | 18–23 | 6 |
| Training Effectiveness | 24–30 | 7 |
| **Total Likert-scale items** | **5–30** | **26** |

The study does not measure knowledge retention, job performance, organisation-level results, employee agility, supervisor support, metacognitive skills, or technical performance of Union Vidya as separate variables.

## 3.4 Research Design

The study follows a quantitative, descriptive, and correlational research design. It is quantitative because it uses structured response options and numerical analysis. It is descriptive because it reports employees’ views of each construct. It is correlational because it examines the direction and strength of the relationships proposed in the hypotheses.

The study is cross-sectional. Each respondent completes the questionnaire once and answers with reference to one hybrid-training programme attended during the previous 12 months. The unit of analysis is the individual employee.

The model is:

    Hybrid Learning ─┐
                     ├──→ Learner Engagement ───→ Training Effectiveness
    Trainer Competence ┘

The arrows show the relationships tested through regression analysis. The model is not a claim that the survey proves a causal process. Unmeasured conditions, including workload, supervisor support, opportunity to apply learning, digital confidence, and the nature of the programme, may also influence employee engagement and training effectiveness.

## 3.5 Population, Sample and Sampling Method

The population consists of employees connected with Union Bank of India Regional Office Chennai South who have attended training that included both digital or online learning and face-to-face learning.

The study uses non-probability purposive sampling. An employee is eligible only when the employee has attended a hybrid-training programme during the previous 12 months. This condition is necessary because employees who have experienced only one delivery mode cannot comment meaningfully on the connection between digital and face-to-face activity.

The proposed final sample is approximately 80 eligible respondents. The final report will state the actual number of questionnaires distributed, returned, excluded, and retained for analysis. The findings will describe the selected respondents and will not be treated as statistically representative of every employee of Union Bank of India or the wider banking sector.

The questionnaire collects only limited profile information: age group, length of service, and job level. These details are used to describe the sample. They are not included as additional predictors or tested as group differences in the present model.

## 3.6 Data Collection Procedure

Data collection is planned after academic approval and appropriate organisational permission. Eligible employees receive a short explanation of the academic purpose of the study, the voluntary nature of participation, and the confidential handling of responses. The questionnaire may be shared through an approved online form, a printed form, or both, depending on practical arrangements at the study site.

Respondents are asked to select one recent hybrid-training programme and answer every scale item with that programme in mind. The questionnaire introduces Union Vidya only as an example of the digital platform used in a programme. It does not assume that every programme used Union Vidya or that every employee had access to identical digital features.

The questionnaire does not request names, employee numbers, customer details, account information, internal passwords, or any other confidential Bank information. Employees may decline to participate or stop before submitting their form without any consequence for their employment or training access. Results are reported in aggregate form.

Question 1 screens for eligibility. Employees who answer “No” do not complete the remaining scale items. Before analysis, responses are checked for eligibility and sufficient completion of the items needed to calculate construct scores. The final dissertation records the number of incomplete responses excluded and the reason for exclusion.

## 3.7 Research Questions

1. What are employees’ perceptions of Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, and Training Effectiveness?
2. Are Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence associated with Learner Engagement?
3. Is Learner Engagement associated with Training Effectiveness?

## 3.8 Data Analysis Techniques

For Questions 5–30, response categories are coded from 1 for Strongly Disagree to 5 for Strongly Agree. A construct score is calculated as the mean of the completed items assigned to that construct. Higher scores indicate a more positive employee perception of the relevant construct.

The analysis has four stages.

| Stage | Technique | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Respondent and item description | Frequency, percentage, mean, and standard deviation | To describe the selected respondent group and the pattern of responses. |
| Reliability | Cronbach’s alpha for each construct | To assess the internal consistency of the Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, and Training Effectiveness item sets. |
| Association | Pearson correlation | To examine the direction and strength of the relationships among the four construct scores. |
| Hypothesis testing | Regression analysis | To test the three relationship hypotheses. |

The first regression model is:

    Learner Engagement = b₀ + b₁(Hybrid Learning) + b₂(Trainer Competence) + e

This multiple-regression model examines whether Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence are each associated with Learner Engagement while the other predictor is held statistically constant.

The second regression model is:

    Training Effectiveness = b₀ + b₁(Learner Engagement) + e

This simple-regression model examines the relationship between Learner Engagement and Training Effectiveness.

For each hypothesis, the null hypothesis is rejected when the relevant p-value is below .05. The report will present the direction of the coefficient, the relevant test statistic, p-value, and explained variance where appropriate. A statistically significant relationship will be described as an association in the selected sample. It will not be described as proof that one construct caused another.

The analysis does not compare online-only and classroom-only programmes, use structural-equation modelling, calculate a formal indirect effect, or evaluate Bank-level performance. These analyses would require a different design, additional data, or a larger sample.

## 3.9 Pilot Study, Reliability and Validity

Before the main survey, the questionnaire is piloted with approximately 20 eligible employees who are not included in the final sample. The pilot checks whether the instructions are clear, whether employees understand the term “hybrid training,” whether the LMS wording is familiar, whether each statement relates to one selected programme, and whether the questionnaire can be completed in a practical time.

Cronbach’s alpha is calculated separately for the four item sets. A value of .70 or above is treated as an initial indication of acceptable internal consistency. The pilot results are considered together with employee comments and item-total statistics. An item may be revised if it is unclear, repetitive, unrelated to employees’ actual training experience, or inconsistent with its intended construct. All wording changes are finalised before main data collection and documented in the dissertation.

Content validity is supported by the questionnaire evidence map. Hybrid Learning items are based on blended-learning integration and workplace access literature. Trainer Competence and Learner Engagement items are based on workplace blended-learning and engagement literature. Training Effectiveness items are based on corporate training evaluation and transfer literature. The academic guide and suitable reviewers should examine whether the items adequately represent the four stated constructs.

Face validity is checked during the pilot by asking employees whether the statements are understandable and relevant to their selected programme. The pilot does not create a claim that the instrument is a fully validated bank-specific scale. It provides a practical check that the literature-informed wording is suitable for the selected workplace setting.

## 3.10 Ethical Considerations and Limitations

Participation is voluntary, and the survey avoids personal identifiers and confidential Bank data. Respondents are informed that the survey is for academic use and that only combined results will be reported. The researcher seeks the required academic and organisational approval before distribution.

The study has several limits. It uses a non-probability sample, so its findings cannot be generalised statistically to all Bank employees. The design is cross-sectional and relies on employee perceptions, so it cannot prove causation. Common response conditions may influence the reported relationships because the same respondent reports the programme experience, trainer competence, engagement, and training effectiveness. The study also does not use objective test scores, system logs, supervisor ratings, customer outcomes, or organisation-level performance records.

These limitations do not remove the value of the study. They define its contribution: a focused account of how a selected group of eligible employees experience hybrid training, trainer support, engagement, and training effectiveness in the Union Bank context.
