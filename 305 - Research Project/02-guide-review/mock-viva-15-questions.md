# Mock Viva: 15 Most Likely Questions

Use this as a practice conversation. Keep each answer calm and direct. Pause after the first answer; add the second paragraph only if the guide asks for more.

## 1. Guide: Explain your research in simple words.

**Student:** I studied how Union Bank employees experience training that combines digital learning and face-to-face learning. I checked whether connected learning and trainer support are related to employee engagement, and whether engaged employees feel that training is useful in their work.

**Guide follow-up:** Does your study prove that hybrid learning causes effectiveness?

**Student:** No. It shows positive associations in the selected cross-sectional self-report sample. It does not prove cause and effect.

## 2. Guide: Why did you select this topic?

**Student:** Bank employees need to learn changing procedures, compliance requirements, products, and digital systems while working. I selected the topic because hybrid training may help make learning accessible, but the digital and trainer-led parts need to work together.

**Guide follow-up:** Why is it relevant to HRM?

**Student:** It concerns employee capability, training design, trainer facilitation, and practical learning application, which are core HR and learning-and-development concerns.

## 3. Guide: What is the gap in your research?

**Student:** Much blended-learning research is from higher education. Banking studies discuss training effectiveness, but I found limited evidence examining Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, and Training Effectiveness together among public-sector bank employees in the Union Vidya context.

**Guide follow-up:** Are you saying no research exists?

**Student:** No. Related research exists. The gap is the limited and fragmented evidence at this specific intersection.

## 4. Guide: Why did you choose four variables?

**Student:** A two-variable model of Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness was manageable, but it did not explain the employee-learning process. Trainer Competence and Learner Engagement help explain how programme design and human support are associated with perceived usefulness of training.

**Guide follow-up:** Why not add knowledge retention and job performance?

**Student:** Those need delayed tests, supervisor ratings, or work-performance records. They would exceed what a short cross-sectional employee questionnaire can support.

## 5. Guide: Why did you use three base papers?

**Student:** One paper did not cover corporate hybrid training, Indian banking, training effectiveness, and the present four constructs together. I therefore used three complementary papers: Sosnova for conceptual grounding, Kim for corporate hybrid-training evidence, and Bahl for Indian banking training-effectiveness evidence.

**Guide follow-up:** Did you copy their model?

**Student:** No. I developed a focused model from their compatible ideas and supporting literature.

## 6. Guide: Why is Bahl relevant when it does not study hybrid learning?

**Student:** Bahl is relevant because it evaluates Training Effectiveness among 402 Indian bank employees through Kirkpatrick’s framework. It supports the banking relevance of the outcome variable; Kim supports the corporate hybrid-training side.

**Guide follow-up:** Does Bahl validate your whole questionnaire?

**Student:** No. It supports the training-effectiveness context. My questionnaire is literature-informed and adapted, not copied from Bahl.

## 7. Guide: What is Union Vidya’s role in your study?

**Student:** Union Vidya is Union Bank’s LMS and part of the digital-learning context. It is included within Hybrid Learning because I studied employees’ experience of access, material availability, and revision within a selected programme.

**Guide follow-up:** Why is it not a separate variable?

**Student:** A separate LMS evaluation would need measures of technical quality, support, uptime, satisfaction, and analytics. That was outside this study’s scope.

## 8. Guide: How did you collect the data?

**Student:** I used the same questionnaire in two modes: 70 paper forms and 62 Google Forms submissions. Paper forms were collected through Chennai South Regional Office, associated locations, and branches; the Google Form supported employees who could not complete a physical form during workload periods.

**Guide follow-up:** Why use both methods?

**Student:** It made participation practical while keeping the questionnaire and scoring consistent.

## 9. Guide: What was your sample and why did you use purposive sampling?

**Student:** I received 132 responses and retained 105 eligible responses: 56 paper and 49 online. I used purposive sampling because respondents needed recent experience of a programme with both digital and face-to-face learning.

**Guide follow-up:** Can you generalise this to all Union Bank employees?

**Student:** No. It is a selected Chennai-context sample, so the findings cannot statistically represent the entire Bank workforce.

## 10. Guide: Why did you exclude 27 responses?

**Student:** Sixteen respondents answered No to the eligibility question. Eleven paper forms had unclear or blank eligibility responses. I excluded them because I could not confirm that they had attended a relevant hybrid programme.

**Guide follow-up:** Did you remove similar-looking responses?

**Student:** No. Similar anonymous patterns do not prove duplicate respondents, so I retained them and documented the pattern check.

## 11. Guide: Explain your two imputations.

**Student:** Two otherwise eligible paper forms each had one unclear Likert item. Manual-25 HL6 was replaced by 3.333, and Manual-46 HL4 was replaced by 4.000, using each respondent’s mean on the remaining items in that same construct.

**Guide follow-up:** Why did you not exclude them?

**Student:** They were otherwise complete and had only one unclear item. The pre-stated within-construct mean rule allowed their retention, and both changes were documented.

## 12. Guide: What did Cronbach’s alpha show?

**Student:** The four scales had high internal consistency: Hybrid Learning .961, Trainer Competence .969, Learner Engagement .958, and Training Effectiveness .971.

**Guide follow-up:** Does that mean your scale is fully validated?

**Student:** No. It supports consistency in this sample. Full validation would need further work, including pilot testing and validation in another sample.

## 13. Guide: What were the main hypothesis results?

**Student:** All three were supported. Hybrid Learning and Learner Engagement had r = .715. Trainer Competence and Learner Engagement had r = .726. Learner Engagement and Training Effectiveness had r = .765. All had p below .001.

**Guide follow-up:** Which was strongest?

**Student:** Learner Engagement and Training Effectiveness was the strongest reported relationship, at .765.

## 14. Guide: Why did you use regression, and what did it show?

**Student:** Regression gave supporting model-level evidence. Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence together explained 57.2% of the observed variation in Learner Engagement. Learner Engagement explained 58.5% of the observed variation in Training Effectiveness.

**Guide follow-up:** Did the regression prove a causal chain?

**Student:** No. It is still based on cross-sectional self-report data, so it supports association analysis only.

## 15. Guide: What practical recommendations do you give Union Bank?

**Student:** I recommend making the digital-to-trainer-led learning sequence clear, keeping material easy to find and revisit, helping trainers connect the two modes and give feedback, creating structured opportunities for questions and practice, and using work-based follow-up prompts.

**Guide follow-up:** Are these proven interventions?

**Student:** No. They are practical review points based on the response pattern and literature. They should be piloted and evaluated before broader implementation.
