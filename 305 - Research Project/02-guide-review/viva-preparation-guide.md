# Viva Preparation Guide

# Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness at Union Bank of India

## How to use this guide

Speak in the first person: **“I studied…”, “I collected…”, “My finding was…”**. Do not memorise every sentence. Remember the point, the supporting number, and the boundary of the finding.

If you do not know a detail, say: **“I would like to check the exact table before giving that figure. The main finding is…”** Do not guess.

## Opening answers

### 30-second introduction

> My study examines how employees experience hybrid training at Union Bank of India in the Chennai context. Hybrid training means that digital learning and face-to-face learning are used as connected parts of one programme. I studied whether Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence are associated with Learner Engagement, and whether Learner Engagement is associated with perceived Training Effectiveness. I collected 132 responses through paper questionnaires and Google Forms, retained 105 eligible responses, and found positive, statistically significant relationships for all three hypotheses.

### Two-minute explanation

> Banking employees need to learn new procedures, digital systems, compliance requirements, products, and customer-service practices while continuing their daily work. Union Bank has digital-learning facilities such as Union Vidya and also trainer-led learning arrangements. However, having a platform and having a good employee learning experience are not the same thing.
>
> My research therefore focused on one recent programme attended by each eligible employee that included both digital and face-to-face learning. I studied four constructs. Hybrid Learning means how well the two modes were connected and how accessible the digital material was. Trainer Competence means the trainer’s knowledge, clarity, digital facilitation, participation support, and feedback. Learner Engagement means attention, effort, participation, interest, and clarification-seeking. Training Effectiveness means whether employees felt the learning was understandable, relevant, useful, and applicable to their work.
>
> I used a 30-question questionnaire. After screening, 105 eligible responses were analysed. The findings show that better reported Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence were each positively associated with Learner Engagement. Higher Learner Engagement was positively associated with perceived Training Effectiveness. The study gives Union Bank practical areas to review, such as making the learning sequence clear, keeping Union Vidya material easy to find, supporting trainers, creating space for questions and practice, and connecting training with routine work. The findings describe associations in this selected Chennai sample; they do not prove cause and effect or represent every Union Bank employee.

### Simple model explanation

```text
Hybrid Learning ─┐
                 ├──→ Learner Engagement ───→ Training Effectiveness
Trainer Competence ┘
```

> The model says that when employees experience the digital and face-to-face parts as connected, and when the trainer gives clear guidance and feedback, they may be more engaged in the programme. Employees who are more engaged may also report that the training is more useful and applicable. I tested these as relationships, not as a proven causal chain or formal mediation model.

# Likely Viva Questions and Answers

## A. Topic, problem, gap, and contribution

### 1. What is your study about?

**Say:** My study examines employee experience of hybrid training at Union Bank of India in the Chennai context. It studies the relationships among Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, and perceived Training Effectiveness.

**Remember:** One recent programme with both digital and face-to-face learning.

**If asked further:** I did not compare online-only training with classroom-only training.

### 2. Why did you choose this topic?

**Say:** Banking employees must learn changing procedures, compliance requirements, digital systems, and products while continuing normal work. I wanted to understand whether connected digital and trainer-led learning supports employee engagement and perceived usefulness of training.

**Remember:** Practical HR and learning-and-development relevance.

**If asked further:** The topic is about employee learning experience, not a technical evaluation of software.

### 3. What do you mean by “hybrid learning”?

**Say:** Hybrid learning is a planned programme in which digital learning and face-to-face learning support each other. For example, employees may complete digital preparation, attend a trainer-led discussion or practice session, and revisit digital material later.

**Remember:** Connected modes, not simply two modes existing in the same programme.

**If asked further:** The right balance can differ by topic; my study does not prescribe one fixed ratio.

### 4. What is the research problem?

**Say:** Union Bank has digital and trainer-led learning infrastructure, but that does not show whether employees experience a particular programme as connected, supported, engaging, and useful in their work. Published evidence on all four of these constructs together in a public-sector banking context is limited.

**Remember:** Infrastructure does not automatically show employee-level effectiveness.

**If asked further:** My study fills an employee-experience gap, not a Bank-wide performance gap.

### 5. What research gap does the study fill?

**Say:** The gap is at the intersection of hybrid learning, trainer competence, learner engagement, and training effectiveness among public-sector bank employees. Much blended-learning research is from higher education, while the Indian banking study I used focuses on training effectiveness without studying hybrid learning or Union Vidya.

**Remember:** Workplace gap + banking gap + mechanism gap + measurement gap.

**If asked further:** I do not claim that no related research exists. I claim the evidence is limited and fragmented at this specific intersection.

### 6. How is your study useful to Union Bank?

**Say:** It provides structured employee feedback on programme connection, trainer support, engagement, and perceived training usefulness. It identifies practical areas that the learning-and-development team can review.

**Remember:** It suggests review areas, not guaranteed solutions.

**If asked further:** The recommendations are based on the response pattern and should be tested before any Bank-wide implementation.

### 7. What is original in your research?

**Say:** The originality is the focused four-construct employee-perception study in the Union Bank Chennai context, with Union Vidya treated as the digital-learning context. The study combines corporate hybrid-training literature with Indian banking training-effectiveness literature.

**Remember:** Originality comes from the focused context and combination, not from claiming a new universal theory.

**If asked further:** The questionnaire is literature-informed and adapted; it is not presented as a globally validated new scale.

### 8. Is this a study of training effectiveness or a study of Union Vidya?

**Say:** It is primarily a study of employee experience of hybrid learning and training effectiveness. Union Vidya is the Bank’s LMS and is included as part of the digital-learning context within Hybrid Learning.

**Remember:** Union Vidya is context, not a fifth scored construct.

**If asked further:** A separate LMS study would require questions on system quality, uptime, technical support, analytics, and user satisfaction.

### 9. Why is this relevant to HRM?

**Say:** HRM is concerned with employee development, capability, and the design of learning opportunities. This study examines conditions that may help employees take part in learning and apply relevant knowledge in their current roles.

**Remember:** Training design and trainer support are HR/L&D concerns.

**If asked further:** The study does not measure financial performance or customer outcomes.

### 10. What is the main message of the research?

**Say:** The main message is that hybrid training should be designed as one connected employee-learning experience. Digital resources, trainer-led explanation, participation, practice, and revision access need to work together.

**Remember:** “Connected learning experience.”

**If asked further:** This is a practical interpretation of the associations found in the selected sample.

## B. Union Bank context and base papers

### 11. Why did you choose Union Bank of India?

**Say:** Union Bank is a suitable setting because its public reports describe both digital-learning resources and trainer-led learning arrangements. The Chennai South context also gave me access to eligible employees who had attended relevant programmes.

**Remember:** Suitability came from documented infrastructure and feasible employee access.

**If asked further:** I do not claim that every Union Bank programme is hybrid.

### 12. What is Union Vidya?

**Say:** Union Vidya is identified by Union Bank as its Learning Management System. The Bank’s reports describe it within a wider system of e-learning, virtual learning, role-based learning, mandatory modules, and a Training Management System.

**Remember:** It is the Bank’s LMS/LXP context.

**If asked further:** Respondents could have used Union Vidya or another authorised platform for their selected programme.

### 13. What evidence shows that hybrid learning is relevant at Union Bank?

**Say:** The annual reports describe digital resources, virtual workshops, e-learning, staggered programmes, and trainer-led learning. The Bank also has Union Learning Academies and Staff Training Centres. A 2026 staff circular gives an example of selected trainer-led sessions being live-streamed through Microsoft Teams with recordings available through Union Vidya.

**Remember:** This establishes organisational context, not employee-level results.

**If asked further:** My survey was needed because organisational documents cannot show how individual employees experienced a programme.

### 14. Why did you use three base papers instead of one?

**Say:** No single accessible paper covered hybrid learning, corporate training, Indian banking, trainer competence, learner engagement, training effectiveness, and a suitable instrument together. The guide permitted up to three base papers with a clear justification, so I selected complementary papers rather than forcing one paper to cover claims outside its scope.

**Remember:** Conceptual + corporate + banking roles.

**If asked further:** Supporting literature was also used for questionnaire wording and theoretical interpretation.

### 15. What is the role of Sosnova et al. (2025)?

**Say:** Sosnova et al. is the guide-suggested conceptual anchor. It studies hybrid education methods and learning effectiveness among students. It supports the broad idea that a carefully designed hybrid approach can be examined in relation to learning outcomes.

**Remember:** Student study; conceptual anchor only.

**If asked further:** I do not present it as direct evidence about Union Bank employees.

### 16. What is the role of Kim (2022)?

**Say:** Kim is the main corporate hybrid-training paper. It studies automotive sales training across traditional, fully digital, and hybrid delivery. It shows why training design, instructor involvement, practice, and employee perceptions should be evaluated.

**Remember:** 583 training instances; corporate setting.

**If asked further:** Kim did not prove that hybrid is always better than classroom training, so I do not make that claim.

### 17. What is the role of Bahl, Kiran and Sharma (2024)?

**Say:** Bahl et al. is the Indian banking anchor. It studied 402 managerial and non-managerial bank employees using Kirkpatrick’s training-evaluation framework. It shows that employee-level training effectiveness is a substantive issue in the banking sector.

**Remember:** Indian banking; 402 employees; Kirkpatrick framework.

**If asked further:** It does not study hybrid learning or my complete model. Its role is specifically to support the banking relevance of training effectiveness.

### 18. Why is Bahl relevant if it does not study hybrid learning?

**Say:** My study has two linked areas: hybrid-learning experience and training effectiveness. Bahl is relevant to the second area because it provides open, India-specific banking evidence that training effectiveness can be evaluated among bank employees. Kim provides the corporate hybrid-training side, while Bahl provides the banking training-effectiveness side.

**Remember:** Do not overstate Bahl’s scope.

**If asked further:** The model was built through compatible evidence from several sources, not copied from Bahl.

### 19. Did you copy a model from one base paper?

**Say:** No. The four-construct model was developed from the combined literature. Sosnova supports the hybrid-learning/effectiveness idea, Kim supports corporate training design and instructor involvement, and Bahl supports banking training-effectiveness evaluation.

**Remember:** Complementary literature, transparent adaptation.

**If asked further:** The model is designed for the present research question and sample, not claimed as a replication.

### 20. Why did you not use a bank-specific hybrid-learning paper as the only base paper?

**Say:** I could not identify an accessible paper that simultaneously had a banking population, hybrid learning, training effectiveness, trainer competence, learner engagement, and suitable measurement items. Using three papers with separate justified roles was more academically honest.

**Remember:** Lack of one perfect source was documented.

**If asked further:** The questionnaire evidence map records the supporting basis for the item areas.

## C. Model, objectives, and variables

### 21. What are your four variables?

**Say:** The four variables are Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, and Training Effectiveness.

**Remember:** HL, TC, LE, TE.

**If asked further:** Union Vidya is included inside Hybrid Learning context; it is not a separate variable.

### 22. Why did you choose four variables instead of two?

**Say:** I first considered a two-variable model: Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness. It was manageable, but it did not explain the employee-learning process. Trainer Competence and Learner Engagement were retained because they help show how programme design and trainer support are related to the employee’s active involvement before perceived effectiveness is considered.

**Remember:** Four constructs give a practical HR explanation without making the model too large.

**If asked further:** Six constructs were considered earlier, but that would have been too complex for a cross-sectional self-report study.

### 23. Why did you not use knowledge retention or job performance?

**Say:** Knowledge retention and job performance need different evidence, such as delayed testing, supervisor ratings, work records, or performance measures. Adding them would have widened the questionnaire and encouraged claims that the present cross-sectional self-report data could not support.

**Remember:** Scope matched data and design.

**If asked further:** They are valid topics for a future longitudinal study.

### 24. Why is Trainer Competence separate from Hybrid Learning?

**Say:** A digital platform can be accessible while a trainer may still fail to explain the subject, connect the activities, invite questions, or give feedback. A capable trainer may also face difficulty when material is hard to access. They are related conditions, but they are not the same condition.

**Remember:** Programme experience and human facilitation are distinct.

**If asked further:** Keeping them separate allowed me to test their separate relationships with engagement.

### 25. Why is Learner Engagement in the middle of the model?

**Say:** A connected programme and a competent trainer can create better learning conditions, but employees must still pay attention, make an effort, participate, and seek clarification. Engagement therefore provides a useful link between training conditions and perceived training effectiveness.

**Remember:** Practical intervening construct.

**If asked further:** I did not test formal mediation or claim that engagement caused effectiveness.

### 26. What is your main objective?

**Say:** My main objective is to examine the relationships among Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, and Training Effectiveness among eligible Union Bank respondents.

**Remember:** Relationships, not causation.

**If asked further:** The study also describes how respondents perceived each construct.

### 27. What are your three hypotheses?

**Say:** H1 states that Hybrid Learning has a significant positive relationship with Learner Engagement. H2 states that Trainer Competence has a significant positive relationship with Learner Engagement. H3 states that Learner Engagement has a significant positive relationship with Training Effectiveness.

**Remember:** H1 HL–LE; H2 TC–LE; H3 LE–TE.

**If asked further:** The null hypotheses state that there is no significant relationship.

### 28. Why did you not have a direct Hybrid Learning to Training Effectiveness hypothesis?

**Say:** The focused model prioritised the employee-learning process. The literature and practical logic suggested first examining whether programme design and trainer support were related to engagement, and whether engagement was related to perceived effectiveness.

**Remember:** The direct relationship is available in the correlation matrix but was not a stated hypothesis.

**If asked further:** A future study can test a broader direct-and-indirect model with an appropriate design.

### 29. Are you proving a mediation model?

**Say:** No. I did not calculate an indirect effect or use a formal mediation method. I tested three relationships using correlation and supporting regression analysis.

**Remember:** No PROCESS, SEM, or formal mediation claim.

**If asked further:** A mediation study would need a stronger design and a larger, suitably planned sample.

### 30. What theory supports your study?

**Say:** The study uses three complementary perspectives: blended-learning integration, trainer-supported learner engagement, and training evaluation and transfer. Together, they explain why connected learning design, trainer facilitation, employee participation, and work relevance matter.

**Remember:** Integration, engagement, transfer.

**If asked further:** The study uses these perspectives as a framework, not as proof of cause and effect.

## D. Questionnaire and measurement

### 31. How did you develop the questionnaire?

**Say:** I used a literature-informed, researcher-adapted questionnaire. The wording was based on the selected base papers and supporting measurement literature, then adapted to the Union Bank employee-training context and reviewed by the academic guide.

**Remember:** Literature-informed and guide-reviewed.

**If asked further:** It is not presented as a ready-made or fully validated Union Bank scale.

### 32. How many questions are in the questionnaire?

**Say:** There are 30 numbered questions. Questions 1 to 4 cover eligibility and respondent profile. Questions 5 to 30 are 26 five-point Likert-scale items.

**Remember:** 4 profile/eligibility + 26 scored items.

**If asked further:** The response scale runs from 1, Strongly Disagree, to 5, Strongly Agree.

### 33. How are the 26 items divided?

**Say:** Hybrid Learning has 7 items, Trainer Competence has 6, Learner Engagement has 6, and Training Effectiveness has 7.

**Remember:** 7 + 6 + 6 + 7 = 26.

**If asked further:** Each construct score is the arithmetic mean of its assigned items.

### 34. What sources supported the Hybrid Learning questions?

**Say:** The Hybrid Learning items were informed by blended-learning integration literature and workplace evidence, especially Han and Ellis on blended-learning environment perceptions and Mulaudzi on workplace access, flexibility, materials, and facilitation.

**Remember:** Connected design, access, navigation, availability, revision.

**If asked further:** The wording was changed from student language to employee-training language.

### 35. What sources supported the Trainer Competence and Engagement questions?

**Say:** Kim supports the relevance of instructor involvement, participation, practice, and training design. Mulaudzi supports workplace facilitation and access. Engagement literature supports attention, effort, interest, participation, and clarification-seeking.

**Remember:** Trainer actions and employee participation were measured separately.

**If asked further:** No selected base paper offered the exact full workplace scale for these two constructs; this limitation is stated openly.

### 36. What sources supported Training Effectiveness questions?

**Say:** Kim supports perceived competence, job-skill relevance, and training evaluation. Bahl provides the Indian banking training-effectiveness context through Kirkpatrick’s framework. Aziz supports employee-level training effectiveness without requiring organisation-level outcomes.

**Remember:** Understanding, relevance, capability, application, confidence.

**If asked further:** I did not include Bank productivity or financial results because respondents cannot validly measure them through a short self-report survey.

### 37. Why did you use a five-point Likert scale?

**Say:** A five-point agreement scale is simple for busy employees to understand and complete. It provides enough variation for descriptive statistics, reliability testing, correlation, and regression analysis.

**Remember:** 1 = Strongly Disagree; 5 = Strongly Agree.

**If asked further:** A longer scale was unnecessary for a short paper-and-online workplace questionnaire.

### 38. How did you define eligibility?

**Say:** A respondent had to have attended at least one programme in the previous 12 months that included both digital or online learning and face-to-face learning. Each respondent answered about one such programme.

**Remember:** Q1 = Yes was required for inclusion.

**If asked further:** Employees who attended only digital or only face-to-face training could not answer meaningfully about the connection between modes.

### 39. Why did you mention Union Vidya in the questionnaire?

**Say:** Union Vidya was named as an example of Union Bank’s digital learning platform to make the questions understandable in the Bank context. The wording also allowed another authorised platform because not every selected programme had to use identical systems.

**Remember:** Example platform; no assumption of identical access.

**If asked further:** I did not ask technical system-performance questions because this was not an LMS evaluation.

### 40. Did you conduct a pilot study?

**Say:** No separate pilot study was conducted before main collection. The questionnaire was reviewed by the academic guide, mapped to literature, and its final-sample internal consistency was checked using Cronbach’s alpha.

**Remember:** Be transparent; do not invent a pilot.

**If asked further:** This is a limitation, and future research should include a pilot or formal content-validation panel.

## E. Sample and data collection

### 41. Who was your population?

**Say:** The population was Union Bank employees connected with the Chennai South Regional Office and related branch-level collection points who had attended a recent programme combining digital and face-to-face learning.

**Remember:** Chennai context; eligible employees only.

**If asked further:** The study does not claim to represent the full national Union Bank workforce.

### 42. What sampling method did you use?

**Say:** I used purposive sampling. I approached employees who met the study’s eligibility condition and were available through the Chennai South Regional Office, associated locations, branches, paper forms, and the Google Forms link.

**Remember:** Eligibility-based non-probability sampling.

**If asked further:** This was appropriate because the study required respondents with relevant hybrid-training experience.

### 43. Why did you use purposive sampling rather than random sampling?

**Say:** Random sampling would include employees who may not have attended a relevant hybrid programme. Purposive sampling allowed me to collect responses from employees who could answer the study questions meaningfully.

**Remember:** Relevance of experience was essential.

**If asked further:** The trade-off is limited statistical generalisation, which I state as a limitation.

### 44. How did you collect data?

**Say:** I collected data through 70 printed questionnaires and 62 Google Forms submissions. Paper responses were collected through Chennai South Regional Office, associated points, and branches. The Google Form enabled participation when employees could not complete a physical form during workload periods.

**Remember:** 70 paper + 62 online = 132 received.

**If asked further:** Both modes used the same approved questionnaire.

### 45. Why did you use both paper forms and Google Forms?

**Say:** Using both modes made participation practical for employees with different workload conditions and access situations. The questionnaire content and scoring were the same in both modes.

**Remember:** Wider participation without disrupting branch work.

**If asked further:** Digital records were reconciled against the original Google Forms export; paper records were manually verified.

### 46. How many responses did you receive and retain?

**Say:** I received 132 responses. After eligibility screening, 105 eligible responses were retained: 56 paper responses and 49 online responses.

**Remember:** 132 received; 105 retained.

**If asked further:** The original target was about 80 eligible responses, so the final usable sample exceeded that target.

### 47. Why were 27 responses excluded?

**Say:** Sixteen respondents answered “No” to the hybrid-training eligibility question. Eleven printed forms had a blank or illegible eligibility response. These records were excluded because eligibility could not be confirmed.

**Remember:** 16 No + 11 unclear eligibility = 27.

**If asked further:** I did not remove records simply because their answers looked similar.

### 48. How did you ensure ethical data collection?

**Say:** Participation was voluntary. The questionnaire did not collect names, confidential Bank information, customer information, or other personal identifiers. Responses were used only for academic analysis and reported in combined form.

**Remember:** Voluntary, anonymous, academic use.

**If asked further:** I obtained verbal permission from the HR Manager for academic data collection.

## F. Data cleaning and analysis

### 49. What data cleaning did you do?

**Say:** I retained only Q1 = Yes responses, checked that all retained records had the 26 item fields, checked values were within the permitted 1 to 5 range, reconciled online records against the original workbook, and documented any unclear paper-form responses.

**Remember:** Structural checks, reconciliation, eligibility screening.

**If asked further:** Repeated anonymous response patterns were noted but retained because similarity alone does not prove duplicate respondents.

### 50. What were the two imputations?

**Say:** Two otherwise eligible paper forms had one unclear item each. Manual-25 had HL6 replaced by 3.333, the mean of the respondent’s other six Hybrid Learning items. Manual-46 had HL4 replaced by 4.000 using the same within-construct mean rule.

**Remember:** Manual-25 HL6 = 3.333; Manual-46 HL4 = 4.000.

**If asked further:** No retained record had two or more unclear scored items; those would have been excluded under the stated rule.

### 51. Why did you use mean imputation for those two items?

**Say:** There was only one unclear item in each otherwise complete record. Using the respondent’s mean for the remaining items in the same construct preserved the record while keeping the replacement tied to that respondent’s own pattern. Both cases were documented transparently.

**Remember:** Two isolated cases only.

**If asked further:** This is a practical small-sample cleaning decision and should not be used casually for many missing answers.

### 52. What is Cronbach’s alpha and why did you use it?

**Say:** Cronbach’s alpha checks whether the items assigned to one construct show internal consistency. I used it to check whether the Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, and Training Effectiveness item sets worked coherently in this sample.

**Remember:** Reliability, not proof of validity.

**If asked further:** Alpha does not prove that the instrument is a fully validated Union Bank scale.

### 53. What were your Cronbach’s alpha values?

**Say:** Hybrid Learning was 0.961, Trainer Competence was 0.969, Learner Engagement was 0.958, and Training Effectiveness was 0.971.

**Remember:** All above .95; high internal consistency.

**If asked further:** Very high values can also mean items are closely related and the common self-report format may contribute, so I interpret them with caution.

### 54. Why did you use Pearson correlation?

**Say:** My hypotheses were about whether construct scores had positive relationships. Pearson correlation measures the direction and strength of the relationship between two numeric construct scores, so it was the primary hypothesis test.

**Remember:** Relationship test; p below .05 leads to rejection of the null hypothesis.

**If asked further:** It shows association, not proof that one variable causes another.

### 55. Why did you also use regression?

**Say:** Regression provides supporting model-level evidence. The first model examined Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence together in relation to Learner Engagement. The second model examined Learner Engagement in relation to Training Effectiveness.

**Remember:** Regression supports the correlation findings.

**If asked further:** The regression was not used to claim a causal pathway or formal mediation.

### 56. What is VIF, and what did it show?

**Say:** VIF, or Variance Inflation Factor, checks whether predictors overlap too strongly in a multiple-regression model. The VIF for Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence was 3.005, with tolerance 0.333. This shows overlap that should be kept in mind, but it was below the common VIF screening value of 5.

**Remember:** VIF 3.005; tolerance .333.

**If asked further:** Because the constructs overlap, I did not claim that one predictor was clearly more important than the other.

### 57. Did you use SPSS?

**Say:** The final scored dataset has reproducible SPSS syntax prepared for the planned descriptive, reliability, correlation, and regression analysis. The final figures should be reproduced and retained in SPSS output before final submission. I will describe SPSS as the verification tool only when the corresponding output files are retained.

**Remember:** Do not falsely state that SPSS produced an output if it was not actually run.

**If asked further:** The calculations are fully documented from the frozen 105-record dataset, so they can be reproduced in SPSS.

## G. Findings, strategies, limitations, and future research

### 58. What are your main findings?

**Say:** All three hypotheses were supported. Hybrid Learning and Learner Engagement had a positive correlation of 0.715. Trainer Competence and Learner Engagement had a positive correlation of 0.726. Learner Engagement and Training Effectiveness had the strongest positive correlation, 0.765. All were significant at p below .001.

**Remember:** H1 .715; H2 .726; H3 .765; all p < .001.

**If asked further:** These are relationships in the selected sample, not causal effects.

### 59. What did the regression analysis show?

**Say:** Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence together explained 57.2% of the observed variation in Learner Engagement. Learner Engagement explained 58.5% of the observed variation in Training Effectiveness. Both models were statistically significant.

**Remember:** R² = .572 for engagement; R² = .585 for effectiveness.

**If asked further:** Explained variation in a cross-sectional self-report model does not prove cause and effect.

### 60. What practical strategies does your study suggest?

**Say:** The study suggests five review areas: make the sequence between digital and trainer-led learning visible; keep material easy to locate and revisit; prepare trainers to connect the two modes and provide feedback; create structured opportunities for questions and practice; and use work-based examples or follow-up prompts to connect learning with routine tasks.

**Remember:** Five strategies; recommendations, not tested interventions.

**If asked further:** The lower-scoring areas were digital preparation before face-to-face sessions, platform navigation, connection between modes, feedback, clarification-seeking, and help with work tasks.

## Final reminder

The safest accurate conclusion is:

> Among the 105 eligible respondents, more positive Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence perceptions were associated with higher Learner Engagement, and higher Learner Engagement was associated with higher perceived Training Effectiveness. The study gives Union Bank focused employee feedback for reviewing hybrid-training design, while remaining limited to a selected, cross-sectional, self-report Chennai sample.

# Hard Cross-Questions: Direct Defence Answers

These questions are more likely when the guide tests the boundaries of the study. Answer them calmly and directly. A limitation stated honestly is more credible than an overclaim.

## H1. You use “hybrid learning” and “blended learning.” Are they different?

**Say:** In much of the literature, hybrid learning and blended learning are used for the planned combination of digital and face-to-face learning. I use **Hybrid Learning** consistently as the name of my construct. The important point in my study is that the two modes are connected as parts of one programme.

**Remember:** The terminology varies; the construct definition is clear.

**If asked further:** I did not treat the terms as two separate variables.

## H2. Sosnova’s study is about Ukrainian students. Why is it acceptable in a workplace bank study?

**Say:** Sosnova is not used as direct evidence about bank employees. It is the guide-suggested conceptual anchor because it examines whether a carefully designed hybrid approach can be related to learning effectiveness. I combine that conceptual evidence with Kim’s corporate training evidence and Bahl’s Indian banking training-effectiveness evidence.

**Remember:** Conceptual role only; no claim of direct transfer from students to bank employees.

**If asked further:** The present data were collected from Union Bank employees, so the findings are based on the workplace sample rather than on the student study.

## H3. Why did you use an automotive sales-training paper for a bank study?

**Say:** Kim is relevant because it examines workplace training, not because automotive sales and banking are identical. It supports the corporate-learning principles of programme design, instructor involvement, practice, participation, and evaluation. Bahl supplies the Indian banking training-effectiveness context.

**Remember:** Corporate learning principle from Kim; banking context from Bahl.

**If asked further:** I do not claim that a result from automotive sales automatically applies to banking.

## H4. What proof do you have that your research gap is real?

**Say:** The literature shows four connected gaps. First, much blended-learning research is based on students rather than workplace employees. Second, Bahl studies training effectiveness in Indian banking but does not study Hybrid Learning, Trainer Competence, Learner Engagement, or Union Vidya. Third, the selected base papers do not test my four-construct employee-experience model together. Fourth, available measures could not be transferred unchanged into a short Union Bank employee questionnaire.

**Remember:** Workplace, banking, mechanism, and measurement gaps.

**If asked further:** I describe the gap as limited and fragmented evidence, not as an absence of all related research.

## H5. Why did you use correlation and regression instead of SEM?

**Say:** My stated hypotheses are simple relationships, and the study is cross-sectional. Pearson correlation directly tests those relationships, and two regression models provide supporting evidence. SEM and formal mediation would require a more demanding model, a larger planned sample, and stronger measurement-validation work than this project was designed to provide.

**Remember:** Method matched the hypotheses and design.

**If asked further:** I did not present the diagram as a confirmed causal path model.

## H6. Why did you choose a cross-sectional design?

**Say:** I used a cross-sectional survey because the study aimed to describe employees’ current recalled experience of one recent hybrid-training programme within the available dissertation fieldwork period. It was feasible for branch employees and suited the focused relationship questions.

**Remember:** Feasible snapshot of reported experience.

**If asked further:** It cannot establish what came first or prove cause and effect. A future pre-training and post-training study would be stronger for that purpose.

## H7. Is 105 respondents enough for four constructs and 26 items?

**Say:** The 105 eligible records are adequate for the planned descriptive statistics, reliability checks, Pearson correlations, and two-predictor regression. The study uses four averaged construct scores for the relationship analysis. It does not claim that this sample is sufficient for full scale validation, SEM, or national representation of Union Bank employees.

**Remember:** Adequate for planned analysis; not for every possible analysis.

**If asked further:** The final usable sample also exceeded the original approximate target of 80 eligible respondents.

## H8. Why did you study Chennai South only, rather than several regions?

**Say:** The study was scoped to the Chennai South fieldwork context because that was where I had access to eligible employees through the Regional Office, associated locations, and branches. This allowed direct data collection within the available academic period.

**Remember:** Defined fieldwork scope and access.

**If asked further:** This is why I limit the findings to the selected Chennai-context respondents and do not generalise to all regions.

## H11. Could paper forms and Google Forms have biased the results?

**Say:** It is possible that collection mode influenced participation because employees with different work schedules or digital comfort may have selected different modes. Both modes used the same approved questionnaire and scoring rules, and online records were reconciled with the original export. I did not separately test mode differences, so this remains a limitation.

**Remember:** Same instrument; mode bias was not tested.

**If asked further:** The study reports the two modes transparently: 70 paper submissions and 62 Google Forms submissions.

## H12. How did you verify the paper-form responses?

**Say:** The paper questionnaires were treated as source records and manually transcribed into structured response records. Eligibility and each scored response were checked from the forms. Blank or illegible eligibility responses were excluded, and the two unclear scored items were documented separately rather than hidden.

**Remember:** Paper source record, manual verification, documented exceptions.

**If asked further:** The final data-quality audit records 70 verified manual records and the two imputation decisions.

## H13. How do you know that similar answers were not duplicate responses?

**Say:** I do not know with certainty from anonymous forms alone. Similar answer patterns were checked and recorded, but I retained them because identical or similar responses do not prove that the same person responded twice. Removing them without identifying evidence would create another form of bias.

**Remember:** Similarity is not proof of duplication.

**If asked further:** The questionnaire did not collect names, because anonymity was part of the ethical approach.

## H14. Your alpha values are .958 to .971. Are your questions redundant?

**Say:** The alpha values show very high internal consistency, meaning the items in each construct moved closely together in this sample. That can be useful for reliability, but it can also indicate that some items are closely related. I therefore do not treat alpha alone as proof of perfect measurement or full scale validation.

**Remember:** Strong consistency plus a caution about possible item overlap.

**If asked further:** Future work can use a larger independent sample, factor analysis, and item refinement to test whether some items can be shortened.

## H15. Hybrid Learning and Trainer Competence correlate at .817. Are they really separate constructs?

**Say:** They are strongly related in this sample, which makes practical sense because a connected programme and trainer support can occur together. However, their questionnaire items measure different things: Hybrid Learning measures integration, access, material availability, and revision; Trainer Competence measures knowledge, clarity, digital facilitation, participation support, and feedback.

**Remember:** Correlation HL–TC = .817; item content differs.

**If asked further:** I do not claim perfect discriminant validity. The VIF of 3.005 and tolerance of .333 show overlap that I treated cautiously in the regression interpretation.

## H16. What is common-method bias, and how does it affect your study?

**Say:** Common-method bias means that the same person answered all the variables in the same questionnaire at one point in time. This can make correlations appear stronger because the response style is shared. My findings may therefore reflect both real reported associations and some common-method influence.

**Remember:** Same respondent, same time, same survey.

**If asked further:** A future study can combine employee responses with knowledge tests, supervisor observations, system records, or data collected at different times.

## H17. Why did you use five response options instead of seven?

**Say:** A five-point scale was selected because it is easy for busy employees to understand and complete in both paper and Google Forms. It provides enough response variation for the planned descriptive and relationship analysis without making the questionnaire longer or more difficult.

**Remember:** Clarity and practicality for field respondents.

**If asked further:** A seven-point scale is also valid in other studies; I chose five points for this short workplace instrument.

## H18. Did you use reverse-coded questions? Why not?

**Say:** No. I kept all statements direct and positively worded so that the paper and online questionnaire would be easy to understand across employees and would reduce confusion during quick completion. The absence of reverse-coded items is a design limitation because agreement-style response tendencies may remain.

**Remember:** Clarity was prioritised; acquiescence bias remains possible.

**If asked further:** A future instrument can test carefully worded reverse items after piloting them with the target employee group.

## H19. Why was Hybrid Learning the lowest-scoring construct if it is central to your topic?

**Say:** Hybrid Learning was still positively rated, with a mean of 4.142 on the five-point scale. It ranked lowest only relative to the other three constructs. This is useful because it identifies the programme-design side as an area where employee experience may be improved, especially digital preparation and platform navigation.

**Remember:** HL mean = 4.142; above neutral, lowest relative rank.

**If asked further:** A lower relative score does not mean that hybrid learning failed. It helps identify where review may be useful.
