That is a strong dissertation topic. It gives you three clear areas to connect:

**Hybrid/blended learning → knowledge retention → workplace performance**, with a final focus on **how organizations should design the digital + classroom mix**.

A clean way to frame the research project is:

> **“Hybrid Learning and Training Effectiveness: Making Digital and In-Person Learning Work Together”**

### 1. Core research problem

Organizations increasingly use a combination of digital learning and instructor-led classroom training. However, simply combining the two formats does not automatically improve learning outcomes.

The key question is:

**Does a well-designed blended learning model improve knowledge retention and employee performance compared with relying mainly on digital or classroom training?**

The dissertation can examine whether the effectiveness of hybrid learning depends not just on the **format**, but on **how the two formats are integrated**.

### 2. Possible research objectives

You could structure the study around these objectives:

1. **To examine the impact of blended learning on employee knowledge retention.**
2. **To evaluate the effect of blended learning on workplace performance.**
3. **To compare the effectiveness of digital-only, classroom-only, and blended training approaches.**
4. **To identify factors that influence the success of blended learning**, such as learner engagement, training duration, interaction, technology usability, and trainer support.
5. **To propose strategies for optimizing the balance between digital and in-person training.**

### 3. Key variables

A simple conceptual structure would be:

**Independent Variable**

* Training delivery model

  * Digital learning
  * Classroom learning
  * Blended learning

**Mediating / influencing factors**

* Learner engagement
* Interaction
* Learning flexibility
* Trainer support
* Technology usability
* Practical application opportunities

**Dependent Variables**

* Knowledge retention
* Training effectiveness
* Job performance

You could also examine **learner experience/satisfaction** as an additional outcome.

### 4. Possible research questions

Your dissertation could use questions such as:

**RQ1:** How does blended learning affect knowledge retention compared with digital-only and classroom-only training?

**RQ2:** Does blended learning improve employee performance after training?

**RQ3:** Which factors contribute most to the effectiveness of blended learning?

**RQ4:** How should organizations divide learning activities between digital and classroom formats?

**RQ5:** What blended learning strategies can improve both learning retention and workplace application?

### 5. Possible hypotheses

For a quantitative dissertation, you could test hypotheses such as:

**H1:** Blended learning has a significant positive effect on knowledge retention.

**H2:** Employees receiving blended training demonstrate higher knowledge retention than employees receiving digital-only training.

**H3:** Employees receiving blended training demonstrate higher workplace performance than employees receiving digital-only or classroom-only training.

**H4:** Learner engagement positively influences the relationship between blended learning and training effectiveness.

**H5:** The integration of practical classroom activities with digital learning positively influences knowledge retention and performance.

### 6. The interesting part of the dissertation

I would avoid making the dissertation simply:

> “Is online learning better than classroom learning?”

That question is fairly basic.

A more useful research angle is:

> **“What should be taught digitally, what should be taught in person, and how should the two formats be connected to maximize retention and performance?”**

For example:

| Digital format works well for | Classroom works well for |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Knowledge introduction        | Discussion               |
| Videos                        | Demonstrations           |
| Reading                       | Role plays               |
| Quizzes                       | Simulations              |
| Self-paced learning           | Collaboration            |
| Revision                      | Problem solving          |
| Compliance/basic concepts     | Hands-on practice        |

This gives your dissertation a practical management angle rather than just comparing two training methods.

### 7. Possible conceptual model

You can eventually build the dissertation around:

**Blended Learning Design**

→ **Learner Engagement**

→ **Knowledge Retention**

→ **Workplace Application**

→ **Employee Performance**

With factors such as **trainer support, technology quality, learner motivation, and training relevance** influencing the relationships.

### 8. Suggested dissertation structure

A strong structure would be:

**Chapter 1 – Introduction**
Background, research problem, objectives, research questions, significance and scope.

**Chapter 2 – Literature Review**
Traditional learning, digital learning, classroom learning, blended learning, knowledge retention, training effectiveness, employee performance and relevant learning theories.

**Chapter 3 – Research Methodology**
Research design, sample, data collection, questionnaire/interviews, variables, measurement scales and analysis methods.

**Chapter 4 – Data Analysis and Findings**
Participant profile, descriptive statistics, reliability, hypothesis testing, comparisons between learning formats and key findings.

**Chapter 5 – Discussion**
Interpretation of findings and comparison with previous research.

**Chapter 6 – Recommendations and Conclusion**
Practical strategies for organizations, limitations, future research and conclusion.

### 9. A particularly good practical framework

Your final recommendation could become a **“Right Content, Right Format” hybrid training framework**:

**Learn digitally → Discuss in person → Practice in person → Reinforce digitally → Apply at work → Assess**

That gives you something tangible to propose at the end of the dissertation rather than finishing with only statistical findings.

For this topic, I would also recommend narrowing the population to a specific employee group or organizational context. That will make the research much easier to conduct and defend academically.
