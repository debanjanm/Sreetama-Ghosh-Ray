### 3.1 Summary — What This Means for Your 42-Item Draft

- **Your current 42 items (HL1-15, TE1-9, KR1-9, JP1-9) have no directly-matching *published, validated* source for most items** — they remain, as files 04/05 already acknowledged, researcher-developed and logically derived rather than adapted from an existing instrument. This is not fatal, but your methodology chapter should say so explicitly and cite the closest analogues found above.
- **Best re-anchoring opportunities found:**
  - HL10-HL12 (technology/digital-experience items) → Ye, Kuang & Liu (2022) ICTSE/UBL items.
  - JP1, JP2, JP4, JP9 (accuracy/quality/efficiency/overall performance) → Koopmans et al. IWPQ task-performance subscale, or the shorter Williams & Anderson (1991) 7-item scale.
  - No good published match was found for **Knowledge Retention (KR1-9)** as a standalone construct — most literature treats retention as a single follow-up-test outcome (e.g., Wakekar et al. 2026's 6-month recall %) rather than a multi-item perception scale. Your KR section may end up being the most "original" part of your instrument — flag this explicitly to your supervisor rather than presenting it as adapted.
- **Constructs entirely absent from your draft but present in the literature as strong candidates** (per madam's original seven, file 02): **learner engagement**, **trainer/instructor competence**, **organizational/supervisor support**, and **training transfer**. If your final conceptual model (see below) adds any of these, validated instruments exist (STE-S or UWES-9 for engagement; Open Praxis or the 8-item scale for trainer competence; SPOS/OSBL for org support; LTSI for transfer) — you would not be starting from zero.
