# 09 — Literature Summary: Decision Basis for the 42-Item Draft Instrument

**Purpose of this document.** This file exists specifically to answer the question a supervisor or viva panel will ask: *"On what basis did you decide this?"* — for every major decision made about the questionnaire (files 04/05) and the conceptual model (file 06/08). It is a decision log, not a new literature review. Every claim below traces back to [08-literature-matrix.md](08-literature-matrix.md) (papers/instruments) or [03-atomic-concepts.md](03-atomic-concepts.md) (decision-filter logic), and every citation given here can be checked against the full matrix entry in file 08.

**How to use this in your defense.** If asked "why does item X read the way it does," find the item below, read the "Basis" line, and cite the paper named. If asked "why isn't variable Y in your model," find it in Section 4, and cite the reasoning given.

## 1. Decision Framework Applied

Every inclusion/exclusion/re-anchoring decision below was filtered through the five questions your guide's suggestion ([02-guide-suggestion.md](02-guide-suggestion.md)) and the atomic-concepts breakdown ([03-atomic-concepts.md](03-atomic-concepts.md), section 11) require:

1. Is there a strong theoretical reason this variable/item matters?
2. Have previous researchers studied it (and is that study verifiable, not just cited secondhand)?
3. Does it explain the relationship between hybrid learning and training outcomes (mediator logic), or does it just co-occur with them?
4. Can it be reliably measured using an established questionnaire?
5. Does it make sense in the Union Bank of India employee context specifically?

Where an item or variable fails (4) — no established questionnaire found — that is stated explicitly rather than glossed over, because a panel member checking your reference list will find the same gap.

This is the citation to use if a panel asks "why five sub-dimensions and not three."

## 4. Item-Level Decision Log: Knowledge Retention (KR1–KR9)

**This is the section with the weakest published-instrument backing, and you should say so directly to your supervisor rather than let a panel discover it.**

| Item | Current wording | Decision | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| KR1–KR9 (all) | Recall main points; remember key concepts weeks/months later; remember procedures; explain in own words; recall when facing similar situation; remember changes to procedures; retain for reasonable period; recall and use when needed; revision materials help retention | **Keep as researcher-developed. Explicitly flag as the most original section of the instrument.** | No paper found in this search publishes a multi-item *self-report perception* scale for knowledge retention comparable to KR1-9. The literature instead measures retention as a **single objective outcome** — typically a delayed test score or recall percentage — not as a multi-dimensional attitude scale. The clearest example: Wakekar et al. (2026), file 08 row 6, measured "6-month retention" as a test-based percentage (78.2% blended vs 62.4% traditional), not as 9 Likert items. |

**Why this matters and what to do with it:** Two defensible paths exist, and this is a decision for your supervisor, not one the literature makes for you:

1. **Keep KR1-9 as a self-report perception scale** (current approach) and justify it explicitly as measuring *perceived* retention — a distinct construct from *objective* retention (test score). This is defensible but must be stated as a conscious choice, with the Wakekar et al. (2026) objective-measurement approach cited as the road not taken, and the reason given (a bank employee survey cannot easily administer a delayed knowledge test across the sample without disrupting operations).
2. **Add a small objective retention check** (e.g., 3-5 factual recall questions about actual training content, scored correct/incorrect) alongside the perception items, following Wakekar et al.'s design logic. This strengthens validity but adds design complexity.

State in your methodology chapter: *"No validated multi-item perceived-retention scale was located in the reviewed literature; the retention items in this study are therefore researcher-developed, informed by the general retention-measurement literature (Wakekar et al., 2026) but adapted to a self-report format due to practical constraints on objective delayed testing in a live banking workforce."* This single sentence pre-empts the most likely panel question on this section.

## 6. Basis for Excluding Variables From Your Guide's Original List of Seven

Your guide ([02-guide-suggestion.md](02-guide-suggestion.md)) listed seven candidate variables: learner engagement, knowledge retention, learning motivation, learner satisfaction, digital literacy/technology readiness, trainer competence, organizational support. Your current 4-variable draft (files 04/05) includes only knowledge retention explicitly (as a full section) and touches technology readiness only lightly (HL10-12). Here is the basis for each exclusion decision, so you can defend — or revisit — each one:

| Candidate variable | Current status | Basis for decision |
|---|---|---|
| **Learning motivation** | Excluded from current draft. | No dedicated instrument search this session found a workplace blended-learning-specific motivation scale distinct from general engagement measures; Ryan & Deci (2000) SDT is the theoretical base but SDT's own instruments (e.g., Intrinsic Motivation Inventory) were not located/verified in this search pass. **Recommendation: leave excluded** unless your supervisor specifically requires it — engagement (below) already captures much of the same variance in the Saroj et al. (2026) model, and adding both risks construct overlap/multicollinearity. |
| **Learner satisfaction** | Excluded from current draft. | Wang (2003) Learner Satisfaction Questionnaire (LSQ) exists and is validated, but Owston et al.'s synthesis (file 08, row 11) explicitly warns that "what improves satisfaction may not necessarily improve objective training effectiveness" — i.e., satisfaction and effectiveness are empirically dissociable. **Recommendation: leave excluded from the core model**, but this is defensible either way — if your supervisor wants it reinstated, LSQ is the ready validated source. |
| **Digital literacy / technology readiness** | Partially present (HL10-12 only, not as a standalone section). | Ye, Kuang & Liu (2022) treat ICT self-efficacy as an **antecedent**, not the central mediator, of blended-learning outcomes — this justifies folding technology items into the Hybrid Learning section (as you currently do) rather than elevating it to a full separate variable/section. |
| **Trainer competence** | Excluded from current 4-variable draft; present only in the *recommended* Model B. | Saroj, Sahney & Sekar (2026) — the single strongest, most recent, directly-verified workplace paper found — includes instructor competence as a direct predictor of engagement. **This is the strongest case among all seven candidates for reinstating a variable your current draft omits.** If time allows, adding a short trainer-competence section (8-item scale, Faculty online competence study 2021) would materially strengthen alignment with your best available primary source. |
| **Organizational/supervisor support** | Excluded from current 4-variable draft; present only in the *recommended* Model B (as moderator). | Saroj, Sahney & Sekar (2026) tests supervisor support as a moderator; Facteau et al. (1995) and Eisenberger et al. (1986) provide 40 years of supporting theory and validated instruments (SPOS). **Second-strongest case for reinstatement** alongside trainer competence. |
| **Learner engagement** | Excluded as a named section in files 04/05; present only in the recommended Model B/C discussion (file 06/08). | This is the variable the literature most consistently identifies as the central mechanism (Saroj et al. 2026; Ashraf et al. 2021; Yang 2023) — **the single most important open gap between your current questionnaire and your own literature review's conclusion.** Two validated short-form options exist if added: STE-S (5 items, training-session-specific) or UWES-9 (9 items, general work engagement, cross-nationally validated). |

**The core tension to raise explicitly with your supervisor:** your literature review (files 06/08) concludes that *learner engagement* (plus trainer competence and organizational support) is the best-supported explanatory mechanism, but your actual drafted questionnaire (files 04/05) does not measure any of the three. Closing this gap — even with a short 5-8 item addition per variable — is the single change most likely to satisfy the original guidance in file 02 ("identify variables that explain the relationship," not just measure the endpoints).

## 7. Basis for the Recommended Conceptual Model (Model B)

Full reasoning already documented in [08-literature-matrix.md](08-literature-matrix.md), section 4. Summarised here for defense purposes:

- **Why not Model A** (Hybrid Learning → Engagement → Effectiveness alone): under-specified relative to the strongest available source (Saroj, Sahney & Sekar, 2026), which tests trainer competence and supervisor support alongside engagement, not engagement in isolation.
- **Why not Model C** (adding training transfer as a further step): theoretically supported (Baldwin & Ford 1988; Blume et al. 2010; LTSI literature) but no workplace *blended-learning* study located in this search tested the full transfer step alongside engagement in one combined model — building it would mean an untested extension rather than following precedent, higher-risk for a time-bound master's project.
- **Why Model B**: it is the largest model for which a single, directly-analogous, independently-verified (fetched, not just search-snippet), recent (2026) workplace study exists — Saroj, Sahney & Sekar (2026), n=375, published VJIKMS 56(2).

**If a panel member asks "why this paper as your anchor and not one of the other 35":** it is the only paper in the entire matrix that (a) is set in a genuine workplace blended-learning training context (not higher education), (b) uses a formal mediation/moderation SEM design rather than a simple comparison or review, (c) was published recently enough (2026) to reflect current blended-learning practice rather than pre-pandemic assumptions, and (d) was independently verified by direct publisher-page fetch in this research session, not taken on faith from a search snippet.
